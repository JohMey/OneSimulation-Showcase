Exercise sheets

Eight sheets for the lab course. Each has a goal, three tasks of rising difficulty and a clear deliverable. The guide contains the worked starting point for every sheet; the sheets ask for the step beyond it.

The sheets state no numerical answers except where a published result exists. Students are asked to measure and to justify, which is also what makes the sheets reusable from year to year.

1 Cooling by radiation

Goal. Compare a numerical time history with a closed-form solution and quantify the error.

  1. Reproduce the radiative-cooling model of the guide and plot temperature against time together with the analytical solution.
  2. Halve the time step three times. Tabulate the maximum error and determine the observed order of the integrator.
  3. Replace the zero-kelvin enclosure by 293 K. Explain why the closed-form solution no longer applies and propose a check that still does.

Hand in. A notebook with the error table, the observed order and one paragraph on the changed reference.

Worked material in the guide:

  • Radiative cooling of one thermal mass

2 A damped oscillator

Goal. Tell temporal error from modelling error.

  1. Run the damped oscillator and compare displacement and energy with the analytical solution.
  2. Change only the time step, then only the damping. State which change alters the physics and which alters the numerics.
  3. Find the largest step for which the amplitude error after ten periods stays below one percent.

Hand in. Two plots, the step-size table and the largest admissible step with its justification.

Worked material in the guide:

  • Reuse the transient workflow for a damped oscillator
  • Separate temporal error from the physical model

3 Order of a finite-difference stencil

Goal. Measure a convergence order instead of quoting it.

  1. Transport a smooth travelling wave with a second-order and a higher-order stencil.
  2. Refine the grid at least three times and plot the error against the spacing on logarithmic axes.
  3. Repeat with a profile that has a kink. Report the observed order and explain the difference.

Hand in. The convergence plot with fitted slopes for both profiles.

Worked material in the guide:

  • Measure finite-difference order on a traveling wave

4 Limiters in finite volumes

Goal. See what a limiter buys and what it costs.

  1. Transport a step profile once without limiting and once with each slope limiter described in the guide.
  2. Measure overshoot, total variation and the conserved integral after one period.
  3. Choose a limiter for a smooth profile and one for a step, and defend the choice with your numbers.

Hand in. A table of overshoot, total variation and conservation error per limiter.

Worked material in the guide:

  • Conservative reconstruction and slope limiting
  • Compare slope limiter responses

5 One model, five discretizations

Goal. Compare methods on equal terms.

  1. Reproduce the method-swap example on 16 × 16 × 16 cells and confirm the published error of each method to its leading digit.
  2. Repeat on 8 and 32 cells per direction. Determine the observed order of each method and compare it with theory.
  3. Plot error against run time for all runs. Which method would you choose for an error of 10⁻⁵, and why would the answer change on a curved geometry?

Hand in. The error table for three meshes, observed orders and the error–time plot.

Worked material in the guide:

  • Change one numerical choice at a time

6 Linear solvers

Goal. Judge an iterative solver by residual, error and time.

  1. Solve the finite-element Poisson system of the previous sheet with a direct solver, conjugate gradients and GMRES with ILU.
  2. Record iterations, final residual, error against the direct solution and run time.
  3. Tighten the tolerance until the solver error is below the discretization error. Explain why a tighter tolerance is wasted effort.

Hand in. A solver comparison table and the tolerance you would use.

Worked material in the guide:

  • Linear solvers and preconditioners
  • Linear solvers against a common direct reference

7 A cantilever in 3D

Goal. Check a field solution against an engineering formula and know the limits of the formula.

  1. Run the cantilever on two meshes and report tip deflection and maximum stress for both.
  2. Compare the deflection with Euler–Bernoulli beam theory. The worked example reports 0.0948 mm against 0.0952 mm.
  3. Double the length at constant load. Check the cubic scaling and explain where a short, thick beam departs from beam theory.

Hand in. Deflection and stress for both meshes, the comparison with beam theory and the scaling check.

Worked material in the guide:

  • Three-dimensional cantilever

8 Your own equation

Goal. Verify a model that has no textbook solution.

  1. Choose a smooth exact solution of your own and derive the forcing for the reaction–diffusion equation of the guide.
  2. Solve on three meshes and compute the error in a norm of your choice.
  3. Report the observed order. If it differs from the expected one, find out why before you hand in.

Hand in. Your derivation, the refinement table and the observed order.

Worked material in the guide:

  • Derive a verification target and its forcing
  • Choose the discretization, solve and verify
  • Read the measured refinement results
