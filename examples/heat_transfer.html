Heat transfer · 2D heat transport in a thin slab

The same heat sources. A cooler temperature field.

Combine conduction, heat advection and diffusion, convective boundaries, radiation and phase-change models.

Heat conductionAdvection–diffusionConvective heat fluxRadiationPhase changeThermal coupling

How it was computed

  • 576 × 384 × 1 finite-volume cells · 221,184 solved temperatures per case
  • Prescribed circulation lowers the peak temperature by 9.95 K · 120 × 80 × 2 mm slab · cooled walls
Inspect the conductive heat flux

Conductive heat flux q = −k∇T. The flow is prescribed, and no momentum or buoyancy equation is solved. The solution is invariant through the insulated thickness.

Resolution and numerical checks

The 288 × 192 versus 576 × 384 comparison gives a 0.000501 K RMS temperature difference; the relative heat-balance defect in the circulating case is 1.67 × 10−7. Constant coefficients and prescribed flow define this illustrative model.
