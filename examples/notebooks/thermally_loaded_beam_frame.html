Thermally loaded beam with 3D beam elements

A simply supported steel beam bent about both axes by temperature differences across its height and its width.

3D beam elementsPrescribed temperature gradientThermal bendingSupport reactionsClosed-form check
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation models the 3 m beam as a frame of 3D Euler-Bernoulli beam elements with six degrees of freedom per joint and applies the linear temperature over the cross-section as a prescribed thermal load, without any heat-conduction solve. The same beam clamped at both ends gives the restraint forces and moments.

What it checks

The midspan deflection is 15.4688 mm towards each warm side and 21.8761 mm in total, equal to beam theory alpha g L^2/8 on 2, 4 and 12 elements (largest joint deviation 2e-15 m), the axial elongation is 1.65 mm, and the clamped beam carries 184.8 kN and 616.0 N m about both axes as E A alpha dT and E I alpha g predict.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A straight steel beam of length \(L=3\) m with a square \(40\times40\) mm cross-section is heated unevenly. Across its height the bottom face is \(\Delta T_z=50\) K warmer than the top face, across its width the face at \(+y\) is \(\Delta T_y=50\) K warmer than the face at \(-y\), and the mean temperature lies 50 K above the stress-free reference. The temperature therefore varies linearly over the section,

\[T(y,z)-T_\text{ref}=\Delta T_m+g_y\,y+g_z\,z,\qquad g_y=\frac{\Delta T_y}{b}=1250\ \text{K/m},\quad g_z=-\frac{\Delta T_z}{h}=-1250\ \text{K/m}.\]

A linear temperature over the section is exactly what a beam element can carry: the mean value stretches the axis, the two gradients impose the free thermal curvatures \(\kappa_y=\alpha g_y\) and \(\kappa_z=\alpha g_z\). OneSimulation models the beam as a SpatialFrame of 3D Euler-Bernoulli beam elements (six degrees of freedom per joint) and applies the temperature field with ThermalFrameMemberLoads.linear_temperature; no temperature is computed, it is prescribed.

| case | supports | beam theory | |---|---|---| | simply supported | \(x=0\): displacements and twist held; \(x=L\): transverse displacements held | statically determinate: free bending, \(v(x)=\kappa_y\,x(L-x)/2\), \(w(x)=\kappa_z\,x(L-x)/2\), \(u(L)=\alpha\Delta T_mL\), no reactions | | clamped | both ends fully held | no displacement; axial force \(N=-EA\,\alpha\Delta T_m\), end moments \(EI\,\alpha g\) about both axes |

Steel: \(E=210\) GPa, \(G=E/2.6\), \(\alpha=11\times10^{-6}\) 1/K.

Reference (B. A. Boley and J. H. Weiner, Theory of Thermal Stresses, Wiley 1960, §9.2; W. C. Young and R. G. Budynas, Roark's Formulas for Stress and Strain, 7th ed., McGraw-Hill 2002, §8.1, thermal loading of beams). The midspan deflection of the simply supported beam is

\[|v(L/2)|=|w(L/2)|=\frac{\alpha\,g\,L^2}{8}=15.47\ \text{mm},\qquad \sqrt{v^2+w^2}=21.88\ \text{mm}.\]
import numpy as np
from onesim.meshing import BeamSection
from onesim.physics.solid_mechanics import (
    FrameMember,
    SpatialFrame,
    ThermalFrameMemberLoads,
)
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Beam model

The beam is divided into \(n\) equal elements; three subdivisions are compared. The section supplies area and second moments.

length, youngs_modulus, alpha = 3.0, 210e9, 11e-6
section = BeamSection("rectangle", (0.04, 0.04))
inertia_y, inertia_z = section.inertia
beam = lambda n: SpatialFrame(
    joints=np.column_stack(
        (np.linspace(0, length, n + 1), np.zeros(n + 1), np.zeros(n + 1))
    ),
    members=tuple(
        FrameMember(
            name=f"element {i}",
            start=i,
            end=i + 1,
            youngs_modulus=youngs_modulus,
            area=section.area,
            bending_inertia_y=inertia_y,
            bending_inertia_z=inertia_z,
            shear_modulus=youngs_modulus / 2.6,
            torsion_constant=0.1406 * 0.04**4,
        )
        for i in range(n)
    ),
)
temperature = ThermalFrameMemberLoads.linear_temperature(
    temperature=343.15,
    reference_temperature=293.15,
    expansion_coefficient=alpha,
    gradient=(1250.0, -1250.0),
)
simply_supported = lambda n: (
    *((0, dof, 0.0) for dof in ("x", "y", "z", "rx")),
    (n, "y", 0.0),
    (n, "z", 0.0),
)
clamped = lambda n: tuple(
    (joint, dof, 0.0) for joint in (0, n) for dof in ("x", "y", "z", "rx", "ry", "rz")
)
runs = {
    n: beam(n).solve(supports=simply_supported(n), member_loads=temperature)
    for n in (2, 4, 12)
}
held = beam(12).solve(supports=clamped(12), member_loads=temperature)

Comparison with beam theory

camber = lambda x, gradient: alpha * gradient * x * (length - x) / 2
print(
    "elements   v(L/2) [mm]   w(L/2) [mm]   total [mm]   u(L) [mm]   largest deviation from beam theory at the joints"
)
for n, result in runs.items():
    x = np.linspace(0, length, n + 1)
    exact = np.column_stack((alpha * 50.0 * x, camber(x, 1250.0), camber(x, -1250.0)))
    mid = result.displacement[n // 2]
    print(
        f"{n:8d} {1e3 * mid[1]:13.4f} {1e3 * mid[2]:13.4f} {1e3 * np.hypot(mid[1], mid[2]):12.4f} {1e3 * result.displacement[-1, 0]:11.4f}"
        f" {np.abs(result.displacement - exact).max():14.1e} m"
    )
print(
    f"beam theory {1e3 * camber(length / 2, 1250.0):11.4f} {1e3 * camber(length / 2, -1250.0):13.4f} "
    f"{1e3 * np.sqrt(2) * camber(length / 2, 1250.0):12.4f} {1e3 * alpha * 50.0 * length:11.4f}"
)
print(
    f"simply supported: largest reaction force {np.abs(runs[12].reaction_force).max():.1e} N, "
    f"largest reaction moment {np.abs(runs[12].reaction_moment).max():.1e} N m (statically determinate, free bending)"
)

axial = youngs_modulus * section.area * alpha * 50.0
moment = youngs_modulus * inertia_y * alpha * 1250.0
reactions = {
    "axial force [kN]": (abs(held.reaction_force[0, 0]) / 1e3, axial / 1e3),
    "moment about y [N m]": (abs(held.reaction_moment[0, 1]), moment),
    "moment about z [N m]": (abs(held.reaction_moment[0, 2]), moment),
}
print("clamped beam, reactions at x = 0 (magnitudes)   simulation    beam theory")
for name, (value, exact) in reactions.items():
    print(f"{name:44s} {value:12.3f} {exact:14.3f}")
print(f"clamped beam: largest displacement {np.abs(held.displacement).max():.1e} m")

Figure

Left: the deflection of the simply supported beam towards the warm side (\(+y\)) and towards the warm underside (\(-z\)), at the joints of the 12-element model, against the beam-theory parabola. Right: the reactions of the clamped beam that hold back the thermal elongation and curvatures.

fine = np.linspace(0, length, 200)
joints = np.linspace(0, length, 13)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 4.8))
plots.line(
    points={
        "v (towards +y)": (joints, 1e3 * runs[12].displacement[:, 1]),
        "w (along z)": (joints, 1e3 * runs[12].displacement[:, 2]),
    },
    reference={
        "v (towards +y)": (fine, 1e3 * camber(fine, 1250.0)),
        "w (along z)": (fine, 1e3 * camber(fine, -1250.0)),
    },
    xlabel="position along the beam x [m]",
    ylabel="deflection [mm]",
    title="Simply supported beam (markers) and beam theory (dashed)",
    legend="center right",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.bars(
    [
        "axial force / 10 [kN]",
        "moment about y [N m] / 100",
        "moment about z [N m] / 100",
    ],
    {
        "simulation": [
            abs(held.reaction_force[0, 0]) / 1e4,
            abs(held.reaction_moment[0, 1]) / 100,
            abs(held.reaction_moment[0, 2]) / 100,
        ]
    },
    reference={"beam theory": [axial / 1e4, moment / 100, moment / 100]},
    ylabel="magnitude (scaled)",
    title="Clamped beam: reactions at x = 0",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Going further

Give the gradients different values, or let the temperature vary in time with the time argument of linear_temperature: the beam follows quasi-statically. Holding only one end against rotation turns the free curvature into a cantilever deflection \(\kappa L^2/2\) at the tip.
