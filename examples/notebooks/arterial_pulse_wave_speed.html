Arterial pulse wave speed

A small pressure pulse travelling along an elastic artery, graded against the Moens-Korteweg wave speed.

1D vesselMoens-KortewegClosed-form check
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation evolves a one-dimensional elastic vessel with a tube wall law and a Windkessel termination and tracks the propagating pulse.

What it checks

The pulse speed matches c0 = sqrt(beta/(2 rho)) (Moens-Korteweg) within about 0.03 %, and the characteristic impedance rho c0/A0 within about 0.04 %.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Blood vessels are compliant tubes: a local rise in pressure widens the vessel, and the interplay between the inertia of the blood and the elasticity of the wall lets a pressure pulse run along the artery much faster than the blood itself moves. For a thin-walled elastic tube of resting radius \(r_0\), wall thickness \(h\), Young's modulus \(E\) and Poisson ratio \(\nu\) the small-amplitude pulse-wave speed is the Moens-Korteweg speed

\[c_0=\sqrt{\frac{E\,h}{2\,\rho\,(1-\nu^2)\,r_0}} .\]

We launch a small, smooth area pulse in a straight vessel, let it split into a forward- and a backward-running pulse, and measure how fast they travel.

OneSimulation integrates the one-dimensional cross-section-averaged blood-flow equations for the lumen area \(A(x,t)\) and the volume flow \(Q(x,t)\),

\[\partial_t A + \partial_x Q = 0,\qquad \partial_t Q + \partial_x\!\Big(\frac{Q^2}{A}\Big) + \frac{A}{\rho}\,\partial_x p = 0,\]

closed by the elastic tube law of ArterialSegment,

\[p(A) = p_\mathrm{ext} + \beta\Big(\sqrt{A/A_0}-1\Big),\]

with a second-order finite-volume scheme (minmod reconstruction, Rusanov flux) and characteristic-based junction and terminal conditions.

Reference. Linearising the system about the resting area \(A_0\) gives the wave speed \(c^2 = (A/\rho)\,\mathrm{d}p/\mathrm{d}A\). For the tube law above \(\mathrm{d}p/\mathrm{d}A = \beta/(2\sqrt{A A_0})\), so at \(A=A_0\)

\[c_0=\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{2\rho}} .\]

Matching the tube law to a thin elastic wall gives \(\beta=\sqrt{\pi}\,E h/\big((1-\nu^2)\sqrt{A_0}\big)\), and with \(A_0=\pi r_0^2\) this \(c_0\) is exactly the Moens-Korteweg speed. A linear forward-running pulse also fixes the ratio of its pressure and flow perturbations, the characteristic impedance \(Z_0=\rho c_0/A_0\); we check both.

from importlib.metadata import version
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

from onesim.physics.biophysics import (
    ArterialNetwork,
    ArterialPressure,
    ArterialSegment,
    WindkesselTerminal,
)

print("OneSimulation:", version("onesimulation"))

Parameters

The vessel is of aortic size with a wall of moderate stiffness, filled with blood. The initial disturbance is a narrow Gaussian area bump of one part in a thousand of the resting area, so the wave stays linear. The run ends before the two pulses reach the ends of the vessel, so the terminals do not influence the speed measurement.

rho = 1060.0          # kg/m^3, blood density
r0 = 0.010            # m, resting lumen radius
h = 1.5e-3            # m, wall thickness
E = 4.0e5             # Pa, Young's modulus of the wall
nu = 0.5              # Poisson ratio of the (incompressible) wall
L = 1.0               # m, vessel length
x0 = 0.5 * L          # m, centre of the initial pulse
sigma = 0.03          # m, width of the Gaussian pulse
amplitude = 1.0e-3    # relative area perturbation (small, so the wave stays linear)
cells = 400           # finite-volume cells along the vessel
final_time = 0.05     # s

A0 = np.pi * r0 ** 2                                   # m^2, resting area
beta = np.sqrt(np.pi) * E * h / ((1.0 - nu ** 2) * np.sqrt(A0))   # Pa, tube-law stiffness
c_formula = np.sqrt(E * h / (2.0 * rho * (1.0 - nu ** 2) * r0))  # m/s, Moens-Korteweg speed
c_tube_law = np.sqrt(beta / (2.0 * rho))                        # m/s, from the model's wall law
Z0 = rho * c_formula / A0                                        # Pa·s/m^3, characteristic impedance
print(f"A0 = {A0*1e4:.3f} cm^2,  beta = {beta:.0f} Pa")
print(f"Moens-Korteweg speed c0 = {c_formula:.4f} m/s,  tube-law speed sqrt(beta/2rho) = {c_tube_law:.4f} m/s,"
      f"  c0*t = {c_formula*final_time:.3f} m")
assert abs(c_tube_law - c_formula) < 1e-12 * c_formula, "The tube-law stiffness must reproduce Moens-Korteweg."


def initial_area(x):
    return A0 * (1.0 + amplitude * np.exp(-((x - x0) ** 2) / (2.0 * sigma ** 2)))


vessel = ArterialSegment(
    name="aorta", start="inlet", end="outlet",
    length=L, cells=cells, reference_area=A0, wall_stiffness=beta,
)
network = ArterialNetwork(
    segments=(vessel,),
    density=rho,
    terminals=(
        # Inlet: held at the resting transmural pressure (a large upstream reservoir).
        ArterialPressure(node="inlet", pressure=0.0),
        # Outlet: three-element windkessel whose proximal resistance equals the
        # characteristic impedance, so an arriving forward pulse is absorbed.
        WindkesselTerminal(
            node="outlet", proximal_resistance=Z0, distal_resistance=5.0 * Z0,
            compliance=1.0e-9, reference_pressure=0.0, initial_pressure=0.0,
        ),
    ),
)
result = network.solve(
    times=np.linspace(0.0, final_time, 6),
    max_step=1.0e-3,
    initial_area={"aorta": initial_area},
    initial_flow={"aorta": lambda x: np.zeros_like(x)},
)
print(f"Integrated to t = {result.times[-1]:.4f} s with {len(result.stage_times)} explicit stages")

Measure the pulse speed and the characteristic impedance

The relative area perturbation \(\delta A/A_0\) at the final time consists of two pulses that keep their shape. The centroid of \(|\delta A|\) on each half of the vessel marks the pulse position; the travelled distance divided by the elapsed time is the measured speed. On the forward pulse the flow and pressure perturbations are tied by \(\delta p = Z_0\,Q\) (and \(\delta p=-Z_0 Q\) on the backward pulse), which gives an independent check of the wall law.

x = result.coordinates["aorta"]
area_final = result.area["aorta"][-1]
flow_final = result.flow["aorta"][-1]
pressure_final = result.pressure["aorta"][-1]
t_end = float(result.times[-1])

delta_a = area_final / A0 - 1.0
weight = np.abs(delta_a)
right = x > x0
left = x < x0
centroid_right = np.sum(x[right] * weight[right]) / np.sum(weight[right])
centroid_left = np.sum(x[left] * weight[left]) / np.sum(weight[left])
speed_right = (centroid_right - x0) / t_end
speed_left = (x0 - centroid_left) / t_end
speed_measured = 0.5 * (speed_right + speed_left)
rel_error = abs(speed_measured - c_formula) / c_formula

print(f"forward  pulse speed = {speed_right:.4f} m/s")
print(f"backward pulse speed = {speed_left:.4f} m/s")
print(f"measured pulse speed = {speed_measured:.4f} m/s")
print(f"relative error vs Moens-Korteweg = {100*rel_error:.2f} %")

# Characteristic impedance from the peak of each pulse.
peak_right = np.argmax(np.where(right, delta_a, -np.inf))
peak_left = np.argmax(np.where(left, delta_a, -np.inf))
Z_right = pressure_final[peak_right] / flow_final[peak_right]
Z_left = -pressure_final[peak_left] / flow_final[peak_left]
Z_measured = 0.5 * (Z_right + Z_left)
Z_error = abs(Z_measured - Z0) / Z0
print(f"peak pressure of the forward pulse = {pressure_final[peak_right]:.2f} Pa,"
      f"  peak flow = {flow_final[peak_right]*1e6:.3f} ml/s")
print(f"characteristic impedance: measured {Z_measured:.4e}, rho*c0/A0 = {Z0:.4e} Pa·s/m^3"
      f"  (relative error {100*Z_error:.2f} %)")

# The speed must match Moens-Korteweg to within a couple of percent, the two
# pulses must be mirror images, and the pressure/flow ratio must match the
# characteristic impedance. The disturbance must stay small and the pulses must
# still be inside the vessel.
assert rel_error < 0.02, "Measured pulse speed deviates more than 2 % from Moens-Korteweg."
assert abs(speed_right - speed_left) / c_formula < 0.02, "The split is not symmetric."
assert Z_error < 0.03, "Pressure/flow ratio deviates more than 3 % from rho*c0/A0."
assert np.max(weight) < 5.0 * amplitude, "The disturbance left the linear regime."
assert np.max(weight) > 0.25 * amplitude, "The pulses have been smeared out too much."
assert centroid_right < 0.9 * L and centroid_left > 0.1 * L, "A pulse reached the end of the vessel."
assert np.all(area_final > 0.0), "The vessel must stay open."
# The forward pulse carries forward flow and the backward pulse backward flow.
assert flow_final[peak_right] > 0.0 and flow_final[peak_left] < 0.0
# Volume is conserved by the network solve.
assert np.max(np.abs(result.volume_balance_residual)) < 1e-15

ax.plot(x, delta_a * 1e3, color="#1f5c73", lw=1.8, label=f"computed, t = {t_end*1e3:.0f} ms")
for centroid in (centroid_left, centroid_right):
    ax.axvline(centroid, color="#b4642e", ls="--", lw=1.2)
for predicted in (x0 - c_formula * t_end, x0 + c_formula * t_end):
    ax.axvline(predicted, color="#4a7a4a", ls=":", lw=1.4)
ax.plot([], [], color="#b4642e", ls="--", lw=1.2, label="measured pulse centroid")

ax.set(xlabel="Position along the vessel x / m", ylabel=r"Area perturbation $\delta A/A_0$ / ‰",

ax.legend(loc="upper right", fontsize=8)
ax.grid(alpha=0.25)

ax = axes[1]

ax.set(xlabel="Position along the vessel x / m", ylabel="Pressure / Pa",
       title="Forward pulse: p = +Z0 Q,  backward pulse: p = -Z0 Q")
ax.legend(loc="upper right", fontsize=8)
ax.grid(alpha=0.25)
plt.show()

Going further

Stiffen the wall (E) or thin the lumen (r0) and watch the pulse run faster as \(\sqrt{E h/r_0}\): arterial stiffening with age shows up directly as a higher pulse-wave velocity. Lengthen final_time so the forward pulse reaches the outlet: with the proximal resistance matched to \(Z_0\) it is absorbed almost without reflection, while a sealed outlet (ArterialInflow(flow=0)) sends it back doubled in pressure. Raise amplitude towards unity and the crest, which travels faster than the trough, steepens into a shock-like front.
