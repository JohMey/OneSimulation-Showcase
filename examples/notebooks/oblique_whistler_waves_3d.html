Oblique whistler and ion-cyclotron waves in Hall MHD

Fourteen small-amplitude waves travelling at different angles to the magnetic field in a 3D periodic plasma: whistler and ion-cyclotron branches with their polarisation.

Pseudo-spectral vs central differencesClassical RK4Exact oblique dispersion
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation advances the compressible Hall-MHD equations in a triply periodic box with Fourier pseudo-spectral derivatives and classical RK4, and repeats the run with second-order central differences, measuring each mode's frequency from its time series.

What it checks

All 14 frequencies match the exact oblique dispersion relation to 1.5e-7 with the spectral scheme, central differences converge at second order onto their predicted discrete frequencies, and energy is conserved to 1e-16.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A uniform plasma (density \(\rho_0\), pressure \(p_0\), adiabatic exponent \(\gamma\)) is threaded by the uniform field \(\mathbf B_0=B_0\hat{\mathbf z}\). In Hall magnetohydrodynamics the electric field carries, besides the ideal part \(-\mathbf v\times\mathbf B\), the Hall term

\[\mathbf E_{\rm Hall}=\eta_H\,\frac{\mathbf J\times\mathbf B}{\rho},\qquad \mathbf J=\nabla\times\mathbf B\qquad(\mu_0=1),\]

which decouples the ions from the field on scales below the ion inertial length \(d_i=\eta_H/\sqrt{\rho_0}\). The shear-Alfvén wave then splits into a right-hand whistler branch whose frequency grows like \(k^2\), and a left-hand ion-cyclotron / kinetic-Alfvén-type branch. Both depend on the angle \(\theta\) between the wave vector \(\mathbf k\) and \(\mathbf B_0\).

Exact reference. Linearising the compressible, adiabatic Hall-MHD equations about the uniform state gives the dispersion relation for oblique propagation (E. Hameiri, A. Ishizawa and A. Ishida, Waves in the Hall-magnetohydrodynamics model, Phys. Plasmas 12, 072109, 2005)

\[\left(\omega^2-k_\parallel^2v_A^2\right)\left(\omega^4-k^2(v_A^2+c_s^2)\,\omega^2+k^2k_\parallel^2v_A^2c_s^2\right) =\omega^2\,k_\parallel^2k^2\,v_A^2\,d_i^2\left(\omega^2-k^2c_s^2\right),\]

with \(k_\parallel=k\cos\theta\), the Alfvén speed \(v_A=B_0/\sqrt{\rho_0}\) and the sound speed \(c_s=\sqrt{\gamma p_0/\rho_0}\). Its three positive roots are the slow, the ion-cyclotron / kinetic-Alfvén-type and the fast / whistler branch. For \(d_i=0\) it factorises into the ideal Alfvén and magnetosonic waves; for \(\theta=0\) it reduces to the parallel whistler \(\omega=kv_A[\sqrt{1+(kd_i/2)^2}+kd_i/2]\) and the ion-cyclotron wave with the minus sign; for large \(k\) the whistler approaches \(\omega\simeq k\,k_\parallel d_i v_A\). The eigenvectors of the same linear system give the polarisation of each branch.

Numerical method. OneSimulation's three-dimensional resistive/Hall-MHD solver integrates the eight conservative fields (density, three momenta, total energy, three magnetic components) in conservative form. Two spatial discretisations of the same equations are compared on a periodic collocation grid:

  • Fourier pseudo-spectral derivatives (exact for every resolved Fourier mode),
  • second-order central finite differences, whose derivative of \(e^{ikx}\) is \(i\tilde k\,e^{ikx}\) with the modified wavenumber \(\tilde k=\sin(kh)/h\).

Time integration is the explicit classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method (RK4).

What is checked. Frequencies of all launched modes against the exact dispersion relation; polarisation; total-energy conservation and amplitude preservation of every mode; a resolution study for both spatial schemes (with the modified-wavenumber prediction for the difference scheme); and a time-step study with the observed order of RK4.

Parameters

Dimensionless units with \(\mu_0=1\): \(\rho_0=1\), \(B_0=1\) (so \(v_A=1\)), \(\eta_H=0.5\) (so \(d_i=0.5\)), \(\gamma=5/3\) and \(c_s=0.5\) (plasma beta \(2p_0/B_0^2=0.3\)). The periodic box has the side \(2\pi\), so the admissible wave vectors are integer triples \((k_x,k_y,k_z)\).

Eight modes are launched on the whistler branch and six on the ion-cyclotron / kinetic-Alfvén branch, each with a magnetic amplitude of \(10^{-7}B_0\) (small enough that the quadratic coupling between the harmonics \(\mathbf k\), \(2\mathbf k\), \(3\mathbf k\) stays below the time-integration error): a family with \(|\mathbf k|=3\) at \(\theta=0^\circ\), \(48.2^\circ\), \(70.5^\circ\) and \(90^\circ\), and families along \(\theta=0^\circ\) and \(\theta=45^\circ\) with \(|\mathbf k|\) between 1 and 4.24 (\(kd_i\) up to 2.1).

Geometry and mesh

The domain is the cube \([0,2\pi)^3\), periodic in \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\) on all six faces (the solver's only boundary condition). The grids have \(N^3\) points with \(N=8\), 16 and 32 for the spectral scheme and \(N=16\), 32 and 64 for the difference scheme; the main run uses the spectral scheme on \(32^3\) points. All fourteen modes are superposed, so the field is a genuinely three-dimensional interference pattern.

Model

Linear theory. For a plane wave \(\propto e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}\) the linearised equations for \(X=(\rho',\mathbf v,p',\mathbf b)\) read \(\dot X=L(\mathbf k)X\) with

\[\dot\rho'=-\rho_0\,i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v,\quad \rho_0\dot{\mathbf v}=-i\mathbf k\,p'-i\mathbf k(\mathbf B_0\cdot\mathbf b)+i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf B_0)\mathbf b,\quad \dot p'=-\gamma p_0\,i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf v,\]
\[\dot{\mathbf b}=i\mathbf k\times(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B_0)-\frac{\eta_H}{\rho_0}\,i\mathbf k\times\big[(i\mathbf k\times\mathbf b)\times\mathbf B_0\big].\]

The eigenvalues of \(L\) are \(-i\omega\); the positive frequencies agree with the three roots of the closed-form relation above (both are printed below), and the eigenvectors supply the initial state of each launched mode. For the difference scheme every derivative of a Fourier mode is multiplied by \(i\tilde{\mathbf k}\) instead of \(i\mathbf k\), so its exact discrete frequency is \(\omega(\tilde{\mathbf k})\) with \(\tilde k_j=\sin(k_jh)/h\); the difference runs are launched with the eigenvectors of \(L(\tilde{\mathbf k})\) so that each excites a single discrete branch.

def skew(a):
    return np.array([[0, -a[2], a[1]], [a[2], 0, -a[0]], [-a[1], a[0], 0]], dtype=complex)


def linear_operator(k):
    ik = 1j * np.asarray(k, float)
    K, C = skew(ik), skew(B0_vec)
    L = np.zeros((8, 8), complex)
    L[0, 1:4] = -rho_0 * ik
    L[1:4, 4] = -ik / rho_0
    L[1:4, 5:8] = (-np.outer(ik, B0_vec) + (ik @ B0_vec) * np.eye(3)) / rho_0
    L[4, 1:4] = -gamma * p_0 * ik
    L[5:8, 1:4] = -K @ C                                   # curl(v x B0)
    L[5:8, 5:8] = (eta_H / rho_0) * K @ C @ K              # -(eta_H/rho0) curl((curl b) x B0)
    return L


def eigenmodes(k):
    """Positive frequencies (ascending) and eigenvectors of the linearised Hall-MHD system."""
    lam, vectors = np.linalg.eig(linear_operator(k))
    omega = (1j * lam).real
    keep = [i for i in np.argsort(omega) if omega[i] > 1e-9]
    return omega[keep], vectors[:, keep]


def dispersion_roots(k_abs, theta):
    """Positive roots of the closed-form oblique Hall-MHD dispersion relation (Hameiri et al. 2005)."""
    k2, kp2 = k_abs**2, (k_abs * np.cos(theta)) ** 2
    lhs = np.polymul([1.0, -kp2 * v_A**2], [1.0, -k2 * (v_A**2 + c_s**2), k2 * kp2 * v_A**2 * c_s**2])
    rhs = kp2 * k2 * v_A**2 * d_i**2 * np.array([1.0, -k2 * c_s**2, 0.0])
    w2 = np.roots(np.polysub(lhs, rhs))
    w2 = w2.real[(np.abs(w2.imag) < 1e-9 * np.abs(w2).max()) & (w2.real > 1e-14)]
    return np.sort(np.sqrt(w2))


def frame(k):
    """Unit vectors e1 (perpendicular to k and B0) and e2 (perpendicular to k, in the k-B0 plane)."""
    khat = np.asarray(k, float) / np.linalg.norm(k)
    e1 = np.cross(khat, B0_vec)
    e1 = np.array([1.0, 0.0, 0.0]) if np.linalg.norm(e1) < 1e-12 else e1 / np.linalg.norm(e1)
    return e1, np.cross(khat, e1)

          f"  {w_eig:12.7f}        {w_closed:12.7f}")


# **Runs.** Each run starts from the uniform state plus the real parts of the fourteen eigenvectors
# (normalised to $|\hat{\mathbf b}|=10^{-7}$). The conservative state is advanced in short
# segments of length `sample_interval`; after each segment the complex amplitude of every launched
# mode is extracted by projecting $\mathbf B-\mathbf B_0$ onto $e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}$ and
# onto the launched polarisation vector. The frequency is the slope of the unwrapped phase of that
# amplitude over the whole run (least squares).

def run(N, scheme, time_step):
    solver = ResistiveMHD3DSolver(mesh_size=N, gamma=gamma, resistivity=0.0, hall_coefficient=eta_H,
                                  final_time=sample_interval, time_step=time_step, time_method="classical_rk4")
    solver.operators = dataclasses.replace(solver.operators, derivative_scheme=scheme)
    h = 2 * np.pi / N
    X = np.meshgrid(*solver.axes, indexing="ij")
    perturbation = np.zeros((8,) + X[0].shape)
    launched = []
    for k, branch in modes:
        kv = np.array(k, float)
        k_scheme = np.sin(kv * h) / h if scheme == "centered_fdm" else kv
        omega, vectors = eigenmodes(k_scheme)
        vector = vectors[:, branch] / np.linalg.norm(vectors[5:8, branch])
        wave = np.exp(1j * (kv[0] * X[0] + kv[1] * X[1] + kv[2] * X[2]))
        perturbation += amplitude * (vector[:, None, None, None] * wave).real
        launched.append(dict(k=kv, branch=branch, polarisation=vector[5:8], omega_discrete=omega[branch],
                             conj_wave=np.conj(wave)))
    density = rho_0 + perturbation[0]
    velocity = perturbation[1:4]
    magnetic = tuple(B0_vec[i] + perturbation[5 + i] for i in range(3))
    momentum = tuple(density * velocity[i] for i in range(3))
    energy = (p_0 + perturbation[4]) / (gamma - 1) + 0.5 * density * np.sum(velocity**2, axis=0) \
        + 0.5 * sum(b * b for b in magnetic)
    state = (density, momentum, energy, magnetic)

    def amplitudes(magnetic):
        b = [magnetic[i] - B0_vec[i] for i in range(3)]
        out = []
        for mode in launched:
            coefficient = np.array([np.mean(b[i] * mode["conj_wave"]) for i in range(3)])
            out.append(coefficient)
        return np.array(out)

    times, series = [0.0], [amplitudes(magnetic)]
    energy_total = [energy.sum()]
    div_b = 0.0
    start = time.perf_counter()
    t = 0.0
    for _ in range(int(round(final_time / sample_interval))):
        result = solver.run(state)
        state = (result.density, result.momentum, result.total_energy, result.magnetic_field)
        t += result.time
        times.append(t)
        series.append(amplitudes(result.magnetic_field))
        energy_total.append(result.total_energy.sum())
        div_b = max(div_b, float(np.max(np.abs(result.divergence))))
    elapsed = time.perf_counter() - start
    times, series = np.array(times), np.array(series)
    for m, mode in enumerate(launched):
        pol = mode["polarisation"]
        a = series[:, m, :] @ np.conj(pol) / (pol @ np.conj(pol))
        mode["omega"] = -np.polyfit(times, np.unwrap(np.angle(a)), 1)[0]
        mode["amplitude_ratio"] = abs(a[-1]) / abs(a[0])
        mode["signal"] = a
        e1, e2 = frame(mode["k"])
        ratio = (series[:, m, :] @ e1) / (series[:, m, :] @ e2)
        mode["polarisation_measured"] = ratio
        del mode["conj_wave"]
    steps = int(round(final_time / time_step))
    print(f"{'spectral' if scheme == 'spectral' else 'central differences':19s} N = {N:2d}, dt = {time_step:.3f}:"
          f" {steps:5d} RK4 steps in {elapsed:6.1f} s")
    final = dict(magnetic=[state[3][i] - B0_vec[i] for i in range(3)], axes=solver.axes)
    return dict(N=N, scheme=scheme, dt=time_step, h=h, times=times, modes=launched,
                energy_drift=np.max(np.abs(np.array(energy_total) / energy_total[0] - 1)), div_b=div_b,
                final=final)

# **Resolution study.** The spectral derivative is exact for every resolved Fourier mode, so the
# spectral frequencies should not change with $N$ (all three spectral runs use $\Delta t=0.008$;
# what remains is the RK4 time error). The central-difference frequencies converge to
# the exact value with second order and must equal the modified-wavenumber prediction
# $\omega(\tilde{\mathbf k})$ on each grid. From the three grids $N=16,32,64$ the observed order is
# $p=\log_2[(\omega_{16}-\omega_{32})/(\omega_{32}-\omega_{64})]$ and the Richardson estimate
# $\omega_{64}+(\omega_{64}-\omega_{32})/(2^p-1)$, which uses no knowledge of the exact value.
# The difference runs use $\Delta t=0.02$, so their agreement with $\omega(\tilde{\mathbf k})$ is
# limited by the RK4 time error of a few $10^{-6}$ (see the time-step study).

print("\nlargest relative deviation from the exact frequency over all 14 modes")
for run_ in spectral_study:
    dev = max(abs(m["omega"] / exact_omega(m) - 1) for m in run_["modes"])
    print(f"  spectral            N = {run_['N']:2d}: {dev:.2e}")
for run_ in fd_study:
    dev = max(abs(m["omega"] / exact_omega(m) - 1) for m in run_["modes"])

    print(f"  central differences N = {run_['N']:2d}: {dev:.2e}   (vs modified-wavenumber prediction: {pred:.1e})")

print("\ncentral differences, per mode: omega on N = 16, 32, 64, observed order, Richardson estimate vs exact")
for m in range(len(modes)):

    w_ref = exact_omega(fd_study[0]["modes"][m])
    p = np.log2((w[0] - w[1]) / (w[1] - w[2]))
    rich = w[2] + (w[2] - w[1]) / (2**p - 1)
    print(f"  {str(modes[m][0]):11s} {branch_name[modes[m][1]]:18s} " + "  ".join(f"{v:.6f}" for v in w)
          + f"   p = {p:.2f}   Richardson {rich:.6f} ({rich/w_ref-1:+.1e})   exact {w_ref:.6f}")

def upsample(field2d, n):
    """Trigonometric interpolation of a periodic 2D slice onto n x n points."""
    N = field2d.shape[0]
    spectrum = np.fft.fftshift(np.fft.fft2(field2d))
    padded = np.zeros((n, n), complex)
    lo = n // 2 - N // 2
    padded[lo:lo + N, lo:lo + N] = spectrum
    return np.fft.ifft2(np.fft.ifftshift(padded)).real * (n / N) ** 2

Going further

Raise the amplitude to \(10^{-2}B_0\) to see the nonlinear frequency shift and the transfer of energy between modes that share wave-vector triads, or set eta_H = 0 to recover the ideal Alfvén and magnetosonic waves (the whistler and ion-cyclotron branches then merge into the shear-Alfvén wave \(\omega=k_\parallel v_A\)). A finite resistivity adds the damping rate \(\eta k^2/2\) to every mode, which the same phase-and-amplitude fit measures directly.
