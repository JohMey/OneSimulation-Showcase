Sound scattered by a rigid ellipsoid

An oblique plane wave meets a rigid ellipsoid with three different semi-axes; nothing in the problem is symmetric.

STEP importScattered-field formulationQuadratic tetrahedraLow-frequency scatteringAdded-mass tensor

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. OneSimulation 1.0 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)Download the geometry (.step)Download the geometry script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation reads the air around the ellipsoid from one STEP file per frequency, with the outer sphere eight wavenumbers out, meshes it with tetrahedra raised to curved quadratic cells and solves the scattered-field Helmholtz problem with a spherical-wave admittance outside and the incident wave as a load on the body. The result is compared with Rayleigh's low-frequency law built from the volume and Lamb's added-mass tensor of the ellipsoid. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

On 400 receivers around the body the scattered pressure approaches the low-frequency law as the frequency falls: largest deviation 2.5 % of its maximum at ka = 0.25, 7.6 % at ka = 0.5 and 23 % at ka = 1, an observed order of 1.6 in ka (about 2 expected, with a floor from the radiation condition); Lamb's coefficients sum to 2.0000 as they must.

Geometry

The notebook starts at the import of the CAD files ellipsoid_scattering.step, ellipsoid_scattering_ka025.step, ellipsoid_scattering_ka050.step: STEP (AP242), readable by any CAD program, with the names of the faces that the boundary conditions use. Put them next to the notebook.

The geometry script

The script ellipsoid_scattering_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Sound scattered by a rigid ellipsoid".

Builds, with the CAD objects of OneSimulation, the air around a rigid triaxial ellipsoid with
semi-axes 100 mm, 60 mm and 40 mm (a sphere scaled along the axes) out to an outer sphere. The outer
radius is eight wavenumbers at each of three frequencies (ka = 0.25, 0.5 and 1): 3.2 m, 1.6 m and
0.8 m, written to ellipsoid_scattering_ka025.step, ellipsoid_scattering_ka050.step and
ellipsoid_scattering.step. The ellipsoid is the part body, the outer sphere the part air.
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import Sphere

body = Sphere(radius=0.1).scaled((1.0, 0.6, 0.4)).named("body")
for name, outer in (
    ("ellipsoid_scattering_ka025", 3.2),
    ("ellipsoid_scattering_ka050", 1.6),
    ("ellipsoid_scattering", 0.8),
):
    air = Sphere(radius=outer).named("air") - body
    air.save(f"{name}.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A plane wave \(p_i=p_0e^{-ik\,\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\mathbf x}\) with direction \(\hat{\mathbf d}=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3\) falls on a rigid ellipsoid with semi-axes \(a_1=100\) mm, \(a_2=60\) mm and \(a_3=40\) mm. The scattered wave \(p_s\) solves the Helmholtz equation outside the body, with the condition that the total normal velocity on the body vanishes. It is computed out to an outer sphere of radius \(R\) with \(kR=8\) for each frequency.

boundarycondition
ellipsoidrigid: \(\partial_np_s=-\partial_np_i\)
outer sphere \(r=R\)outgoing spherical wave, \(v_n=p_s\,(1+1/(ikR))/(\rho_0c)\)

The outer condition is exact for the monopole part of the scattered wave and reflects the dipole part by about \(1/(kR)^2\approx1.5\) %; a frequency-domain perfectly matched layer would remove that but is not used here.

Reference. For a body small compared with the wavelength the scattered wave is a monopole, because the body displaces fluid, plus a dipole, because the fluid has to flow around it (Lord Rayleigh, Phil. Mag. 44, 28, 1897; G. Dassios and R. Kleinman, Low Frequency Scattering, Oxford 2000, ch. 3):

\[p_s(\mathbf x)\simeq p_0k^2\,\frac{e^{-ikr}}{4\pi r}\Big[-V+\Big(1-\frac{i}{kr}\Big)\, \hat{\mathbf r}\cdot(V\mathbf I+\mathbf A)\cdot\hat{\mathbf d}\Big],\]

with the volume \(V\) and the added-mass tensor \(\mathbf A\) of the body (per unit fluid density). For an ellipsoid \(\mathbf A\) is diagonal in the axes with \(A_j=V\alpha_j/(2-\alpha_j)\) and \(\alpha_j=a_1a_2a_3\int_0^\infty\mathrm d\lambda/\big((a_j^2+\lambda)\sqrt{(a_1^2+\lambda)(a_2^2+\lambda)(a_3^2+\lambda)}\big)\) (H. Lamb, Hydrodynamics, 6th ed., §114). The law is the first term of an expansion in \(ka\); the simulation must approach it as the frequency falls, with a deviation that shrinks like \((ka)^2\).

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.meshing import LocalRefinement, TetrahedralMesher, with_order
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange
from onesim.physics.acoustics import (
    AcousticHarmonicPressure,
    ComplexPressurePencil,
    ConstantPressureForms,
    PressureAdmittanceBoundary,
    PressureTraceSamples,
    SampledPressureAdmittanceForms,
    SampledPressureVolumeForms,
    pressure_space_volume_samples,
)
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme
from scipy.integrate import quad
from scipy.sparse.linalg import spsolve

theme.set()

c0, rho0, amplitude = 343.0, 1.204, 1.0
impedance = rho0 * c0
axes = np.array([0.10, 0.06, 0.04])
direction = np.ones(3) / np.sqrt(3)
cases = {
    0.25: "ellipsoid_scattering_ka025.step",
    0.5: "ellipsoid_scattering_ka050.step",
    1.0: "ellipsoid_scattering.step",
}

Geometry and meshes

Each STEP file holds the air between the ellipsoid and an outer sphere of radius \(8/k\). The tetrahedra are a sixth of the wavelength far away and 12 mm on the ellipsoid; with_order lifts them to quadratic cells whose edge nodes lie on the curved faces.

parts = {ka: load(name) for ka, name in cases.items()}
meshes = {
    ka: with_order(
        TetrahedralMesher(
            target_size=2 * np.pi * axes[0] / ka / 6,
            refinements=(
                LocalRefinement(region=part.boundaries.body, target_size=0.012),
            ),
        ).generate(part),
        2,
    )
    for ka, part in parts.items()
}

part = parts[1.0]
view = plots.Camera(elevation=25, azimuth=-50)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(15, 7))
plots.geometry(
    part,
    selection={"ellipsoid": part.boundaries.body, "outer sphere": part.boundaries.air},
    labels=False,
    camera=view,
    title="Air around the ellipsoid, ka = 1 (STEP)",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes[1.0],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    camera=view,
    title=f"ka = 1: {meshes[1.0].cell_count} curved quadratic tetrahedra",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

Quadratic Lagrange elements; SampledPressureVolumeForms integrates the volume forms and the traces of ellipsoid and outer sphere come from the space's trace_data. The outer sphere carries the admittance, the ellipsoid the load \(-\int\rho_0^{-1}\partial_np_i\,\phi_i\,\mathrm dS\) with the normal of the ellipsoid. AcousticHarmonicPressure solves each frequency on its own mesh.

incident = lambda k, points: amplitude * np.exp(-1j * k * points @ direction)
body_normal = (
    lambda points: points / axes**2 / np.linalg.norm(points / axes**2, axis=1)[:, None]
)
incident_flux = (
    lambda k, points: (body_normal(points) @ direction) * 1j * k * incident(k, points)
)


def solve_ellipsoid(ka):
    """Scattered pressure at one frequency on its mesh."""
    mesh, faces, k = meshes[ka], parts[ka].boundaries, ka / axes[0]
    space = FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2)).bind_space(mesh)
    volume = ConstantPressureForms(
        *SampledPressureVolumeForms(
            size=space.dof_count,
            samples=lambda: pressure_space_volume_samples(space),
            inverse_density=lambda omega, sample: 1 / rho0,
            compressibility=lambda omega, sample: 1 / impedance / c0,
        )(0.0)
    )
    traces = {
        face: SampledPressureAdmittanceForms(
            size=space.dof_count,
            admittance=lambda omega, sample: 1.0,
            samples=lambda face=face: iter(
                [
                    PressureTraceSamples(space.cell_dofs[owner], points, weights, basis)
                    for owner, points, weights, _, basis, _ in map(
                        space.trace_data, mesh.facets(getattr(faces, face))
                    )
                ]
            ),
        )(0.0)
        for face in ("body", "air")
    }
    outer = parts[ka].size[0] / 2
    pressure = (
        AcousticHarmonicPressure(
            operator=ComplexPressurePencil(
                volume,
                boundary=PressureAdmittanceBoundary(
                    lambda omega: (1 + 1 / (1j * k * outer)) / impedance * traces["air"]
                ),
            ),
            angular_frequencies=[c0 * k],
            load=lambda omega: -traces["body"]
            @ incident_flux(k, space.dof_coordinates)
            / rho0,
            linear_solver=spsolve,
        )
        .run()
        .pressure[0]
    )
    return dict(
        space=space, mesh=mesh, pressure=pressure, dofs=space.dof_count, outer=outer
    )


runs = {ka: solve_ellipsoid(ka) for ka in cases}

Reference

Volume, Lamb's coefficients and the low-frequency law.

volume = 4 * np.pi / 3 * np.prod(axes)
alpha = np.array(
    [
        np.prod(axes)
        * quad(lambda s: 1 / ((a**2 + s) * np.sqrt(np.prod(axes**2 + s))), 0, np.inf)[0]
        for a in axes
    ]
)
added = volume * alpha / (2 - alpha)
rayleigh = (
    lambda k, points: amplitude
    * k**2
    * np.exp(-1j * k * np.linalg.norm(points, axis=1))
    / (4 * np.pi * np.linalg.norm(points, axis=1))
    * (
        -volume
        + (1 - 1j / (k * np.linalg.norm(points, axis=1)))
        * (
            (points / np.linalg.norm(points, axis=1)[:, None])
            @ ((volume + added) * direction)
        )
    )
)
print(
    f"V = {1e6 * volume:.1f} cm3; Lamb coefficients alpha = {np.round(alpha, 4)} (sum {alpha.sum():.4f}); "
    f"added mass / V = {np.round(added / volume, 4)}"
)

Results

The scattered pressure on 400 points spread over a sphere of radius \(R/2\) around the body, against the low-frequency law.

golden = np.pi * (3 - np.sqrt(5)) * np.arange(400)
heights = 1 - (np.arange(400) + 0.5) / 200
directions = np.column_stack(
    (
        np.sqrt(1 - heights**2) * np.cos(golden),
        np.sqrt(1 - heights**2) * np.sin(golden),
        heights,
    )
)
deviation = {}
for ka, run in runs.items():
    k = ka / axes[0]
    points = run["outer"] / 2 * directions
    computed, law = np.asarray(
        run["space"].evaluate(run["pressure"], points)
    ), rayleigh(k, points)
    deviation[ka] = np.max(np.abs(computed - law)) / np.max(np.abs(law))
    print(
        f"ka = {ka:.2f} ({run['dofs']} unknowns, R = {run['outer']:.1f} m): largest deviation from the low-frequency law "
        f"{deviation[ka]:.3f} of its maximum; RMS |p_s| {np.sqrt(np.mean(np.abs(computed) ** 2)):.3e} Pa "
        f"(law {np.sqrt(np.mean(np.abs(law) ** 2)):.3e} Pa)"
    )
orders = [
    np.log(deviation[b] / deviation[a]) / np.log(b / a)
    for a, b in ((0.25, 0.5), (0.5, 1.0))
]
print(
    f"observed order of the deviation in ka: {orders[0]:.2f}, {orders[1]:.2f} (about 2 expected; the reflection of the outer condition adds a floor)"
)

Figure

Left: the total pressure on the ellipsoid at \(ka=1\) - largest where the wave arrives, smallest in the shadow. Middle: the scattered amplitude at the 400 receivers at \(ka=0.25\), simulation against law; on the diagonal they agree. Right: the deviation against \(ka\).

fine = runs[1.0]
surface = np.asarray(fine["mesh"].facets(parts[1.0].boundaries.body))
nodes = np.unique(surface)
points = np.asarray(fine["mesh"].coordinates)[nodes]
total = np.abs(
    np.asarray(fine["space"].evaluate(fine["pressure"], points))
    + incident(1.0 / axes[0], points)
)
low = runs[0.25]
receivers = low["outer"] / 2 * directions
simulated, law = np.abs(
    np.asarray(low["space"].evaluate(low["pressure"], receivers))
), np.abs(rayleigh(0.25 / axes[0], receivers))
fig = plots.figure(1, 3, size=(22, 6.5))
plots.trisurface(
    points,
    np.searchsorted(nodes, surface),
    total,
    camera=view,
    label="|p| / p0",
    title="Total pressure on the ellipsoid, ka = 1",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.line(
    points={"receivers on r = R/2": (1e3 * law, 1e3 * simulated)},
    reference={"equality": ([0, 1e3 * law.max()], [0, 1e3 * law.max()])},
    xlabel="|p_s|, low-frequency law [mPa]",
    ylabel="|p_s|, simulation [mPa]",
    title="Scattered amplitude, ka = 0.25",
    legend="upper left",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.line(
    np.array(list(deviation)),
    {"largest deviation": np.array(list(deviation.values()))},
    markers=True,
    slope=2.0,
    scale="loglog",
    xlabel="ka",
    ylabel="deviation from the low-frequency law",
    title="Approach to the low-frequency law",
    ax=fig[2],
)
plots.show()

Going further

Raise \(ka\) beyond 1 in ellipsoid_scattering_geometry.py (outer sphere \(8/k\)) to enter the region where the ellipsoid casts a real shadow and the low-frequency law no longer holds; or turn the incidence onto a principal axis, where the field becomes axisymmetric for a spheroid.
