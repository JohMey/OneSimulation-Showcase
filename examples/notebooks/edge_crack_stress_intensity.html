Stress intensity factor of an edge crack

A steel plate with an edge crack is pulled at its ends, for four crack lengths, and the stress intensity factor at the crack tip is computed.

STEP importJ-integralQuadratic trianglesMesh study
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)Download the geometry script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation reads each cracked plate from a STEP file, cuts it to its cross-section, meshes it with quadratic triangles graded towards the crack tip and solves plane-stress elasticity; the J-integral is evaluated as a domain integral on three rings around the tip and converted to K_I. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

K_I agrees with the handbook solution of Tada, Paris and Irwin within -0.57 % to +0.10 % for a/W = 0.2 to 0.5, the three rings agree within 0.3 %, the mesh study changes K_I by 0.26 % between the two finest meshes, and the crack opening follows Irwin's profile.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Whether an existing crack grows is decided in linear elastic fracture mechanics by the stress intensity factor \(K_I\): near the tip the stresses grow like \(K_I/\sqrt{2\pi r}\), and the crack becomes unstable when \(K_I\) reaches the fracture toughness \(K_{Ic}\) of the material. \(K_I\) depends on the load, the crack length and the shape of the part, so it has to be computed.

The most robust way is the J-integral of Rice, the energy released per unit crack advance. As a domain integral over a ring around the tip it reads

\[J=\int_A\Big(\sigma_{ij}\,\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_1}-W\,\delta_{1j}\Big)\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_j}\,\mathrm dA , \qquad K_I=\sqrt{E\,J}\quad\text{(plane stress)},\]

with the strain energy density \(W\) and a weight \(q\) that is 1 inside the ring and 0 outside. \(J\) is the same for every ring; checking that is part of the result.

Setup. A steel plate (\(E=200\) GPa, \(\nu=0.3\)), width \(W=100\) mm, height 400 mm, 10 mm thick, with an edge crack of length \(a\) at half height, \(a/W=0.2\), 0.3, 0.4 and 0.5, in plane stress. The plates come from the CAD files edge_crack_stress_intensity_<a/W in %>.step, written by edge_crack_stress_intensity_geometry.py with the CAD objects of OneSimulation; the crack is a sharp wedge 0.2 mm wide at its mouth.

| side | condition | |---|---| | y_min (bottom) | held, \(u_x=u_y=0\) | | y_max (top) | tension \(\sigma=100\) MPa | | x_min, x_max, crack.side | traction free |

Reference. H. Tada, P.C. Paris and G.R. Irwin, The Stress Analysis of Cracks Handbook, 3rd ed., ASME Press 2000: \(K_I=\sigma\sqrt{\pi a}\,F(a/W)\) with \(F=1.12-0.231\,\alpha+10.55\,\alpha^2-21.72\,\alpha^3+30.39\,\alpha^4\), \(\alpha=a/W\), accurate to 0.5 % for \(\alpha\le0.6\). Near the tip the crack opens like Irwin's profile \(\delta(r)=(8K_I/E)\sqrt{r/2\pi}\).

import numpy as np

from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher
from onesim.physics.solid_mechanics import SmallStrainSolid, small_strain_j_integral
from onesim.results.comparisons import convergence
from onesim.visualization import theme, plots

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns each plate, section its cross-section at half thickness with the names of the cut faces on its sides. The triangles are 0.5 mm at the crack tip and grow to 10 mm away from it; for the mesh study the plate with \(a/W=0.3\) is also meshed with 2 mm and 1 mm at the tip.

ratios = (0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5)
plates = {ratio: load(f"edge_crack_stress_intensity_{round(100 * ratio)}.step") for ratio in ratios}
width, height, thickness = plates[0.3].size
sections = {ratio: plate.section((0, 0, 1), origin=(0, 0, thickness / 2)) for ratio, plate in plates.items()}
tips = {ratio: np.array([ratio * width, height / 2]) for ratio in ratios}
graded = lambda tip, size: TriangularMesher(target_size=0.01, size_field=lambda p: np.minimum(
    size + 0.3 * np.hypot(p[:, 0] - tip[0], p[:, 1] - tip[1]), 0.01))
meshes = {ratio: graded(tips[ratio], 0.0005).generate(section) for ratio, section in sections.items()}
tip_sizes = [0.002, 0.001, 0.0005]
study_meshes = [graded(tips[0.3], size).generate(sections[0.3]) for size in tip_sizes[:2]] + [meshes[0.3]]

faces = sections[0.3].boundaries
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(12, 7))
plots.geometry(plates[0.3], selection={"held": plates[0.3].boundaries.y_min, "pulled": plates[0.3].boundaries.y_max,
                                       "crack": plates[0.3].boundaries["crack.side"]},
               labels=False, camera=plots.Camera(elevation=30, azimuth=-60), title="Plate with a/W = 0.3", ax=fig[0])
plots.mesh(meshes[0.3], groups=("all",), legend=False, title=f"a/W = 0.3: {meshes[0.3].cell_count} triangles", ax=fig[1])
plots.show()

Model

Plane stress with quadratic triangles. The virtual crack advance \(q\) is a smooth step from 1 inside the radius \(r_1\) to 0 outside \(r_2\); small_strain_j_integral takes its gradient and returns \(J\) per unit thickness. Three rings: 2-6 mm, 4-10 mm and 6-15 mm.

stress, youngs_modulus = 100e6, 200e9
steel = SmallStrainSolid(youngs_modulus=youngs_modulus, poisson_ratio=0.3, kinematics="plane_stress", thickness=thickness)
solve = lambda mesh, faces: steel.solve(mesh=mesh, times=(0.0,), order=2,
                                        displacements=((faces.y_min, "x", 0.0), (faces.y_min, "y", 0.0)),
                                        tractions=((faces.y_max, (0.0, stress)),))
results = {ratio: solve(meshes[ratio], section.boundaries) for ratio, section in sections.items()}
study = [solve(mesh, faces) for mesh in study_meshes]
advance = lambda tip, inner, outer: lambda p: np.einsum("i,q,qj->qij", (1.0, 0.0), -6 * (s := np.clip(
    (np.hypot(*(p - tip).T) - inner) / (outer - inner), 0, 1)) * (1 - s) / ((outer - inner) * np.hypot(*(p - tip).T)), p - tip)
rings = [(0.002, 0.006), (0.004, 0.010), (0.006, 0.015)]
intensity = {ratio: [np.sqrt(youngs_modulus * small_strain_j_integral(result, advance(tips[ratio], *ring))) for ring in rings]
             for ratio, result in results.items()}
study_intensity = [[np.sqrt(youngs_modulus * small_strain_j_integral(result, advance(tips[0.3], *ring))) for ring in rings]
                   for result in study]
tada = lambda a: stress * np.sqrt(np.pi * a * width) * (1.12 - 0.231 * a + 10.55 * a**2 - 21.72 * a**3 + 30.39 * a**4)

Comparison with the reference

\(K_I\) on the three rings for every crack length against the handbook, and the mesh study for \(a/W=0.3\).

print(f"{'a/W':>5s} {'triangles':>10s} {'K_I, ring 1':>12s} {'ring 2':>9s} {'ring 3':>9s} {'Tada':>9s} {'deviation':>10s}  [MPa m^0.5]")
for ratio in ratios:
    values = intensity[ratio]
    print(f"{ratio:5.1f} {meshes[ratio].cell_count:10d} {values[0] / 1e6:12.3f} {values[1] / 1e6:9.3f} {values[2] / 1e6:9.3f}"
          f" {tada(ratio) / 1e6:9.3f} {100 * (np.mean(values) / tada(ratio) - 1):+9.2f} %")
print("mesh study, a/W = 0.3: size at the tip [mm], mean K_I [MPa m^0.5], deviation from Tada")
for size, values in zip(tip_sizes, study_intensity):
    print(f"  {1e3 * size:4.1f} mm   {np.mean(values) / 1e6:8.3f}   {100 * (np.mean(values) / tada(0.3) - 1):+6.2f} %")
print(f"change between the two finest meshes: {100 * convergence([np.mean(v) for v in study_intensity], tip_sizes).change:.2f} %")

Figure

The vertical displacement around the crack of \(a/W=0.3\), the normalised stress intensity \(F=K_I/\sigma\sqrt{\pi a}\) against the handbook curve, the mesh study on the three rings, and the crack opening behind the tip against Irwin's profile.

result, tip = results[0.3], tips[0.3]
nodes = len(meshes[0.3].coordinates)
behind = np.linspace(2e-4, 0.25 * tip[0], 60)
opening = (result.evaluate_displacement(np.column_stack((tip[0] - behind, np.full_like(behind, tip[1] + 6e-4))))[:, 1]
           - result.evaluate_displacement(np.column_stack((tip[0] - behind, np.full_like(behind, tip[1] - 6e-4))))[:, 1])
alpha = np.linspace(0.15, 0.55, 81)
fig = plots.figure(2, 2, size=(14, 11))
plots.heatmap(1e3 * result.coordinates[:nodes, 0], 1e3 * result.coordinates[:nodes, 1], 1e6 * result.displacement[0, :nodes, 1],
              triangles=np.asarray(meshes[0.3].cells), limits=(60, 240), contours=12, label="u_y [µm]",
              xlabel="x [mm]", ylabel="y [mm]", xlimits=(0, 60), ylimits=(180, 220), title="Vertical displacement, a/W = 0.3", ax=fig[0])
plots.line(points={f"ring {k + 1}": (np.array(ratios), [intensity[ratio][k] / (stress * np.sqrt(np.pi * ratio * width)) for ratio in ratios])
                   for k in range(3)},
           reference={"Tada, Paris and Irwin": (alpha, tada(alpha) / (stress * np.sqrt(np.pi * alpha * width)))},
           xlabel="a / W", ylabel="F = K_I / (σ √(πa))", title="Geometry factor of the edge crack", legend="upper left", ax=fig[1])
plots.line(points={f"ring {k + 1}": (1e3 * np.array(tip_sizes), [values[k] / 1e6 for values in study_intensity]) for k in range(3)},
           hlines={"Tada, Paris and Irwin": tada(0.3) / 1e6}, ylimits=(0.99 * tada(0.3) / 1e6, 1.005 * tada(0.3) / 1e6),
           xlabel="element size at the tip [mm]", ylabel="K_I [MPa m^0.5]", title="Mesh study, a/W = 0.3", legend="lower left", ax=fig[2])
plots.line(y={"simulation": (1e3 * behind, 1e6 * opening)},
           reference={"Irwin's profile with the handbook K_I": (1e3 * behind, 1e6 * 8 * tada(0.3) / youngs_modulus * np.sqrt(behind / (2 * np.pi)))},
           xlabel="distance behind the tip r [mm]", ylabel="crack opening δ [µm]", title="Crack opening near the tip, a/W = 0.3",
           legend="upper left", ax=fig[3])
plots.show()

Going further

Change the crack length or the plate height in edge_crack_stress_intensity_geometry.py and write the STEP files again; at small heights the bending constraint of the held end lowers \(K_I\). Compare with the fracture toughness of a steel (about 50 MPa m\(^{0.5}\)) to find the critical stress for each crack length.

What you need to run it. An installed, activated OneSimulation runtime (Python 3.13). As a student or course instructor, you have free access. Everyone else can start with the 7-day free trial.
