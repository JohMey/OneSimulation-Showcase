Pressure acoustics · 3D

Acoustic modes of a rigid box

The standing sound waves of a closed rectangular room: axial, tangential and oblique modes, including exactly degenerate pairs.

High-order Q1-Q4 hexahedraShift-invert LanczosExact eigenfrequencies
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation assembles the pressure Helmholtz operator with tensor-product Lagrange hexahedra of order 1 to 4 (and P1-P3 tetrahedra on an unstructured mesh) and solves the generalised eigenproblem with shift-and-invert Lanczos.

What it checks

The first 20 eigenfrequencies converge at the theoretical orders 2, 4 and 6; cubic elements reach 5e-8, mode shapes agree to 1 - MAC = 1e-11, and degenerate pairs stay degenerate.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A closed room or enclosure filled with air only "rings" at discrete frequencies: its acoustic eigenmodes. For small-amplitude sound the pressure \(p(\mathbf x)\,e^{i\omega t}\) obeys the Helmholtz equation

\[\nabla\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_0}\nabla p\Big)+\frac{\omega^2}{\rho_0 c^2}\,p=0 \quad\text{in the box},\qquad \frac{\partial p}{\partial n}=0\quad\text{on all six walls},\]

where the wall condition says that a rigid (sound-hard) wall does not move: the normal particle velocity \(v_n=-\partial_n p/(i\omega\rho_0)\) vanishes. The box measures \(L_x\times L_y\times L_z=1.0\times0.7\times0.5\) m and holds air at 20 °C (\(c=343\) m/s, \(\rho_0=1.204\) kg/m³).

| boundary | condition | |---|---| | walls \(x=0\) and \(x=L_x\) | rigid, \(\partial p/\partial n=0\) | | walls \(y=0\) and \(y=L_y\) | rigid, \(\partial p/\partial n=0\) | | walls \(z=0\) and \(z=L_z\) | rigid, \(\partial p/\partial n=0\) |

Reference (exact solution of the 3D problem). The eigenmodes separate into products of cosines (Lord Rayleigh, The Theory of Sound, Vol. II, §267, 1896; P. M. Morse and K. U. Ingard, Theoretical Acoustics, McGraw-Hill 1968, §9.4),

\[p_{lmn}=\cos\frac{l\pi x}{L_x}\,\cos\frac{m\pi y}{L_y}\,\cos\frac{n\pi z}{L_z},\qquad f_{lmn}=\frac{c}{2}\sqrt{\Big(\frac{l}{L_x}\Big)^2+\Big(\frac{m}{L_y}\Big)^2+\Big(\frac{n}{L_z}\Big)^2}.\]

Modes with one non-zero index are axial, with two tangential and with three oblique. Because \(L_x=2L_z\), the modes \((2,0,0)\) and \((0,0,1)\) have exactly the same frequency (343 Hz), and so do \((2,1,0)\)/\((0,1,1)\) and \((2,2,0)\)/\((0,2,1)\): there the eigenvalue is double and every combination of the two shapes is an eigenmode too. The first twenty non-zero modes reach up to 665 Hz; the twenty-first lies at 686 Hz.

Numerical method. - Spatial: continuous Galerkin finite elements. Primary: tensor-product Lagrange elements \(Q_p\) of degree \(p=1,2,3,4\) on hexahedra; cross-check: Lagrange \(P_p\) (\(p=1,2,3\)) on an unstructured tetrahedral mesh from OneSimulation's CAD mesher. The element stiffness \(\int\rho_0^{-1}\nabla\phi_i\cdot\nabla\phi_j\) and the compressibility mass \(\int(\rho_0c^2)^{-1}\phi_i\phi_j\) come from OneSimulation's acoustic Helmholtz element kernel on the quadrature of each finite-element space. - The rigid walls are the natural boundary condition of this weak form: no wall term enters the matrices, and the computed modes are checked for \(\partial p/\partial n\approx 0\) afterwards. - Eigenproblem: the generalized symmetric eigenvalue problem \(K\mathbf p=\omega^2M\mathbf p\) is solved with shift-and-invert Lanczos iterations (OneSimulation's sparse symmetric eigensolver, sparse LU of \(K-\sigma M\) with a small negative shift \(\sigma\)). The constant pressure mode at \(\omega=0\) is the expected null mode of a closed box. - Time integration: none (eigenvalue problem).

Discretisation error. For a conforming Galerkin method the computed eigenvalues lie above the exact ones and their error falls like \(h^{2p}\) (I. Babuška and J. Osborn, "Eigenvalue problems", Handbook of Numerical Analysis II, 1991). In wave terms this is the dispersion error: on a given mesh the error grows quickly with frequency, i.e. with fewer elements per wavelength, and high-order elements push it down by orders of magnitude at the same number of unknowns.

Parameters

Lx, Ly, Lz = 1.0, 0.7, 0.5          # m, box dimensions
c0 = 343.0                          # m/s, speed of sound in air at 20 C

print("degenerate pairs:", [(indices[i], indices[j]) for j, i in enumerate(group_of) if i != j])


def exact_shape(lmn, points):
    l, m, n = lmn
    x, y, z = np.asarray(points).T
    return np.cos(l * np.pi * x / Lx) * np.cos(m * np.pi * y / Ly) * np.cos(n * np.pi * z / Lz)

Geometry and mesh

The air volume is a Box. The hexahedral meshes are uniform with cubic cells of side \(h=0.1\,\mathrm m/k\) (10 x 7 x 5 cells at \(k=1\), 40 x 28 x 20 at \(k=4\)); the element degree \(p\) adds \(p-1\) nodes per cell edge. The tetrahedral mesh is generated by OneSimulation's CAD mesher with target size 0.1 m. It is genuinely unstructured, so its discrete operator is not a tensor product of one-dimensional ones: it tests the 3D discretisation without any help from the box's separable shape.

geometry = Box(length=Lx, width=Ly, height=Lz)


def hex_mesh(level):
    return StructuredHexahedralMesher(cells=(10 * level, 7 * level, 5 * level)).generate(geometry)


tet_mesh = TetrahedralMesher(target_size=tet_size).generate(geometry)
print(f"unstructured tetrahedral mesh: {tet_mesh.cell_count} cells")

Model

Global stiffness \(K\) and compressibility mass \(M\) are assembled cell by cell from the Lagrange space bound to the mesh; pressure_helmholtz_element evaluates \(\int\rho_0^{-1}\nabla\phi_i\cdot\nabla\phi_j\,\mathrm dV\) and \(\int(\rho_0c^2)^{-1}\phi_i\phi_j\,\mathrm dV\) on the space's own quadrature rule. The rigid walls need no boundary term (natural condition, see the parameters table above).

def acoustic_matrices(mesh, degree):
    space = FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=degree)).bind_space(mesh)
    n, local = space.dof_count, space.cell_dofs.shape[1]
    rows, cols, k_values, m_values = [], [], [], []
    for cell, dofs in enumerate(space.cell_dofs):
        _, weights, (basis, gradients, _) = space.element_data(cell)
        k_cell, m_cell = pressure_helmholtz_element(
            basis, gradients, weights, density=rho0, compressibility=compressibility)
        rows.append(np.repeat(dofs, local))
        cols.append(np.tile(dofs, local))
        k_values.append(np.asarray(k_cell).ravel())
        m_values.append(np.asarray(m_cell).ravel())
    rows, cols = np.concatenate(rows), np.concatenate(cols)
    K = sp.csr_matrix((np.concatenate(k_values), (rows, cols)), shape=(n, n))
    M = sp.csr_matrix((np.concatenate(m_values), (rows, cols)), shape=(n, n))
    return space, K, M


def shift_invert(stiffness, mass, shift):
    factor = splu((stiffness - shift * mass).tocsc())
    return LinearOperator(stiffness.shape, matvec=factor.solve)


eigensolver = SparseSymmetricEigenSolver(shift_inverse=shift_invert)


def acoustic_modes(mesh, degree):
    started = time.perf_counter()
    space, K, M = acoustic_matrices(mesh, degree)
    # Shift sigma = -1 rad^2/s^2 sits just below the null mode; the 21 lowest pairs are
    # the constant mode plus the 20 graded modes.
    frequencies, vectors = pressure_eigenmodes(
        K, M, zero_tolerance=1e-3,
        eigensolver=lambda mass, stiffness: eigensolver(mass, stiffness, mode_count=mode_count + 1,
                                                        target=-1.0))
    return dict(space=space, M=M, f=frequencies[1:], null=frequencies[0], modes=vectors[:, 1:],
                dofs=space.dof_count, seconds=time.perf_counter() - started)

Comparison with the reference

Mode shapes (MAC). The modal assurance criterion compares a computed mode \(\mathbf p_h\) with the exact shape \(\mathbf q\) sampled at the nodes, in the mass inner product: \(\mathrm{MAC}=(\mathbf p_h^\top M\mathbf q)^2/ \big((\mathbf p_h^\top M\mathbf p_h)(\mathbf q^\top M\mathbf q)\big)\), 1 for identical shapes. For a degenerate pair the solver may return any rotation of the two shapes, so the criterion measures the fraction of \(\mathbf p_h\) that lies in the span of both exact shapes (subspace MAC).

def subspace_mac(run):
    space, M, V = run["space"], run["M"], run["modes"]
    exact_nodal = np.column_stack([exact_shape(lmn, space.dof_coordinates) for lmn in indices])
    mac = np.empty(mode_count)
    for j in range(mode_count):
        members = [i for i in range(mode_count) if group_of[i] == group_of[j]]
        Q = exact_nodal[:, members]
        gram = Q.T @ (M @ Q)
        projection = Q.T @ (M @ V[:, j])
        mac[j] = projection @ np.linalg.solve(gram, projection) / (V[:, j] @ (M @ V[:, j]))
    return mac

# **Mesh convergence and observed order.** Error measure: the largest relative
# frequency error over the twenty modes. With $h$ halved the error should fall by

print("\nConvergence of the largest relative frequency error over the 20 modes")

    text = "  ".join(f"h = {10 / k:.2f} cm: {e:.3e}" for k, e in zip(levels, errors))
    print(f"Q{degree}: {text}")

def mode_on_faces(run, vector, n=41):
    """Pressure of a nodal vector on the three visible walls, normalised to max |p| = 1."""
    space = run["space"]
    s, t = np.meshgrid(np.linspace(1e-6, 1 - 1e-6, n), np.linspace(1e-6, 1 - 1e-6, n))
    faces = {
        "x": (np.full_like(s, Lx * (1 - 1e-9)), s * Ly, t * Lz),
        "y": (s * Lx, np.full_like(s, 1e-9 * Ly), t * Lz),
        "z": (s * Lx, t * Ly, np.full_like(s, Lz * (1 - 1e-9))),
    }
    values = {key: np.asarray(space.evaluate(vector, np.column_stack([c.ravel() for c in xyz]))).reshape(s.shape)
              for key, xyz in faces.items()}
    peak = max(np.max(np.abs(v)) for v in values.values())
    corner = np.asarray(space.evaluate(vector, np.array([[Lx * (1 - 1e-9), 1e-9, Lz * (1 - 1e-9)]])))[0]
    sign = 1.0 if corner >= 0 else -1.0
    return faces, {k: sign * v / peak for k, v in values.items()}


def rotated_pair(run, members):
    """Rotate the computed degenerate eigenspace onto the exact shapes of the pair."""
    space, M, V = run["space"], run["M"], run["modes"][:, members]
    out = []
    for i in members:
        q = exact_shape(indices[i], space.dof_coordinates)
        out.append(V @ np.linalg.solve(V.T @ (M @ V), V.T @ (M @ q)))
    return out

Going further

Make one wall absorbing: a locally reacting impedance \(Z\) adds the boundary matrix \(i\omega\int Z^{-1}\phi_i\phi_j\,\mathrm dS\) and turns the problem into a quadratic eigenproblem with complex frequencies whose imaginary parts give the reverberation decay. Or replace the box by a room with a non-rectangular floor plan from the CAD kernel: the unstructured tetrahedral route above carries over unchanged, while the exact cosine modes no longer exist and the mesh study becomes the reference.
