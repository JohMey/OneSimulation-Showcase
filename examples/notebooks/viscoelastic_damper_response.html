Viscoelasticity · 3D

Viscoelastic structural damper

A building damper of steel plates bonded by two viscoelastic layers under harmonic wind or seismic load: stiffness, loss factor and the energy dissipated per cycle.

Frequency-domain FEMProny seriesRecursive-convolution time stepping
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation assembles 3D elasticity with quadratic tetrahedra on its P2 space and solves the steady harmonic response with the exact complex modulus of an 18-term Prony series, then integrates the same model in time with Prony internal variables and an exponential recursive-convolution update.

What it checks

Pin work and layer dissipation balance to 3e-11, the response is within a few percent of the simple-shear limit for the right physical reasons, the dissipated energy is mesh-converged to 0.4 %, and time and frequency domain agree.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Viscoelastic dampers reduce the wind-induced sway and the seismic response of tall buildings: a stiff steel frame forces thin layers of a soft polymer into shear, and the polymer turns part of every vibration cycle into heat. The device studied here (K. L. Shen and T. T. Soong, J. Eng. Mech. 121, 694-701, 1995; S. W. Park, Int. J. Solids Struct. 38, 8065-8092, 2001) has a central steel plate, bolted to the structure through two holes at the bottom, and two outer steel plates, each bonded to the central plate by one viscoelastic layer and pinned to the bracing at the top. The plates lie in the \((y,z)\) plane and are stacked along \(x\):

| part | position along \(x\) | footprint in \((y,z)\) | |---|---|---| | left plate, 10 mm | 0 ... 10 mm | 50 × 50 mm pad plus an upward tab with one Ø16 mm pin hole | | viscoelastic layer 1, 10 mm | 10 ... 20 mm | 50 × 50 mm | | central plate, 10 mm | 20 ... 30 mm | pad plus a downward tab with two Ø16 mm bolt holes | | viscoelastic layer 2, 10 mm | 30 ... 40 mm | 50 × 50 mm | | right plate, 10 mm | 40 ... 50 mm | like the left plate |

The original description gives the device only as a drawing; the dimensions above are a reconstruction with the same layout: 10 mm plates and Ø16 mm pin holes (consistent with the 16 mm × 10 mm bearing area of the original load definition) and 50 × 50 × 10 mm layers.

Material. The layers follow the generalized Maxwell model of Park (2001): an equilibrium spring \(G_\infty\) in parallel with 18 spring-dashpot branches \((G_k,\tau_k)\) acting on the strain deviator, with an elastic bulk modulus \(K\),

\[\boldsymbol\sigma = K\,\mathrm{tr}\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon\,\mathbf I + 2\int_{0}^{t} G(t-s)\,\dot{\mathbf e}(s)\,\mathrm ds,\qquad G(t)=G_\infty+\sum_{k=1}^{18}G_k\,e^{-t/\tau_k}.\]

For harmonic motion \(e^{i\omega t}\) the relaxation integral becomes the exact complex shear modulus (Ferry, Viscoelastic Properties of Polymers, 3rd ed., Wiley 1980)

\[G^*(\omega)=G'+iG''=G_\infty+\sum_k G_k\,\frac{i\omega\tau_k}{1+i\omega\tau_k},\qquad G'=G_\infty+\sum_k G_k\frac{\omega^2\tau_k^2}{1+\omega^2\tau_k^2},\quad G''=\sum_k G_k\frac{\omega\tau_k}{1+\omega^2\tau_k^2}.\]

The plates are steel (AISI 4340), linear elastic.

Loads and supports (as in the original set-up). The two bottom bolt holes are fixed. The right pin hole carries the vertical load \(8.5\,\text{MPa}\cdot\cos(\omega t)\) and is guided in \(x\) and \(y\); the left pin hole carries \((0.5,\,0,\,8.5)\,\text{MPa}\cdot \sin(\omega t)\) and is guided in \(y\); \(f=3\) Hz. The loads are tractions on the hole surfaces whose resultant is \(8.5\,\text{MPa}\times\pi\times16\,\text{mm}\times10\,\text{mm} = 4.27\) kN per plate. All other faces are traction-free.

Methods. (1) Frequency domain: by the elastic-viscoelastic correspondence principle the steady harmonic state solves one complex linear problem \((\mathbf K_\text{steel}+\mathbf K_K+G^*(\omega)\,\mathbf B-\omega^2\mathbf M)\,\hat{\mathbf u} =\hat{\mathbf f}\), where \(\mathbf B\) is the deviatoric stiffness of the layers for a unit shear modulus. All matrices are assembled from OneSimulation's continuous Galerkin space of quadratic (P2) Lagrange tetrahedra on a block-structured, graded mesh of the whole device; the complex system is solved with a sparse direct LU factorisation (nested-dissection ordering from PETSc, one step of iterative refinement). (2) Time domain: each Prony branch keeps a history vector \(\mathbf h_k\) with \(\dot{\mathbf h}_k+\mathbf h_k/\tau_k=\dot{\mathbf u}\), integrated with the exponential (recursive-convolution) rule of R. L. Taylor, K. S. Pister and G. L. Goudreau (Int. J. Numer. Meth. Eng. 2, 45-59, 1970). It is exact for motion that is linear within a step and unconditionally stable for all 18 relaxation times from \(10^{-7}\) to \(10^{3}\) s; it is second-order accurate for the branches with \(\tau_k\gg\Delta t\) and acts as a backward-Euler dashpot for the much faster ones, so the overall convergence rate lies between one and two. The effective stiffness is constant, so it is factorised once per time-step size. The start-up from rest is followed for 3 s (nine cycles) with the loads ramped in over 0.1 s, as in the original study; separate runs that start from the exact steady state measure the integrator error alone.

Reference. There is no closed-form solution for the full device. The notebook uses (i) the exact storage and loss moduli of the Prony series above; (ii) the closed-form simple-shear model of one layer between rigid plates, \(\hat w=\hat F h/(G^*A)\), phase lag \(\delta=\arg G^*\) and dissipated energy per cycle \(W=\pi\,G''\,\hat\gamma^2\,V =\pi\,\hat\tau^2\,G''V/|G^*|^2\), which the 3D field must approach (it differs by the free-edge, bending and steel-compliance effects that only the 3D model contains); (iii) the energy balance: the work of the pin forces per cycle (the hysteresis-loop area) must equal the dissipation integrated over the layers; (iv) a three-level mesh study with observed order and Richardson extrapolation; (v) the time-domain solution, which must converge to the frequency-domain motion as the time step shrinks; (vi) the assembled stiffness, checked against OneSimulation's SmallStrainSolid solver on the same mesh with the real storage modulus.

def complex_modulus(f):
    """Exact complex shear modulus G* of the Prony series at frequency f (Hz)."""
    w = 2 * np.pi * np.atleast_1d(f)[:, None]
    return G_inf + np.sum(G_branch * 1j * w * tau_branch / (1 + 1j * w * tau_branch), axis=1)

Geometry and mesh

The in-plane shape (pad, tabs and the three holes) is meshed with quadrilateral blocks (transfinite interpolation): the pad is graded towards its four free edges, where the layers have their edge zones, and every hole is surrounded by an O-grid of four blocks graded towards the hole. Every quadrilateral is split into two triangles, the plane mesh is extruded along \(x\) through the five parts (pad + up tab for the outer plates, pad + down tab for the central plate, pad only for the layers), and each prism is split into three tetrahedra with a vertex-ordering rule that keeps neighbouring faces conforming. The layers and plates therefore share their nodes on the bonded faces, and every tetrahedron lies in one material. Level \(L\) uses \(2L\) cells per quarter hole, \(2L\) cells across each layer and \(L+1\) across each plate; the displacement is quadratic (P2).

def line(p, q):
    p, q = np.asarray(p, float), np.asarray(q, float)
    return lambda t: (1 - t)[:, None] * p + t[:, None] * q


def arc(centre, radius, a0, a1):
    c = np.asarray(centre, float)
    return lambda t: c + radius * np.column_stack((np.cos(a0 + (a1 - a0) * t), np.sin(a0 + (a1 - a0) * t)))


def transfinite(bottom, top, left, right, u, v):
    B, T, L, R = bottom(u), top(u), left(v), right(v)
    U, V = np.meshgrid(u, v, indexing="ij")
    U, V = U[..., None], V[..., None]
    return ((1 - V) * B[:, None] + V * T[:, None] + (1 - U) * L[None] + U * R[None]
            - (1 - U) * (1 - V) * B[0] - U * (1 - V) * B[-1] - (1 - U) * V * T[0] - U * V * T[-1])


def graded(n, ratio):
    """n cells on [0, 1]; the last cell is `ratio` times the first."""
    g = ratio ** (1.0 / max(n - 1, 1))
    sizes = g ** np.arange(n)
    x = np.r_[0.0, np.cumsum(sizes)] / sizes.sum()
    x[-1] = 1.0
    return x


def two_sided(n, ratio):
    """n cells on [0, 1], refined towards both ends (centre cells `ratio` times larger)."""
    sizes = 1.0 + (ratio - 1.0) * np.sin(np.pi * (np.arange(n) + 0.5) / n)
    x = np.r_[0.0, np.cumsum(sizes)] / sizes.sum()
    x[-1] = 1.0
    return x


def plane_blocks(L):
    """Quadrilateral blocks of the (y, z) footprint: (region, bottom, top, left, right, u, v)."""
    n_tab, n_radial, n_margin, n_pad = 2 * L, L + 1, L + 1, 4 * L + 2
    across = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, n_tab + 1)
    margin = graded(n_margin, 3.0)                       # fine at the free pad edge
    z_pad = two_sided(n_pad, 3.0)
    radial = graded(n_radial, 1 / 2.5)                   # fine at the hole
    blocks = []
    for ya, yb, u in ((0.0, tab_y0, margin), (tab_y0, tab_y1, across), (tab_y1, pad_w, 1 - margin[::-1])):
        blocks.append(("pad", line((ya, 0), (yb, 0)), line((ya, pad_h), (yb, pad_h)),
                       line((ya, 0), (ya, pad_h)), line((yb, 0), (yb, pad_h)), u, z_pad))
    for region, za, zb, n in (("down", -35 * mm, 0.0, 2 * L + 1), ("up", pad_h, 112 * mm, 4 * L + 2)):
        blocks.append((region, line((tab_y0, za), (tab_y1, za)), line((tab_y0, zb), (tab_y1, zb)),
                       line((tab_y0, za), (tab_y0, zb)), line((tab_y1, za), (tab_y1, zb)),
                       across, np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, n + 1)))
    for region, zc in (("down", z_bolts[0]), ("down", z_bolts[1]), ("up", z_pin)):
        z0, z1 = zc - 15 * mm, zc + 15 * mm
        corners = ((tab_y0, z0), (tab_y1, z0), (tab_y1, z1), (tab_y0, z1))
        for k in range(4):
            a = (1.25 + 0.5 * k) * np.pi
            ends = [np.array((y_c, zc)) + r_hole * np.array((np.cos(t), np.sin(t))) for t in (a, a + np.pi / 2)]
            blocks.append((region, line(corners[k], corners[(k + 1) % 4]), arc((y_c, zc), r_hole, a, a + np.pi / 2),
                           line(corners[k], ends[0]), line(corners[(k + 1) % 4], ends[1]), across, radial))
    return blocks


def plane_mesh(L):
    points, index, quads, regions = [], {}, [], []
    for region, bottom, top, left, right, u, v in plane_blocks(L):
        X = transfinite(bottom, top, left, right, u, v)
        ids = np.empty(X.shape[:2], dtype=int)
        for i in range(X.shape[0]):
            for j in range(X.shape[1]):
                key = (round(X[i, j, 0] / 1e-9), round(X[i, j, 1] / 1e-9))
                if key not in index:
                    index[key] = len(points)
                    points.append(X[i, j])
                ids[i, j] = index[key]
        for i in range(X.shape[0] - 1):
            for j in range(X.shape[1] - 1):
                quads.append((ids[i, j], ids[i + 1, j], ids[i + 1, j + 1], ids[i, j + 1]))
                regions.append(region)
    return np.array(points), np.array(quads), np.array(regions)


PRISM_ROTATIONS = np.array([[0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5], [1, 2, 0, 4, 5, 3], [2, 0, 1, 5, 3, 4],
                            [3, 5, 4, 0, 2, 1], [4, 3, 5, 1, 0, 2], [5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0]])


def split_prism(vertices):
    v = np.asarray(vertices)[PRISM_ROTATIONS[int(np.argmin(vertices))]]
    if min(v[1], v[5]) < min(v[2], v[4]):
        return [(v[0], v[1], v[2], v[5]), (v[0], v[1], v[5], v[4]), (v[0], v[4], v[5], v[3])]
    return [(v[0], v[1], v[2], v[4]), (v[0], v[4], v[2], v[5]), (v[0], v[4], v[5], v[3])]


PARTS = (("left", 0.0, t_plate, ("pad", "up")), ("layer_1", t_plate, h_layer, ("pad",)),
         ("centre", t_plate + h_layer, t_plate, ("pad", "down")),
         ("layer_2", 2 * t_plate + h_layer, h_layer, ("pad",)),
         ("right", 2 * t_plate + 2 * h_layer, t_plate, ("pad", "up")))


def layer_index(x):
    """1 or 2 inside the viscoelastic layers, 0 in the steel."""
    x = np.asarray(x)
    return np.where((x > t_plate) & (x < t_plate + h_layer), 1,
                    np.where((x > 2 * t_plate + h_layer) & (x < 2 * t_plate + 2 * h_layer), 2, 0))


@dataclass(frozen=True, eq=False, kw_only=True)
class Damper(Geometry):
    """Steel plates and viscoelastic layers with named hole surfaces."""

    dimension = 3

    @property
    def volume(self):
        return self.domain()

    def boundary_groups(self):
        names = ("bottom_holes", "left_hole", "right_hole", "free")
        return {**{name: (name,) for name in names}, "all": names}


def build_mesh(L):
    plane, quads, regions = plane_mesh(L)
    triangles, tri_regions = [], []
    for (a, b, c, d), region in zip(quads, regions):
        if np.linalg.norm(plane[a] - plane[c]) <= np.linalg.norm(plane[b] - plane[d]):
            triangles += [(a, b, c), (a, c, d)]
        else:
            triangles += [(a, b, d), (b, c, d)]
        tri_regions += [region, region]
    triangles, tri_regions = np.array(triangles), np.array(tri_regions)
    x_nodes, slabs = [0.0], []
    for name, x0, thickness, footprint in PARTS:
        n = 2 * L if name.startswith("layer") else L + 1
        for k in range(1, n + 1):
            x_nodes.append(x0 + thickness * k / n)
            slabs.append(footprint)
    n_plane, node_of, coordinates, cells = len(plane), {}, [], []

    def node(key):
        if key not in node_of:
            node_of[key] = len(coordinates)
            coordinates.append((x_nodes[key // n_plane], *plane[key % n_plane]))
        return node_of[key]

    for k, footprint in enumerate(slabs):
        for a, b, c in triangles[np.isin(tri_regions, footprint)]:
            prism = (a + n_plane * k, b + n_plane * k, c + n_plane * k,
                     a + n_plane * (k + 1), b + n_plane * (k + 1), c + n_plane * (k + 1))
            cells += [tuple(node(int(v)) for v in tet) for tet in split_prism(prism)]
    coordinates, cells = np.array(coordinates), np.array(cells)
    p = coordinates[cells]
    signed = np.einsum("ij,ij->i", p[:, 1] - p[:, 0], np.cross(p[:, 2] - p[:, 0], p[:, 3] - p[:, 0]))
    cells[signed < 0] = cells[signed < 0][:, [0, 2, 1, 3]]
    geometry = Damper()
    groups = {name: [] for name in geometry.boundary_groups()["all"]}
    for face in boundary_faces(cells, coordinates):
        q = coordinates[np.asarray(face.vertices)]
        on_hole = lambda zc: np.all(np.abs(np.hypot(q[:, 1] - y_c, q[:, 2] - zc) - r_hole) < 1e-7)
        if on_hole(z_bolts[0]) or on_hole(z_bolts[1]):
            name = "bottom_holes"
        elif on_hole(z_pin):
            name = "left_hole" if q[:, 0].max() <= t_plate + 1e-9 else "right_hole"
        else:
            name = "free"
        groups[name].append(tuple(face.vertices))
    mesh = UnstructuredSimplexMesh(geometry=geometry, coordinates=coordinates, cells=cells, boundary_facets=groups)
    return geometry, mesh, groups

Model

The stiffness and mass matrices are assembled cell by cell from OneSimulation's P2 tetrahedral Lagrange space (27-point quadrature, exact for the quadratic stiffness and the quartic mass integrands). Steel cells contribute \(\int\mathbf S^T\mathbf C_\text{steel} \mathbf S\); layer cells contribute the bulk part \(K\,\mathbf m\mathbf m^T\) and, separately for each layer, the unit-shear-modulus deviatoric part \(\mathbf B_j\), so that any shear modulus, real or complex, enters as \(G\,\mathbf B_j\). Boundary conditions on every face:

| face | condition | |---|---| | two bottom bolt holes (central plate) | fixed, \(\mathbf u=0\) | | right pin hole | \(u_x=u_y=0\), traction \(t_z=\tilde q\cos\omega t\) | | left pin hole | \(u_y=0\), traction \((t_x,t_z)=(\tilde q/17,\ \tilde q)\sin\omega t\) | | all other faces (plate faces, edges, layer edges, tab ends) | traction-free |

The straight-sided P2 faces approximate each hole by a polygonal cylinder, so the hole traction \(\tilde q\) is scaled from 8.5 MPa by the ratio of the exact to the polygonal hole area: the resultant is exactly the documented 4.27 kN on every mesh.

D_steel = np.asarray(IsotropicHookeMaterial(E_steel, nu_steel).tangent_voigt())
m_vec = np.array([1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0])
D_bulk = K_layer * np.outer(m_vec, m_vec)
D_shear = np.diag([2.0, 2.0, 2.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0]) - 2.0 / 3.0 * np.outer(m_vec, m_vec)   # sigma = G * D_shear eps


def assemble(L):
    """Mesh, P2 space, matrices, pin loads and supports of mesh level L."""
    geometry, mesh, groups = build_mesh(L)
    space = FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2)).bind_space(mesh)
    n = 3 * space.dof_count
    corner_x = np.asarray(space.dof_coordinates)[space.cell_dofs[:, :4], 0].mean(axis=1)
    owner = layer_index(corner_x)
    rows, cols = [], []
    blocks = {key: [] for key in ("steel", "bulk", "B1", "B2", "mass")}
    zero = np.zeros((30, 30))
    for cell, nodes in enumerate(space.cell_dofs):
        _, weights, (basis, gradient, _) = space.element_data(cell, order=3)
        S = voigt_strain_matrix(gradient)                                  # (q, 30, 6)
        SW = (S * weights[:, None, None]).transpose(1, 0, 2).reshape(30, -1)
        local = lambda D: SW @ (S @ D).transpose(1, 0, 2).reshape(30, -1).T
        index = (np.asarray(nodes)[:, None] * 3 + np.arange(3)).ravel()
        rows.append(np.repeat(index, 30))
        cols.append(np.tile(index, 30))
        density = rho_steel if owner[cell] == 0 else rho_layer
        blocks["mass"].append(np.kron(density * (basis.T * weights) @ basis, np.eye(3)))
        blocks["steel"].append(local(D_steel) if owner[cell] == 0 else zero)
        blocks["bulk"].append(local(D_bulk) if owner[cell] else zero)
        blocks["B1"].append(local(D_shear) if owner[cell] == 1 else zero)
        blocks["B2"].append(local(D_shear) if owner[cell] == 2 else zero)
    rows, cols = np.concatenate(rows), np.concatenate(cols)
    matrices = {key: coo_matrix((np.asarray(value).ravel(), (rows, cols)), shape=(n, n)).tocsr()
                for key, value in blocks.items()}
    b = geometry.boundaries

    def pin_load(boundary, direction):
        faces = mesh.facets(boundary)
        traces = [space.trace_data(face) for face in faces]
        polygon_area = sum(trace[2].sum() for trace in traces)
        traction = q_load * (np.pi * 2 * r_hole * t_plate) / polygon_area * np.asarray(direction)
        f = np.zeros(n)
        for cell, _, weights, _, basis, _ in traces:
            index = (np.asarray(space.cell_dofs[cell])[:, None] * 3 + np.arange(3)).ravel()
            f[index] += np.outer(basis.T @ weights, traction).ravel()
        return f

    def nodes_of(boundary):
        return np.asarray(space.boundary_dofs(mesh.facets(boundary)))

    f_right = pin_load(b.right_hole, (0.0, 0.0, 1.0))           # multiplies cos(wt)
    f_left = pin_load(b.left_hole, (0.5 / 8.5, 0.0, 1.0))       # multiplies sin(wt)
    fixed = np.unique(np.concatenate(
        [3 * nodes_of(b.bottom_holes) + c for c in range(3)]
        + [3 * nodes_of(b.right_hole) + c for c in (0, 1)] + [3 * nodes_of(b.left_hole) + 1]))
    return dict(level=L, geometry=geometry, mesh=mesh, groups=groups, space=space, n=n, M=matrices,
                f_right=f_right, f_left=f_left, free=np.setdiff1d(np.arange(n), fixed),
                left_nodes=nodes_of(b.left_hole), right_nodes=nodes_of(b.right_hole))


def direct_solver(A):
    """Sparse direct LU of A with a nested-dissection ordering and one refinement step."""
    pattern = abs(A).tocsr()
    pattern.sort_indices()
    graph = PETSc.Mat().createAIJ(size=pattern.shape, csr=(pattern.indptr.astype(PETSc.IntType),
                                                           pattern.indices.astype(PETSc.IntType), pattern.data))
    order = graph.getOrdering("nd")[0].getIndices()
    graph.destroy()
    lu = splu(A[order][:, order].tocsc(), permc_spec="NATURAL", diag_pivot_thresh=0.0,
              options=dict(SymmetricMode=True))

    def solve(rhs):
        x = np.empty_like(rhs, dtype=np.result_type(rhs, A.dtype))
        x[order] = lu.solve(rhs[order])
        r = rhs - A @ x
        x[order] += lu.solve(r[order])
        return x

    return solve


def harmonic(model, G, w, inertia=True):
    """Complex displacement amplitude for shear modulus G (complex) at angular frequency w."""
    M, free = model["M"], model["free"]
    A = M["steel"] + M["bulk"] + G * (M["B1"] + M["B2"]) - (w**2 * M["mass"] if inertia else 0.0)
    f = model["f_right"] - 1j * model["f_left"]          # cos -> 1, sin -> -i
    u = np.zeros(model["n"], complex)
    u[free] = direct_solver(A.tocsr()[free][:, free])(f[free])
    return u


def observables(model, u, G):
    """Pin-hole displacements, dissipated energy per cycle per layer and work of the loads."""
    U = u.reshape(-1, 3)
    f = model["f_right"] - 1j * model["f_left"]
    return dict(
        w_right=U[model["right_nodes"], 2].mean(), w_left=U[model["left_nodes"], 2].mean(),
        W_1=np.pi * G.imag * np.real(np.conj(u) @ (model["M"]["B1"] @ u)),
        W_2=np.pi * G.imag * np.real(np.conj(u) @ (model["M"]["B2"] @ u)),
        W_loads=-np.pi * np.imag(np.conj(f) @ u))

# **Time domain** on the middle mesh, quasi-static. With a_k = exp(-dt/tau_k) and
# b_k = tau_k (1 - a_k)/dt the branch histories obey h_k(n+1) = a_k h_k(n) + b_k (u(n+1) - u(n)),
# and equilibrium at the new time reads
# (K_steel + K_K + (G_inf + sum G_k b_k) B) u(n+1) = f(n+1) - B sum_k G_k (a_k h_k(n) - b_k u(n)).
# Two runs: (a) the start-up from rest with the ramped loads, over 3 s; (b) the integrator

def ramp(t):
    """Load ramp of the original set-up: 0 before 0.02 s, rising linearly to 1 at 0.12 s."""
    return np.clip(10.0 * (np.asarray(t) - 0.02), 0.0, 1.0)


def time_domain(model, steps_per_period, end_time, start=None):
    """Exponential Prony integration; start=None is rest with ramped loads, else a steady phasor."""
    M, free = model["M"], model["free"]
    B = M["B1"] + M["B2"]
    dt = 1.0 / frequency / steps_per_period
    times = np.arange(0.0, end_time + 0.5 * dt, dt)
    a = np.exp(-dt / tau_branch)
    b_ = tau_branch * (1 - a) / dt
    solve = direct_solver((M["steel"] + M["bulk"] + (G_inf + np.sum(G_branch * b_)) * B).tocsr()[free][:, free])
    load = (lambda t: ramp(t)) if start is None else (lambda t: 1.0)
    if start is None:
        u, h = np.zeros(model["n"]), np.zeros((len(tau_branch), model["n"]))
    else:
        u = np.real(start)
        h = np.real((1j * omega * tau_branch / (1 + 1j * omega * tau_branch))[:, None] * start[None, :])
    history = [u.reshape(-1, 3)]
    for t in times[1:]:
        f = load(t) * (np.cos(omega * t) * model["f_right"] + np.sin(omega * t) * model["f_left"])
        memory = B @ ((G_branch * a) @ h - np.sum(G_branch * b_) * u)
        u_new = np.zeros(model["n"])
        u_new[free] = solve((f - memory)[free])
        h = a[:, None] * h + b_[:, None] * (u_new - u)[None, :]
        u = u_new
        history.append(u.reshape(-1, 3))
    w_left = np.array([U[model["left_nodes"], 2].mean() for U in history])
    w_right = np.array([U[model["right_nodes"], 2].mean() for U in history])
    return times, w_left, w_right

Comparison with the reference

The closed-form simple-shear layer is the limit of rigid plates, no free edges and no bending; the 3D device must come out somewhat softer. The right plate is guided in \(x\) at its pin, so its deviation measures the free-edge zones and the steel compliance only. The left plate is guided in \(y\) only and also carries the small \(x\) force, so it tilts out of its plane and works its layer harder than simple shear predicts.

def richardson(values, h):
    """Observed order and extrapolated value from three meshes with sizes h (h1 > h2 > h3)."""
    f1, f2, f3 = values
    r21, r32 = h[0] / h[1], h[1] / h[2]
    eps21, eps32 = f2 - f1, f3 - f2
    if eps21 == 0 or eps32 == 0 or np.sign(eps21) != np.sign(eps32):
        return np.nan, f3
    g = lambda p: eps21 / eps32 - (r21**p - 1) / (1 - r32**-p)
    p = brentq(g, 0.05, 12.0) if g(0.05) * g(12.0) < 0 else np.nan
    return p, f3 + eps32 / (r32**p - 1)

Going further

Raise the excitation frequency towards 30 Hz: the polymer stiffens (the branches with \(\tau\approx 5\) ms freeze) and the loop narrows in displacement but its loss factor stays above one over a wide band, which is why such materials are chosen. Replace the 18 branches by a pruned set (only branches with \(0.3\ \text{Hz}<1/(2\pi\tau_k)<30\) Hz, the faster ones lumped into a dashpot, the slower ones into the equilibrium spring) and compare the loop with the full series. Doubling the layer thickness halves the stiffness and, for the same force, doubles the energy dissipated per cycle.
