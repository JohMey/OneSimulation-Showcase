Water hammer after a fast valve closure

A valve at the end of a reservoir-fed pipe closes in 0.2 s and sends a pressure wave up and down the pipe.

Pressure wavesMesh studyClosed-form check

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. Runtime Alpha 0.2.1a7 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

What it computes

OneSimulation solves the one-dimensional water-hammer equations for pressure and flow on a staggered pipe grid with an energy-conserving implicit-midpoint step, with a constant-head reservoir and a closing valve as pipe-end components. Three grids show how the steep wave fronts sharpen.

What it checks

The pressure plateau matches the Joukowsky rise rho a v0 = 12.00 bar to four digits and the period 2.00005 s against 4L/a = 2 s; the deviation from the exact characteristic solution falls from 1.4 % to 0.13 % of the rise from 100 to 400 cells, with the pipe energy conserved to 4e-13.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A constant-head reservoir feeds a horizontal pipe of length \(L\) and diameter \(D\) that ends in a valve discharging to the atmosphere. Water flows steadily at the mean velocity \(v_0\). At \(t=0\) the valve starts to close and is shut after the closure time \(t_c\). The decelerated water column is slightly compressible and the pipe wall slightly elastic, so the stopped flow sends a pressure wave up the pipe at the speed \(a\); it is reflected at the reservoir and returns to the valve.

OneSimulation solves the one-dimensional water-hammer equations for the pressure \(p(x,t)\) and the volume flow \(Q(x,t)\),

\[\frac{A}{\rho a^2}\,\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}=0,\qquad \frac{\rho}{A}\,\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} +\frac{f\rho}{2DA^2}\,Q\,|Q|=0,\]

on a staggered pipe grid with an energy-conserving implicit-midpoint time step.

EndCondition
\(x=0\), reservoir\(p=p_r\) (constant head)
\(x=L\), valve\(p=R\,Q\,|Q|/\tau(t)^2\), opening \(\tau\) from 1 to 0 in \(t_c\)

Reference. For a closure faster than the return time \(2L/a\) the pressure at the valve rises by the Joukowsky value (N. Joukowsky, 1898; E. B. Wylie and V. L. Streeter, Fluid Transients in Systems, Prentice Hall 1993, ch. 1-3)

\[\Delta p_J=\rho\,a\,v_0,\]

stays there for \(2L/a\), then drops to \(p_r-\Delta p_J\) for another \(2L/a\): without friction the valve pressure is a square wave of period \(T=4L/a\). The exact solution of the frictionless pipe follows from the characteristics: with the valve wave \(F(t)=Z\,(Q_0-Q_v(t))\), \(Z=\rho a/A\), and the reflection \(-1\) at the reservoir,

\[p_v(t)-p_r=F(t)+2\sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^k\,F\!\left(t-\frac{2kL}{a}\right),\]

where \(Q_v\) is the valve flow during the closure, from the valve law and the characteristic \(p_v=p_r+Z\,(Q_0-Q_v)\).

import numpy as np
from onesim.physics.systems import (
    HydraulicPressurePort,
    HydraulicValvePort,
    WaterHammerNetwork,
    WaterHammerPipe,
)
from onesim.results.sampling import crossing_times
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Pipe and valve

A steel water main of 600 m and 0.5 m diameter with a wave speed of 1200 m/s, fed by a reservoir 150 m above the valve. The valve loss coefficient is chosen so that the initial flow is 1 m/s; the valve closes with a smooth cosine schedule in 0.2 s, a fifth of the return time \(2L/a=1\) s.

density, length, diameter, wave_speed, velocity = 1000.0, 600.0, 0.5, 1200.0, 1.0
reservoir_pressure, closure_time = density * 9.81 * 150.0, 0.2
area = np.pi * diameter**2 / 4
flow = velocity * area
resistance = reservoir_pressure / flow**2
impedance = density * wave_speed / area
closing = lambda t: 0.5 + 0.5 * np.cos(np.pi * np.clip(t / closure_time, 0.0, 1.0))
joukowsky, period = density * wave_speed * velocity, 4.0 * length / wave_speed
print(
    f"initial flow {flow * 1e3:.1f} L/s, reservoir pressure {reservoir_pressure / 1e5:.2f} bar"
)
print(
    f"Joukowsky rise ρ a v0 = {joukowsky / 1e5:.2f} bar, period 4L/a = {period:.2f} s, closure in {closure_time / (2 * length / wave_speed):.0%} of 2L/a"
)

Simulation on three grids

Eight seconds (four periods) on 100, 200 and 400 cells with a time step of half the cell transit time. The pipe starts in the steady state: uniform reservoir pressure and the initial flow everywhere.

times = np.linspace(0.0, 8.0, 1601)
runs = {
    cells: WaterHammerNetwork(
        pipes=(
            WaterHammerPipe(
                name="main",
                start="reservoir",
                end="valve",
                length=length,
                diameter=diameter,
                wave_speed=wave_speed,
                cells=cells,
            ),
        ),
        ports=(
            HydraulicPressurePort(node="reservoir", pressure=reservoir_pressure),
            HydraulicValvePort(
                node="valve",
                downstream_pressure=0.0,
                resistance=resistance,
                opening=closing,
            ),
        ),
        density=density,
    ).solve(
        times=times,
        max_step=0.5 * length / wave_speed / cells,
        initial_pressure=reservoir_pressure,
        initial_flow=flow,
    )
    for cells in (100, 200, 400)
}
result = runs[400]
surge = {
    cells: run.pressure["main"][:, -1] - reservoir_pressure
    for cells, run in runs.items()
}
distance = {
    cells: length - run.pressure_coordinates["main"][-1] for cells, run in runs.items()
}

Characteristic solution

The pressure is compared in the cell next to the valve, half a cell upstream of it. At the distance \(d\) from the valve the outgoing wave arrives \(d/a\) later and the wave reflected at the reservoir \(d/a\) earlier than at the valve itself. The valve wave \(F(t)\) solves the quadratic valve law along the characteristic; before \(t=0\) it is zero by construction.

def characteristic_surge(t, d):
    total = reservoir_pressure + impedance * flow
    wave = lambda s: impedance * (
        flow
        - 2
        * total
        * closing(s)
        / (
            impedance * closing(s)
            + np.sqrt((impedance * closing(s)) ** 2 + 4 * total * resistance)
        )
    )
    return sum(
        (-1) ** k
        * (
            wave(t - (2 * k * length + d) / wave_speed)
            - wave(t - (2 * (k + 1) * length - d) / wave_speed)
        )
        for k in range(9)
    )


exact = {cells: characteristic_surge(times, d) for cells, d in distance.items()}

Comparison

The plateau pressure is the median of the surge in the first half period after the closure; the period comes from the interpolated downward zero crossings. The overshoot at the steep wave fronts and the rms deviation from the exact solution shrink as the grid is refined.

plateau = (times > 2 * closure_time) & (times < 2 * length / wave_speed)
crossings = {
    cells: crossing_times(times, p, 0.0, direction="falling")
    for cells, p in surge.items()
}
print(
    " cells   plateau rise [bar]   Joukowsky [bar]   peak [bar]   period [s]   rms deviation from exact"
)
for cells, p in surge.items():
    print(
        f"{cells:6d} {np.median(p[plateau]) / 1e5:17.4f} {joukowsky / 1e5:17.4f} {p.max() / 1e5:12.3f} "
        f"{np.mean(np.diff(crossings[cells])):12.5f} {np.sqrt(np.mean((p - exact[cells]) ** 2)) / joukowsky:15.2%}"
    )
print(
    f"period 4L/a = {period:.5f} s;  valve flow after closure: {np.abs(result.node_inflow['valve'][times > closure_time]).max():.1e} m³/s"
)
energy = result.stored_energy + result.dissipated_energy - result.boundary_work
print(
    f"energy balance of the pipe (400 cells): drift {np.ptp(energy) / energy[0]:.1e} of the initial energy"
)

Figure

The pressure next to the valve on the three grids against the characteristic solution, and the pressure in the whole pipe over the first two periods: the fronts travel at \(a\) and reverse sign at the reservoir. Dotted lines: the Joukowsky values \(\pm\rho a v_0\).

window = times <= 2.0 * period
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(15, 5))
plots.line(
    times,
    {f"{cells} cells": p / 1e5 for cells, p in surge.items()},
    reference={"characteristic solution": exact[400] / 1e5},
    hlines=[joukowsky / 1e5, -joukowsky / 1e5],
    xlimits=(0.0, 3.5),
    xlabel="time t [s]",
    ylabel="pressure surge p - p_r [bar]",
    title="Pressure next to the valve",
    legend="lower left",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.heatmap(
    result.pressure_coordinates["main"],
    times[window],
    (result.pressure["main"][window] - reservoir_pressure).T / 1e5,
    colormap="diverging",
    centre=0.0,
    limits=(-1.1 * joukowsky / 1e5, 1.1 * joukowsky / 1e5),
    label="p - p_r [bar]",
    xlabel="position along the pipe x [m]",
    ylabel="time t [s]",
    aspect="auto",
    title="Pressure waves in the pipe",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Going further

Close the valve more slowly than \(2L/a\) and the first relief wave from the reservoir arrives before the closure is complete: the peak falls below \(\rho a v_0\) (Michaud's estimate \(\Delta p\approx 2\rho L v_0/t_c\)). Give the pipe a darcy_friction and the square wave decays, with the line-packing rise above the Joukowsky value. A HydraulicPumpPort instead of the reservoir, or a branch to a second valve, turns the case into the classical pump-trip and network studies.
