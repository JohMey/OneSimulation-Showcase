Temperature field in a cooling flange

A fused-silica flange with three cooling fins: hot process fluid inside, air cooling outside that depends on the angle around the pipe.

ConductionNatural convectionMesh convergence
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation solves steady 3D heat conduction with quadratic elements on a graded mesh of the finned flange, with forced convection in the bore and an angle- and temperature-dependent natural-convection coefficient on the outside, iterated to the nonlinear boundary condition.

What it checks

The heat entering through the bore equals the heat leaving to the air to 0.01 %, and the cooling power and the extreme temperatures converge at second order over three meshes, changing by 0.2 % and 0.05 K between the last two.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Reaction fluids are often cooled by passing them through a glass pipe with cooling flanges: the fluid heats the bore, the heat is conducted through the glass into the flanges, and the surrounding room air carries it away by natural convection. The pipe axis is horizontal (\(x\)), gravity points along \(-y\).

The glass is described by the stationary heat equation

\[\nabla\cdot(k\nabla T)=0 .\]

The bore sees forced convection to the process fluid with a constant coefficient, \(-k\,\partial_n T=h_i\,(T-T_i)\). On all outer surfaces the free-convection film coefficient is taken from the laminar correlation for a horizontal cylinder of diameter \(D\) (the flange outer diameter), with a local factor \(f(\theta)\) that depends on the angle \(\theta\) measured from the lowest point of the cylinder (B. Sundén, Kompendium i värmeöverföring, Sec. 10-3, Lund Institute of Technology, 2003):

\[h_a=\frac{k_\mathrm{air}}{D}\,f(\theta)\,Gr^{1/4},\qquad Gr=\frac{g\,\beta\,(T-T_a)\,D^3}{\nu^2},\qquad \beta=1/T_a ,\]

so the outward flux \(h_a(T-T_a)\propto f(\theta)\,(T-T_a)^{5/4}\) is nonlinear in the surface temperature and varies around the circumference. The temperature therefore depends on all three coordinates: axially (pipe, flanges), radially (wall, fins) and around the axis (stronger cooling at the bottom than at the top).

OneSimulation solves the problem with quadratic (Q2) Lagrange finite elements on a block-structured hexahedral mesh of the half flange \(z\ge 0\); the vertical plane through the axis is a plane of symmetry because gravity lies in it. Boundary conditions on every face:

| face | condition | |---|---| | bore | forced convection to the process fluid, \(h_i=15\) W/(m² K), \(T_i=363\) K | | all outer surfaces (pipe, flange shoulders, fins, fin tips) | natural convection \(h_a(\theta, T)\) to air at \(T_a=298\) K | | the two annular end faces of the pipe | thermally insulated (zero flux) | | vertical symmetry plane \(z=0\) | symmetry (zero flux) |

The nonlinear, angle-dependent air-side law is a NonlinearHeatFlux: the outward flux is given directly as a function of the surface temperature and position, together with its slope \(\partial q/\partial T\), and the solver applies it with Newton steps.

Reference. There is no closed-form solution for this geometry. The notebook uses (i) the global energy balance (heat entering through the bore equals heat leaving to the air), (ii) a three-level mesh-convergence study of the total heat rate and of the peak and minimum temperatures with observed order and Richardson extrapolation, and (iii) as a sanity bound only, the 1D radial conduction estimate through the pipe wall near the insulated pipe end, where the field is closest to one-dimensional.

import time
import numpy as np

from onesim.materials import IsotropicSolid
from onesim.meshing import Block, Geometric, arc, bezier, line, merge_blocks, revolve
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.numerics.nonlinear import Newton
from onesim.physics.heat_transfer import ConvectiveHeatFlux, HeatConduction, HeatFlux, NonlinearHeatFlux
from onesim.results.comparisons import convergence
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.visualization import theme, plots

theme.set()

Parameters

Flange geometry: a glass pipe of 16 mm bore with a 4 mm wall carries three flanges, 4 mm wide and 10 mm tall, with filleted shoulders and rounded tips; a 12 mm long connecting pipe with a 3 mm wall joins on one side and widens smoothly to the flange wall. Fused silica: \(k=1.38\) W/(m K) (Incropera et al., Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer, Table A.3, 300 K). Air at 298 K: \(k_\mathrm{air}=0.0262\) W/(m K), \(\nu=18\times10^{-6}\) m²/s.

mm = 1e-3
r_in = 8.0 * mm
l_pipe = 12.0 * mm
t_pipe = 3.0 * mm
t_wall = 4.0 * mm
h_flange = 10.0 * mm
w_flange = 4.0 * mm
n_flanges = 3
D = 2 * (r_in + t_wall + h_flange)

k_glass = 1.38
h_in, T_in = 15.0, 363.0
T_air = 298.0
k_air, nu_air, g = 0.0262, 18e-6, 9.81
theta_table = np.radians([0, 90, 100, 110, 120, 130, 140, 150, 160, 180])
f_table = np.array([0.48, 0.46, 0.45, 0.435, 0.42, 0.38, 0.35, 0.28, 0.22, 0.15])
grashof_per_kelvin = g * (1.0 / T_air) * D**3 / nu_air**2
print(f"outer diameter D = {D / mm:.0f} mm,  Gr = {grashof_per_kelvin:.3e} * (T - T_air)/K")


def incidence(y, z):
    """Angle from the lowest point of the horizontal cylinder (gravity along -y)."""
    return np.arctan2(np.abs(z), -y)


def air_prefactor(y, z):
    """C(theta) in q = C(theta) (T - T_air)^(5/4)."""
    return k_air / D * np.interp(incidence(y, z), theta_table, f_table) * grashof_per_kelvin**0.25


def air_flux(temperature, y, z):
    return air_prefactor(y, z) * np.maximum(temperature - T_air, 0.0) ** 1.25


def air_flux_slope(temperature, y, z):
    return 1.25 * air_prefactor(y, z) * np.maximum(temperature - T_air, 0.0) ** 0.25

Geometry and mesh

The flange is a solid of revolution. Its meridian section is covered by curved quadrilateral blocks: straight sides, Bezier fillets at the flange shoulders and the pipe transition, and circular arcs at the fin tips. merge_blocks joins them into one planar quadrilateral mesh, and revolve sweeps it by \(180^\circ\) about the pipe axis \(x\) into hexahedra. (Fillets that end tangent to a block side would give curved 27-node cells a vanishing Jacobian at that corner, so the cells are trilinear; the temperature is still approximated quadratically.) The block sides name the boundaries (bore, pipe end, flange end, outer surface), the two ends of the half revolution form the symmetry plane.

revolve takes the section in (radius, axial position) coordinates; the helper P writes a section point given as (x, r) in that order.

def P(x, r):
    return (r, x)


def section_blocks(level):
    """Curved blocks of the meridian section; neighbouring blocks share their sides."""
    L = level
    fin = Geometric(3 * L, growth=1.15 ** (-2 / L))
    y0, y1 = r_in, r_in + t_wall
    x1 = -2 * l_pipe / 3
    blocks = [
        Block(line(P(-l_pipe, y0), P(x1, y0)), line(P(x1, y0), P(x1, y0 + t_pipe)),
              line(P(-l_pipe, y0 + t_pipe), P(x1, y0 + t_pipe)), line(P(-l_pipe, y0), P(-l_pipe, y0 + t_pipe)),
              cells=(L, 2 * L), names=("bore", None, "outer", "pipe_end")),
        Block(line(P(x1, y0), P(0, y0)), line(P(0, y0), P(0, y1)),
              bezier(P(x1, y0 + t_pipe), P(-l_pipe / 3, y1), P(-l_pipe / 3, y1), P(0, y1)),
              line(P(x1, y0), P(x1, y0 + t_pipe)),
              cells=(2 * L, 2 * L), names=("bore", None, "outer", None)),
    ]
    w = w_flange
    for n in range(n_flanges):
        x0 = 2 * w * n
        shoulder, top = y1 + w / 2, y1 + h_flange - w / 2
        last = n == n_flanges - 1
        a, b, c, d = x0, x0 + w / 2, x0 + 3 * w / 2, x0 + 2 * w
        tip = lambda start, end: arc(P(x0 + w, top), w / 2, np.pi / 2 - start, np.pi / 2 - end)
        blocks += [
            Block(line(P(a, y0), P(b, y0)), line(P(b, y0), P(b, shoulder)),
                  bezier(P(a, y1), P(b, y1), P(b, shoulder)), line(P(a, y0), P(a, y1)),
                  cells=(L, 2 * L), names=("bore", None, "outer", None)),
            Block(line(P(b, y0), P(c, y0)), line(P(c, y0), P(c, shoulder)),
                  line(P(b, shoulder), P(c, shoulder)), line(P(b, y0), P(b, shoulder)),
                  cells=(2 * L, 2 * L), names=("bore", None, None, None)),
            Block(line(P(c, y0), P(d, y0)), line(P(d, y0), P(d, y1)),
                  bezier(P(c, shoulder), P(c, y1), P(d, y1)), line(P(c, y0), P(c, shoulder)),
                  cells=(L, 2 * L), names=("bore", "flange_end" if last else None, "outer", None)),
            Block(line(P(b, shoulder), P(c, shoulder)), line(P(c, shoulder), P(c, top)),
                  line(P(b, top), P(c, top)), line(P(b, shoulder), P(b, top)),
                  cells=(2 * L, 3 * L), grading=(None, fin), names=(None, "outer", None, "outer")),
            Block(line(P(b, top), P(c, top)), tip(0.0, np.pi / 4),
                  tip(3 * np.pi / 4, np.pi / 4), tip(np.pi, 3 * np.pi / 4),
                  cells=(2 * L, L), names=(None, "outer", "outer", "outer")),
        ]
    return blocks


def build_mesh(level):
    section = merge_blocks(section_blocks(level))
    return revolve(section, 4 * level, angle=np.pi, axis=(1.0, 0.0, 0.0), radial=(0.0, 1.0, 0.0),
                   names=("symmetry", "symmetry"))

Model

Glass is homogeneous and isotropic. The bore carries a ConvectiveHeatFlux, the end faces and the symmetry plane an explicit zero HeatFlux, and the whole outer surface the NonlinearHeatFlux \(q=C(\theta)\,(T-T_a)^{5/4}\) with its slope \(\partial q/\partial T=\tfrac54\,C(\theta)\,(T-T_a)^{1/4}\).

glass = IsotropicSolid(density=2203.0, specific_heat_capacity=703.0, thermal_conductivity=k_glass,
                       youngs_modulus=73.1e9, poisson_ratio=0.17, thermal_expansion_coefficient=5.5e-7)


def solve_level(mesh):
    geometry = mesh.geometry
    b = geometry.boundaries
    model = HeatConduction(domain=geometry.domain(), material=glass, heating=0.0)
    problem = Problem(name="Glass cooling flange", geometry=geometry, physics=(model,), boundary_conditions=(
        ConvectiveHeatFlux(boundary=b.bore, physics=model, heat_transfer_coefficient=h_in, ambient_temperature=T_in),
        NonlinearHeatFlux(boundary=b.outer, physics=model, flux=air_flux, derivative=air_flux_slope),
        HeatFlux(boundary=b.pipe_end | b.flange_end | b.symmetry, physics=model, value=0.0),
    ))
    numerics = Numerics(spatial=FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2)), linear_solver=SparseDirect(),
                        nonlinear_solver=Newton(), initial_guess={model.temperature: 330.0})
    field = Simulation(problem=problem, mesh=mesh, numerics=numerics).solve_stationary().field(model.temperature)
    nodal = np.asarray(field.evaluate(mesh.coordinates)).reshape(-1)
    return dict(field=field, b=b, Q_in=-2 * field.boundary_flux(b.bore), Q_out=2 * field.boundary_flux(b.outer),
                T_max=float(nodal.max()), T_min=float(nodal.min()))

Solve

Three meshes with cell counts in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4 in every direction.

levels = (2, 3, 4)
runs = []
for level in levels:
    start = time.time()
    mesh = build_mesh(level)
    out = solve_level(mesh)
    runs.append(out)
    print(f"level {level}: {mesh.cell_count:6d} hexahedra, Q = {out['Q_in']:.5f} W, "
          f"T in [{out['T_min']:.3f}, {out['T_max']:.3f}] K  ({time.time() - start:.0f} s)")
out = runs[-1]
field, b = out["field"], out["b"]

Comparison with the reference

Energy balance and mesh convergence. For three meshes with sizes \(h\propto 1/\ell\) convergence returns the observed order \(p\) and the Richardson estimate of each quantity.

print("Energy balance on the finest mesh (full flange)")
print(f"  heat entering through the bore   Q_in  = {out['Q_in']:.5f} W")
print(f"  heat leaving to the air          Q_out = {out['Q_out']:.5f} W")
print(f"  relative imbalance               {abs(out['Q_in'] - out['Q_out']) / out['Q_in']:.2e}")
print("Mesh study (h ~ 1/level)")
study = {}
for label, key in (("Q_in / W", "Q_in"), ("Q_out / W", "Q_out"), ("T_max / K", "T_max"), ("T_min / K", "T_min")):
    vals = [o[key] for o in runs]
    study[key] = estimate = convergence(vals, [1.0 / level for level in levels])
    order = f"{estimate.order:.2f}" if estimate.order is not None else "n/a"
    extrapolated = f"{estimate.extrapolated:.6f}" if estimate.extrapolated is not None else "n/a"
    print(f"  {label:10s} " + "  ".join(f"{v:.6f}" for v in vals)
          + f"   order {order}, extrapolated {extrapolated}, change last two meshes {abs(vals[-1] - vals[-2]):.1e}"
          + (f" ({100 * abs(vals[-1] / vals[-2] - 1):.3f} %)" if key.startswith("Q") else " K"))
Q_ext = study["Q_in"].extrapolated
if Q_ext is not None:
    print(f"  finest-mesh heat rate vs Richardson estimate: {100 * (out['Q_in'] / Q_ext - 1):+.3f} %")

1D radial wall estimate (sanity bound). On the insulated end face of the pipe the axial temperature gradient vanishes, so this is where the wall comes closest to pure radial conduction. For radial conduction the local bore flux \(q_i\) fixes the drop across the wall, \(\Delta T_\mathrm{1D}=q_i\,r_i\ln(r_o/r_i)/k\), and the outer flux must be \(q_o=q_i\,r_i/r_o\). The connecting pipe is only 12 mm long, shorter than the axial conduction length \(\sqrt{k\,t/(h_i+h_a)}\approx 15\) mm of the glass wall, so part of the heat entering the bore there still flows axially into the flanges: the 3D wall drop and outer flux must lie below the 1D values. The comparison is a plausibility bound, not the main reference.

r_out = r_in + t_pipe
print("1D radial estimate at the pipe end, x = -11.9 mm")
for theta_deg in (0.0, 90.0, 180.0):
    th = np.radians(theta_deg)
    direction = np.array([0.0, -np.cos(th), np.sin(th)])
    inner = np.array([-11.9 * mm, 0.0, 0.0]) + r_in * (1 + 1e-6) * direction
    outer = np.array([-11.9 * mm, 0.0, 0.0]) + r_out * (1 - 1e-6) * direction
    T_i, T_o = (float(np.asarray(field.evaluate([p]))[0]) for p in (inner, outer))
    q_i = h_in * (T_in - T_i)
    dT_1d = q_i * r_in * np.log(r_out / r_in) / k_glass
    q_o = float(air_flux(T_o, outer[1], outer[2]))
    print(f"  theta = {theta_deg:5.1f} deg: wall drop 3D {T_i - T_o:.4f} K vs 1D {dT_1d:.4f} K "
          f"({100 * ((T_i - T_o) / dT_1d - 1):+.1f} %);  outer flux 3D {q_o:.2f} vs 1D {q_i * r_in / r_out:.2f} W/m^2 "
          f"({100 * (q_o / (q_i * r_in / r_out) - 1):+.1f} %)")

limits = (out["T_min"], out["T_max"])

Going further

Sweep the bore radius between 6 and 10 mm at fixed flange dimensions: the bore-side film resistance dominates, so a wider bore raises the cooling power noticeably. Add a fourth flange, or replace glass by a metal, and compare how much of the temperature drop then sits in the wall versus the two films.
