Gravity-driven film on an inclined plane

A laminar liquid film running down an inclined plane, graded against the Nusselt film profile.

IncompressibleFree surfaceClosed-form check
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation solves the steady incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with a traction-free free surface and a gravity body force.

What it checks

The velocity profile, the surface speed g sin(theta) h^2/(2 nu) and the flow rate g sin(theta) h^3/(3 nu) match to solver precision.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A thin layer of a viscous liquid of thickness \(h\) runs down a plane inclined at the angle \(\theta\). The liquid sticks to the plane (no slip) while its free surface is in contact with air and carries no shear stress. Gravity supplies the streamwise body force \(\rho g \sin\theta\) per unit volume. Once the film is fully developed the velocity depends only on the wall-normal coordinate \(y\), and the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations reduce to

\[\mu\,\frac{d^2 u}{dy^2} + \rho g\sin\theta = 0,\qquad u(0)=0,\qquad \mu\,\frac{du}{dy}\Big|_{y=h}=0 .\]

OneSimulation solves the full 3D stationary Navier-Stokes system with Taylor-Hood finite elements (quadratic velocity, linear pressure) and a Newton solver. The free surface is modelled as a flat, traction-free boundary (zero Cauchy traction \(\sigma\cdot n = 0\)), the plane as a no-slip wall.

Reference. With the kinematic viscosity \(\nu=\mu/\rho\) the Nusselt solution is the half-parabola

\[u(y)=\frac{g\sin\theta}{2\nu}\,(2hy-y^2),\qquad u_s=\frac{g\sin\theta\,h^2}{2\nu},\qquad q=\int_0^h u\,dy=\frac{g\sin\theta\,h^3}{3\nu},\]

where \(u_s\) is the surface speed and \(q\) the flow rate per unit width. We check the computed profile, the surface speed and the flow rate.

Parameters

The film thickness lies along \(y\); the streamwise length (\(x\)) and spanwise width (\(z\)) only serve to form a short 3D slab of liquid. The flow is fully developed, so the solution is independent of \(x\) and \(z\). The liquid is a viscous oil with kinematic viscosity \(\nu = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}\); the density only scales the gravitational body force and the (vanishing) inertia.

length = 0.020        # m, streamwise extent (x)
h = 0.002             # m, film thickness (y), the only physically resolved direction
span = 0.005          # m, spanwise extent (z)
rho = 1.0             # kg/m^3, density (unit value: inertia vanishes in a developed film)
mu = 1.0e-3           # Pa·s, dynamic viscosity  ->  nu = mu/rho = 1e-3 m^2/s (viscous oil)
nu = mu / rho
g = 9.81              # m/s^2
theta = np.deg2rad(30.0)
g_parallel = g * np.sin(theta)        # m/s^2, gravity component along the plane
body_force = rho * g_parallel         # N/m^3, streamwise body force per unit volume

u_surface_reference = g_parallel * h**2 / (2.0 * nu)
flow_rate_reference = g_parallel * h**3 / (3.0 * nu)   # m^2/s (per unit width)
reynolds = flow_rate_reference / nu
print(f"g sin(theta) = {g_parallel:.3f} m/s^2,  surface speed = {1000*u_surface_reference:.3f} mm/s,  "
      f"flow rate q = {1e6*flow_rate_reference:.3f} mm^2/s,  Re = q/nu = {reynolds:.4f} (laminar film)")

Model

The film thickness \(h\) is the box width (the \(y\) extent). The plane (y_min) is a no-slip wall and the free surface (y_max) is traction-free; the upstream, downstream and spanwise faces carry the fully developed Nusselt trace, so each of the six boundary faces has an explicit condition. The traction-free face also fixes the pressure level (zero at the surface), hence no pressure gauge constraint is needed.

geometry = Box(length=length, width=h, height=span)
mesh = StructuredTetrahedralMesher(cells=(6, 8, 2)).generate(geometry)
print("Mesh:", mesh.cell_count, "tetrahedra")

fluid = NewtonianFluid(density=rho, dynamic_viscosity=mu)


def gravity(y):
    # Constant streamwise body force (per unit volume); y only fixes the array shape.
    return np.column_stack((np.full_like(y, body_force), np.zeros_like(y), np.zeros_like(y)))


def nusselt_profile(y):
    u = g_parallel / (2.0 * nu) * (2.0 * h * y - y**2)
    return np.column_stack((u, np.zeros_like(y), np.zeros_like(y)))


def traction_free(y):
    # Zero Cauchy traction on the free surface (no shear, zero gauge pressure).
    return np.zeros((len(y), 3))


model = IncompressibleNavierStokes(domain=geometry.volume, material=fluid, body_force=gravity)
developed = (geometry.boundaries.x_min | geometry.boundaries.x_max
             | geometry.boundaries.z_min | geometry.boundaries.z_max)
problem = Problem(
    name="Inclined plane film flow",
    geometry=geometry,
    physics=(model,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        NoSlipWall(boundary=geometry.boundaries.y_min, physics=model),
        FlowTraction(boundary=geometry.boundaries.y_max, physics=model, value=traction_free),
        FixedVelocity(boundary=developed, physics=model, value=nusselt_profile),
    ),
    initial_conditions=(InitialValue(model.velocity, (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)),),
)
simulation = Simulation(
    problem=problem,
    mesh=mesh,
    numerics=Numerics(
        spatial=FiniteElement(element=TaylorHood()),
        nonlinear_solver=Newton(
            linear_solver=SparseDirect(), absolute_tolerance=1e-11, relative_tolerance=0.0,
        ),
        initial_guess={model.velocity: (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)},
    ),
)
result = simulation.solve_stationary()

Comparison with the Nusselt profile

We sample the streamwise velocity along a wall-normal line at mid-slab and compare with the closed-form half-parabola. Taylor-Hood velocities are quadratic, so the exact profile is represented essentially to solver precision.

y_samples = np.linspace(0.0, h, 41)
line = np.column_stack((np.full_like(y_samples, length / 2),
                        y_samples,
                        np.full_like(y_samples, span / 2)))
velocity = np.asarray(result.field(model.velocity).evaluate(line))
u_numeric = velocity[:, 0]
u_exact = g_parallel / (2.0 * nu) * (2.0 * h * y_samples - y_samples**2)

max_abs_error = float(np.max(np.abs(u_numeric - u_exact)))
rel_error = max_abs_error / u_surface_reference
u_surface_numeric = float(u_numeric[-1])
print(f"Surface speed: FEM {1000*u_surface_numeric:.4f} mm/s,  reference {1000*u_surface_reference:.4f} mm/s")
print(f"Maximum absolute profile error: {max_abs_error:.3e} m/s  ({100*rel_error:.2e} % of u_s)")

# Flow rate per unit width from the sampled profile (Simpson on 41 points).
flow_rate_numeric = float(simpson(u_numeric, x=y_samples))
print(f"Flow rate per unit width: FEM {1e6*flow_rate_numeric:.4f} mm^2/s,  "
      f"reference {1e6*flow_rate_reference:.4f} mm^2/s")

# Surface shear stress: central difference on the quadratic profile near y = h.
shear_surface = mu * (u_numeric[-1] - u_numeric[-2]) / (y_samples[-1] - y_samples[-2])
shear_wall = mu * (u_numeric[1] - u_numeric[0]) / (y_samples[1] - y_samples[0])
shear_wall_reference = rho * g_parallel * h
print(f"Shear stress: surface {shear_surface:.3e} Pa (should vanish),  wall {shear_wall:.4e} Pa "
      f"(reference rho g sin(theta) h = {shear_wall_reference:.4e} Pa)")

# Gauge pressure is zero at the traction-free surface and uniform across the film.

Going further

Change the inclination angle or the film thickness: the surface speed scales with \(h^2\sin\theta\) and the flow rate with \(h^3\sin\theta\). Add a prescribed air shear stress on the free surface through the traction value (co-current or counter-current) and recover the superposition of the Nusselt parabola with a linear Couette profile. Increase the flow rate until the film Reynolds number approaches the long-wave instability threshold \(\mathrm{Re}_c=\tfrac{5}{6}\cot\theta\), where a flat developed film ceases to be the physically observed state.
