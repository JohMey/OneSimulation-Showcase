Waterflood with horizontal wells

Water injected through a horizontal well near the base of an oil reservoir pushes the oil up to a horizontal producer under the cap rock.

STEP importTwo-phase Darcy flowPeaceman wellsGravity

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. Runtime Alpha 0.2.1a7 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)Download the geometry (.step)Download the geometry script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation reads the reservoir block from a STEP file, meshes it with mapped hexahedra and solves 3D incompressible water-oil flow with Corey relative permeabilities and gravity; both wells are connected to the cells along their trajectory by Peaceman well indices, the injector under rate control, the producer at a fixed bottom-hole pressure. Two grids are compared. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

Water and oil volumes balance to about 1e-6 m³ over ten years (1e-12 of the pore volume), the oil rate equals the injection rate before breakthrough to six digits, breakthrough comes after about half a year on both grids, and the recovery after ten years is 25.4 % and 25.9 % of the pore volume on the fine and the coarse grid.

Geometry

The notebook starts at the import of the CAD file horizontal_wells_waterflood.step: STEP (AP242), readable by any CAD program, with the names of the faces that the boundary conditions use. Put it next to the notebook.

The geometry script

The script horizontal_wells_waterflood_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the file. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

OneSimulation and writes horizontal_wells_waterflood.step. The faces keep the names x_min, x_max,
y_min, y_max, z_min (base) and z_max (cap rock); all of them are sealed in the simulation.
"""

Box(length=600.0, width=300.0, height=30.0).save("horizontal_wells_waterflood.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Horizontal wells drain a thin oil reservoir along their whole length. In a waterflood a second horizontal well near the base injects water, which pushes the oil up towards the producer; the heavier water stays below the oil, so the displacement is stabilised by gravity. Until water reaches the producer the oil rate equals the injection rate; after breakthrough the well produces more and more water.

The model is incompressible immiscible two-phase flow in the 3D rock,

\[\phi\,\frac{\partial S_\alpha}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf u_\alpha=q_\alpha,\qquad \mathbf u_\alpha=-\frac{k\,k_{r\alpha}(S_w)}{\mu_\alpha}\big(\nabla p-\rho_\alpha\mathbf g\big),\qquad\alpha=w,o ,\]

with Corey relative permeabilities \(k_{rw}=S_w^2\), \(k_{ro}=(1-S_w)^2\). Each well is connected to the grid cells it passes through by the well index of Peaceman (Soc. Petrol. Eng. J. 23, 1983, 531-543), \(\mathrm{WI}=2\pi k\,\Delta x/\ln(r_e/r_w)\), with the equivalent radius \(r_e\) of the cell.

Setup. A reservoir 600 m × 300 m × 30 m, porosity 0.25, permeability 100 mD, water viscosity 0.5 mPa s and density 1000 kg/m³, oil viscosity 2 mPa s and density 850 kg/m³, initially full of oil. Both wells run 400 m along the middle of the reservoir, radius 0.1 m; the injector 4.5 m above the base injects 500 m³ of water per day, the producer 4.5 m below the cap rock is held at a bottom-hole pressure of 20 MPa. The reservoir comes from the CAD file horizontal_wells_waterflood.step, written by horizontal_wells_waterflood_geometry.py with the CAD objects of OneSimulation; all its faces are sealed.

Checks. For incompressible fluids the volumes must balance exactly: injected water equals produced fluid, and the oil produced equals the oil that left the pores; before breakthrough the oil rate equals the injection rate. The breakthrough time and the recovery are compared on two grids.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.meshing import CadHexahedralMesher
from onesim.physics.geotechnics import (
    ImmiscibleReservoir,
    ReservoirPerforation,
    ReservoirWell,
)
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns the reservoir block; it is meshed with mapped hexahedra, 30 m × 33 m × 5 m and, for the comparison, 50 m × 60 m × 7.5 m.

reservoir = load("horizontal_wells_waterflood.step")
length, width, thickness = reservoir.size
grids = {"fine": (20, 9, 6), "coarse": (12, 5, 4)}
meshes = {
    name: CadHexahedralMesher(cells=cells).generate(reservoir)
    for name, cells in grids.items()
}

view = plots.Camera(elevation=28, azimuth=-60)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(15, 5.5))
plots.geometry(
    reservoir,
    selection={
        "base": reservoir.boundaries.z_min,
        "cap rock": reservoir.boundaries.z_max,
    },
    labels=False,
    camera=view,
    title="Reservoir block",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes["fine"],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    camera=view,
    title=f"{meshes['fine'].cell_count} hexahedra",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Wells

A well perforates every cell whose centre lies on its trajectory, within half a cell; the Peaceman index of each perforation uses the cell size across the well.

day, year = 86400.0, 365.25 * 86400.0
rock = ImmiscibleReservoir(
    porosity=0.25,
    permeability=1e-13,
    water_viscosity=0.5e-3,
    oil_viscosity=2e-3,
    water_density=1000.0,
    oil_density=850.0,
)
wells = {}
for name, (nx, ny, nz) in grids.items():
    size = np.array([length / nx, width / ny, thickness / nz])
    centres = np.asarray(meshes[name].coordinates)[np.asarray(meshes[name].cells)].mean(
        axis=1
    )
    along = lambda height: np.flatnonzero(
        (np.abs(centres[:, 0] - length / 2) < 200.0)
        & (np.abs(centres[:, 1] - width / 2) < size[1] / 2)
        & (np.abs(centres[:, 2] - height) < size[2] / 2)
    )
    perforate = lambda cells: tuple(
        ReservoirPerforation.peaceman(
            cell=int(cell),
            transverse_lengths=(size[1], size[2]),
            transverse_permeabilities=(1e-13, 1e-13),
            completion_length=size[0],
            radius=0.1,
        )
        for cell in cells
    )
    wells[name] = (
        ReservoirWell(
            name="injector",
            perforations=perforate(along(4.5)),
            rate=500.0 / day,
            pressure_limit=40e6,
        ),
        ReservoirWell(
            name="producer",
            perforations=perforate(along(thickness - 4.5)),
            pressure=20e6,
        ),
    )

Model

Ten years with output every two months; gravity points down.

months = np.linspace(0.0, 10 * year, 61)
results = {
    name: rock.solve(
        mesh=meshes[name],
        water_saturation=0.0,
        times=months,
        max_step=10 * day,
        wells=wells[name],
        gravity=(0.0, 0.0, -9.81),
    )
    for name in grids
}

Results

Cumulative volumes from the well records (positive into the reservoir), the volume balance, the rates from the cumulative volumes, breakthrough (water cut above 1 %) and recovery.

print(
    f"{'grid':7s} {'cells':>6s} {'perforations':>13s} {'water balance [m³]':>19s} {'oil balance [m³]':>17s}"
    f" {'oil rate before bt / injection':>31s} {'breakthrough [years]':>21s} {'recovery after 10 years [%]':>28s}"
)
rates = {}
for name, result in results.items():
    pore_volume = result.pore_volume.sum()
    in_place = result.water_saturation @ result.pore_volume
    injected, produced_water, produced_oil = (
        result.well_phase_volume[:, 0, 0],
        -result.well_phase_volume[:, 1, 0],
        -result.well_phase_volume[:, 1, 1],
    )
    oil_rate, water_rate = np.diff(produced_oil) / np.diff(months), np.diff(
        produced_water
    ) / np.diff(months)
    cut = water_rate / (water_rate + oil_rate)
    rates[name] = (oil_rate, cut)
    breakthrough = months[1:][np.argmax(cut > 0.01)] / year
    print(
        f"{name:7s} {meshes[name].cell_count:6d} {len(wells[name][0].perforations):6d} + {len(wells[name][1].perforations):<4d}"
        f" {np.abs(injected - produced_water - in_place).max():19.2e} {np.abs(produced_oil - in_place).max():17.2e}"
        f" {oil_rate[0] * day / 500.0:31.6f} {breakthrough:21.2f} {100 * produced_oil[-1] / pore_volume:28.2f}"
    )

Figure

The water saturation in the vertical section through the wells after two and six years, and the oil rate and water cut at the producer on both grids.

fine = results["fine"]
section = np.abs(fine.cell_centers[:, 1] - width / 2) < width / 30
x, z = fine.cell_centers[section, 0], fine.cell_centers[section, 2]
order = np.lexsort((x, z))
shape = (grids["fine"][2], grids["fine"][0])
mid_months = (months[1:] + months[:-1]) / 2 / year
fig = plots.figure(2, 2, size=(16, 9))
for panel, years in ((fig[0], 2), (fig[1], 6)):
    plots.heatmap(
        np.unique(x),
        np.unique(z),
        fine.water_saturation[6 * years, section][order].reshape(shape).T,
        limits=(0, 1),
        aspect=5.0,
        label="water saturation",
        xlabel="x [m]",
        ylabel="z [m]",
        title=f"Section through the wells after {years} years",
        ax=panel,
    )
plots.line(
    y={
        f"{name} grid": (mid_months, oil_rate * day)
        for name, (oil_rate, cut) in rates.items()
    },
    hlines={"injection rate": 500.0},
    ylimits=(0, 550),
    xlabel="time [years]",
    ylabel="oil rate [m³/day]",
    title="Oil production",
    legend="lower left",
    ax=fig[2],
)
plots.line(
    y={
        f"{name} grid": (mid_months, 100 * cut)
        for name, (oil_rate, cut) in rates.items()
    },
    ylimits=(0, 100),
    xlabel="time [years]",
    ylabel="water cut [%]",
    title="Water cut at the producer",
    legend="upper left",
    ax=fig[3],
)
plots.show()

Going further

Lengthen the wells, place them side by side instead of one above the other, or lower the vertical permeability with a permeability tensor: the breakthrough moves and the recovery at breakthrough changes. Switch off gravity to see the water slump less favourably.
