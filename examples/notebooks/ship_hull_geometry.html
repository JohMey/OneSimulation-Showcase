Ship hull geometry lofted through its stations

The Wigley hull of ship hydrodynamics, lofted through eleven stations with exact parabolic sides and cut at the waterline.

LoftBezier profilesHydrostaticsClosed-form check

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. Runtime Alpha 0.2.1a7 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation builds every station as an exact profile of straight lines and quadratic Bezier sides, runs a smooth loft through all eleven, cuts the underwater body at the waterline and meshes it with tetrahedra from a cut grid of cubes; displacement, waterplane and centre of buoyancy come from the exact body.

What it checks

The displacement of 175.1998 l matches the closed-form integral of the hull equation (175.2000 l) to -1e-6, the waterplane area to -4.6e-5, the centre of buoyancy lies at exactly -3/8 T and on the midship plane, Simpson's rule over the exact station areas gives 175.2000 l, and the mesh of 172,353 tetrahedra holds the displacement within 1 %.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A hull is drawn as a set of cross-sections, the stations, along its length; the shell runs smoothly through them. Loft(sections=[(profile, position), ...]) does exactly that: with more than two sections the surface is a smooth interpolation through all of them.

The Wigley hull of length \(L\), beam \(B\) and draft \(T\) has the half-breadth

\[y(x,z) = \frac{B}{2}\Big(1-\big(\tfrac{2x}{L}\big)^2\Big)\Big(1-\big(\tfrac{z}{T}\big)^2\Big), \qquad -\tfrac{L}{2}\le x\le \tfrac{L}{2},\ -T\le z\le 0 .\]

At every station the side \(y(z)\) is a parabola, which is exactly a quadratic Bézier curve: from the waterline point \((b,0)\) with control point \((b,-T/2)\) to the keel point \((0,-T)\), where \(b\) is the half-breadth of the station. Above the waterline the sides rise vertically to the deck at a freeboard of \(F\). The stem and the stern are cut at \(|x| = 0.45L\), where the hull still has a tenth of its beam.

Reference. Integrating the hull equation between the end stations \(\pm x_e\),

\[\nabla = \frac{4}{3} B T \Big(x_e - \frac{4x_e^3}{3L^2}\Big),\qquad A_{WP} = 2B\Big(x_e - \frac{4x_e^3}{3L^2}\Big),\qquad z_B = -\frac{3}{8}T ,\]

displacement volume, waterplane area and height of the centre of buoyancy (W. C. S. Wigley, A comparison of experiment and calculated wave-profiles and wave-resistances for a form having parabolic waterlines, Proc. Royal Society A 144 (1934) 144-159).

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d2 import Path, PlanarRegion, Profile, Segment
from onesim.cad.d3 import Box, Loft
from onesim.meshing import OctreeMesher
from onesim.meshing.quality import quality
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Stations and loft

A model hull of 4 m length, 0.4 m beam, 0.25 m draft and 0.1 m freeboard, eleven stations from stern to stem. Each station is a closed profile in its own plane: deck line, side, keel, side.

hull_length, beam, draft, freeboard = 4.0, 0.4, 0.25, 0.1
end = 0.45 * hull_length
stations = np.linspace(-end, end, 11)
half_breadth = lambda x: beam / 2 * (1 - (2 * x / hull_length) ** 2)
sections = [
    (
        Profile(
            Path(
                (b, freeboard),
                (
                    Segment((b, 0.0)),
                    Segment((0.0, -draft), control=((b, -draft / 2),)),
                    Segment((-b, 0.0), control=((-b, -draft / 2),)),
                    Segment((-b, freeboard)),
                    Segment((b, freeboard)),
                ),
            )
        ),
        x,
    )
    for x, b in zip(stations, half_breadth(stations))
]
hull = Loft(sections=sections, axis=(1.0, 0.0, 0.0))
print(
    f"hull: {len(hull.faces)} faces, bounding box {np.round(hull.bounding_box[0], 4)} to {np.round(hull.bounding_box[1], 4)} m"
)

The underwater body

The part of the hull below the waterline \(z=0\) is the hull less a box above it. It is filled with tetrahedra cut from a grid of cubes of 30 mm at the shell, which grow to 60 mm inside; the grid follows a fine triangulation of the lofted shell (faceted()).

underwater = hull - Box(
    length=hull_length,
    width=beam,
    height=freeboard,
    origin=(-hull_length / 2, -beam / 2, 0.0),
)
mesh = OctreeMesher(target_size=0.03, coarsening=1).generate(underwater.faceted())

view = plots.Camera(elevation=35, azimuth=-50)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(17, 6))
plots.geometry(
    hull,
    selection={
        "shell": hull.boundaries.side,
        "transom and stem": hull.boundaries.bottom | hull.boundaries.top,
    },
    labels=False,
    camera=view,
    title="Wigley hull lofted through 11 stations",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    mesh,
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    camera=view,
    title=f"Underwater body: {mesh.cell_count} tetrahedra",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Results

Displacement of the CAD body and of its mesh, the waterplane area (the section of the hull at the waterline) and the centre of buoyancy against the closed-form integrals; the station areas are exact, so Simpson's rule over them is a second, discrete check of the loft.

integral = end - 4 * end**3 / (3 * hull_length**2)
displacement = 4 / 3 * beam * draft * integral
station_areas = (
    np.array([profile.area for profile, _ in sections])
    - 2 * half_breadth(stations) * freeboard
)
simpson = (
    (stations[1] - stations[0])
    / 3
    * (
        station_areas[0]
        + 4 * station_areas[1:-1:2].sum()
        + 2 * station_areas[2:-1:2].sum()
        + station_areas[-1]
    )
)
waterplane = hull.section((0, 0, 1), (0.0, 0.0, 0.0))
print(
    f"displacement: CAD {underwater.measure * 1e3:.4f} l, mesh {quality(mesh)['size'].sum() * 1e3:.4f} l, Simpson over the stations "
    f"{simpson * 1e3:.4f} l, closed form {displacement * 1e3:.4f} l ({underwater.measure / displacement - 1:+.2e})"
)
print(
    f"waterplane area: CAD {waterplane.measure:.6f} m2, closed form {2 * beam * integral:.6f} m2 ({waterplane.measure / (2 * beam * integral) - 1:+.2e})"
)
print(
    f"centre of buoyancy: x {underwater.center_of_mass[0]:+.5f} m (0 by symmetry), z {underwater.center_of_mass[2]:+.5f} m, "
    f"closed form {-3 / 8 * draft:+.5f} m"
)
print(
    f"block coefficient over the cut length: {underwater.measure / (2 * end * beam * draft):.4f}"
)

Figure

The waterplane with its two parabolic waterlines, the station areas along the hull against the closed form, and the midship station with its two parabolic sides.

x = np.linspace(-end, end, 101)
fig = plots.figure(1, 3, size=(20, 6))
plots.geometry(waterplane, title="Waterplane: section at z = 0", ax=fig[0])
plots.line(
    points={"station profiles": (stations, station_areas)},
    reference={"closed form 4/3 b(x) T": (x, 4 / 3 * half_breadth(x) * draft)},
    xlabel="x [m]",
    ylabel="underwater section area [m2]",
    title="Sectional area curve",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.geometry(
    PlanarRegion(profile=sections[5][0]),
    title="Midship station: deck line, sides, keel",
    ax=fig[2],
)
plots.show()

Going further

Mesh the water around the hull (Box(...) - underwater) for a flow model, thicken the shell with hull.surface(hull.boundaries.side).thickened(0.004) for a structural model, or change the stations to the lines of a real ship.
