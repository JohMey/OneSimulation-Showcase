Electric sensor: permittivity sweep over two objects in a box

Two charge probes on the top electrode of a capacitive sensor box image two dielectric objects below them; a batch of 3D solutions sweeps the permittivities of both and reads an unknown one back.

STEP importBatch sweepRegion-wise permittivityVariational bounds

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. The current pilot runs on macOS on Apple Silicon with CPython 3.13. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

What it computes

OneSimulation reads the air box, a bar and a disc from STEP files, meshes the box once with tetrahedra that embed both objects and solves 3D electrostatics with quadratic elements for every pair of permittivities of the batch; probe charges come from the field at the electrode, capacitances from the reaction of the potential. The script that builds the STEP files comes with the notebook.

What it checks

The empty box reproduces the parallel-plate charge and capacitance to 1e-11; every capacitance of the batch lies between the Thomson and Dirichlet bounds (at 0.32 to 0.42 of the interval); each probe sees its own object (+14.75 pC/m² for the bar from eps_r 1 to 8) and the other one only by 0.05 %; the permittivity 3 of an unknown bar is read back from the probe as 3.11.

Geometry

The notebook starts at the import of the CAD files electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging.step, electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_bar.step, electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_disc.step: STEP (AP242), readable by any CAD program, with the names of the faces that the boundary conditions use. Put them next to the notebook.

The geometry script

The script electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Electric sensor: permittivity sweep over two objects in a box".

Builds the air box of the sensor, 5 m x 3 m and 1 m high, with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and
writes electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging.step with the face groups top (the driven electrode,
z = 1 m), bottom (the grounded electrode, z = 0) and walls. The two objects standing on the bottom
electrode go to one file each: a rectangular bar (1.0 m x 0.6 m, 0.8 m high, centred at x = 1.5 m,
y = 1.5 m) to electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_bar.step and a disc (radius 0.4 m, 0.5 m high,
centred at x = 3.5 m, y = 1.5 m) to electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_disc.step.
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import Box, Cylinder

box = Box(length=5.0, width=3.0, height=1.0)
faces = box.boundaries
box.grouped(
    top=faces.z_max,
    bottom=faces.z_min,
    walls=faces.x_min | faces.x_max | faces.y_min | faces.y_max,
).save("electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging.step")
Box(length=1.0, width=0.6, height=0.8, origin=(1.0, 1.2, 0.0)).save(
    "electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_bar.step"
)
Cylinder(radius=0.4, length=0.5, origin=(3.5, 1.5, 0.0)).save(
    "electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_disc.step"
)

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A capacitive sensor tells materials apart by their permittivity. The box is 5 m long, 3 m wide and \(h=1\) m high, with an electrode on its top held at \(V_0=1\) V and a grounded electrode on its bottom; the side walls are insulating. On the bottom electrode stand a rectangular bar of \(1.0\times0.6\) m, 0.8 m high, centred at \(x=y=1.5\) m, and a disc of radius 0.4 m, 0.5 m high, centred at \(x=3.5\) m, \(y=1.5\) m. The potential follows from \(\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\nabla V)=0\), with \(V\) and \(D_n\) continuous on the surfaces of the objects. Two probes on the top electrode, at \((1.5, 1.5, 1.0)\) m above the bar and at \((3.5, 1.5, 1.0)\) m above the disc, read the surface charge \(\sigma=-\varepsilon_0E_z\).

The batch sweeps the relative permittivity of the bar over 1, 2, 4 and 8 with the disc at 1 and at 3. One more solution, with the bar at \(\varepsilon_r=3\), plays an unknown object whose permittivity is read back from the bar probe.

The air box comes from the CAD file electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging.step with the face groups top, bottom and walls, the objects from electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_bar.step and ..._disc.step. The script electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_geometry.py builds them with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.

| face | condition | |---|---| | top | \(V=V_0\) | | bottom | \(V=0\) | | walls | \(\mathbf D\cdot\mathbf n=0\) |

Reference. With both objects at \(\varepsilon_r=1\) the box is an ideal parallel-plate capacitor, \(\sigma=\varepsilon_0V_0/h\) and \(C=\varepsilon_0A/h\) exactly. For the other cases the two variational principles of electrostatics bound the capacitance from both sides (J. D. Jackson, Classical Electrodynamics, 3rd ed., Sec. 1.12 and the capacitance bounds in the problems of Chapter 1): Dirichlet's principle with the uniform field as trial potential gives \(C_\mathrm{up}=\varepsilon_0\big(Ah+\sum_i(\varepsilon_{r,i}-1)V_i\big)/h^2\), Thomson's principle with a vertical displacement that is constant in each column gives \(C_\mathrm{low}=\varepsilon_0\int_A\mathrm dA/(h-t+t/\varepsilon_r)\), with the object height \(t\) in the column. The same series column, \(\sigma_\mathrm{col}=\varepsilon_0V_0/(h-t+t/\varepsilon_r)\), is the charge a probe would read above a laterally infinite layer; above an object of finite size the fringing field lowers it.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d2 import Plane
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import VacuumPermittivity
from onesim.meshing import LocalRefinement, TetrahedralMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.physics.electrostatics import (
    FixedPotential,
    PoissonEquation,
    ZeroNormalElectricDisplacement,
)
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation, regionwise
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns the box with its named faces and the two objects; the tetrahedral mesher embeds the objects so that their surfaces run along faces of the mesh. Cells of 0.15 m in the air, 0.1 m in the objects. All cases of the batch share this mesh.

box = load("electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging.step")
faces = box.boundaries
bar, disc = (
    load(f"electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_{name}.step")
    for name in ("bar", "disc")
)
length, width, height = box.size
mesh = TetrahedralMesher(
    target_size=0.15,
    embedded_regions=(bar, disc),
    refinements=(
        LocalRefinement(region=bar, target_size=0.1),
        LocalRefinement(region=disc, target_size=0.1),
    ),
).generate(box)

view = plots.Camera(elevation=30, azimuth=-60)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(15, 6))
plots.geometry(
    [bar.named("bar"), disc.named("disc")],
    camera=view,
    title="The two objects on the bottom electrode",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    mesh,
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    camera=view,
    title=f"Air box 5 m x 3 m x 1 m: {mesh.cell_count} tetrahedra",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Batch of solutions

regionwise gives the objects their permittivity, with point tests from the CAD bounds of the bar and the disc. One problem per pair of permittivities, all on the same mesh with quadratic elements.

voltage, eps0 = 1.0, float(VacuumPermittivity().evaluate())
(bx0, by0, _), (bx1, by1, bar_height) = bar.bounding_box
(dx0, dy0, _), (dx1, dy1, disc_height) = disc.bounding_box
radius, centre = (dx1 - dx0) / 2, ((dx0 + dx1) / 2, (dy0 + dy1) / 2)
in_bar = (
    lambda p: (p[:, 0] >= bx0)
    & (p[:, 0] <= bx1)
    & (p[:, 1] >= by0)
    & (p[:, 1] <= by1)
    & (p[:, 2] <= bar_height)
)
in_disc = lambda p: (np.hypot(p[:, 0] - centre[0], p[:, 1] - centre[1]) <= radius) & (
    p[:, 2] <= disc_height
)
cases = [
    (eps_bar, eps_disc) for eps_disc in (1.0, 3.0) for eps_bar in (1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 8.0)
] + [(3.0, 1.0)]
models = {
    case: PoissonEquation(
        domain=box.volume,
        charge_density=0.0,
        permittivity=regionwise(
            ((in_bar, case[0] * eps0), (in_disc, case[1] * eps0)),
            default=eps0,
            unit="F/m",
        ),
    )
    for case in cases
}
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2)), linear_solver=SparseDirect()
)
results = {
    case: Simulation(
        problem=Problem(
            name="Electric sensor",
            geometry=box,
            physics=(model,),
            boundary_conditions=(
                FixedPotential(boundary=faces.top, physics=model, value=voltage),
                FixedPotential(boundary=faces.bottom, physics=model, value=0.0),
                ZeroNormalElectricDisplacement(boundary=faces.walls, physics=model),
            ),
        ),
        mesh=mesh,
        numerics=numerics,
    ).solve_stationary()
    for case, model in models.items()
}

Results

For every case: the two probe charges, the capacitance and its place between the bounds. Then the sensitivity of each probe to its own object and to the other one, and the permittivity of the "unknown" bar read back from the bar probe by interpolating the sweep with the disc at 1.

probes = np.array([[1.5, 1.5, height - 1e-6], [centre[0], centre[1], height - 1e-6]])
sigma = {
    case: -eps0 * result.field(models[case].electric_field).evaluate(probes)[:, 2]
    for case, result in results.items()
}
capacitance = {
    case: result.field(models[case].potential).reaction(faces.top) / voltage
    for case, result in results.items()
}
area, footprints = length * width, ((bx1 - bx0) * (by1 - by0), np.pi * radius**2)
column = lambda eps, t: eps0 * voltage / (height - t + t / eps)
bounds = {
    case: (
        sum(
            f * column(e, t)
            for f, e, t in zip(footprints, case, (bar_height, disc_height))
        )
        / voltage
        + eps0 * (area - sum(footprints)) / height,
        eps0
        / height**2
        * (
            area * height
            + (case[0] - 1) * footprints[0] * bar_height
            + (case[1] - 1) * footprints[1] * disc_height
        ),
    )
    for case in cases
}
print(
    f"parallel plates: σ = {1e12 * eps0 * voltage / height:.4f} pC/m^2, C = {1e12 * eps0 * area / height:.4f} pF"
)
print(
    "eps_bar eps_disc   σ bar probe   series col.   σ disc probe   C [pF]   C_low [pF]   C_up [pF]   between the bounds"
)
for case in cases[:-1]:
    low, up = bounds[case]
    place = (
        "exact" if up - low < 1e-18 else f"{(capacitance[case] - low) / (up - low):.3f}"
    )
    print(
        f"{case[0]:6.0f} {case[1]:8.0f} {1e12 * sigma[case][0]:13.4f} {1e12 * column(case[0], bar_height):13.4f}"
        f" {1e12 * sigma[case][1]:14.4f} {1e12 * capacitance[case]:8.4f} {1e12 * low:12.4f} {1e12 * up:11.4f} {place:>20s}"
    )
print(
    f"empty box (both eps_r = 1): σ deviates by {100 * np.max(np.abs(sigma[1.0, 1.0] / (eps0 * voltage / height) - 1)):.1e} %, "
    f"C by {100 * (capacitance[1.0, 1.0] / (eps0 * area / height) - 1):+.1e} %"
)
own = sigma[8.0, 1.0][0] - sigma[1.0, 1.0][0]
cross = sigma[1.0, 3.0][0] - sigma[1.0, 1.0][0]
print(
    f"bar probe: +{1e12 * own:.4f} pC/m^2 from eps_bar 1 -> 8, {1e12 * cross:+.4f} pC/m^2 from eps_disc 1 -> 3 "
    f"(crosstalk {100 * cross / own:.2f} % of the own signal)"
)
print(
    f"disc probe: {1e12 * (sigma[1.0, 3.0][1] - sigma[1.0, 1.0][1]):+.4f} pC/m^2 from eps_disc 1 -> 3, "
    f"{1e12 * (sigma[8.0, 1.0][1] - sigma[1.0, 1.0][1]):+.4f} pC/m^2 from eps_bar 1 -> 8"
)
sweep = [(1.0, 1.0), (2.0, 1.0), (4.0, 1.0), (8.0, 1.0)]
read_back = np.interp(
    sigma[3.0, 1.0][0], [sigma[case][0] for case in sweep], [case[0] for case in sweep]
)
print(
    f"unknown bar: probe reads {1e12 * sigma[3.0, 1.0][0]:.4f} pC/m^2 -> eps_r = {read_back:.2f} read back from the sweep (true 3)"
)

Figure

The surface charge on the top electrode for the bar at \(\varepsilon_r=8\) and the disc at 3, the field strength in the section through both objects, the surface charge along the middle line \(y=1.5\) m for the bar sweep, and both probe signals over the permittivity of the bar against the series-column value.

field = results[8.0, 3.0].field(models[8.0, 3.0].electric_field)
x = np.linspace(0.01, length - 0.01, 400)
line = np.column_stack((x, np.full_like(x, 1.5), np.full_like(x, height - 1e-6)))
eps_axis = np.linspace(1.0, 8.0, 71)
fig = plots.figure(2, 2, size=(15, 11))
plots.surface(
    field,
    boundary=faces.top,
    component=lambda E, position: -1e12 * eps0 * E[:, 2],
    limits=(8.0, 22.0),
    contours=15,
    subdivisions=2,
    label="σ [pC/m²]",
    title="Surface charge on the top electrode (bar 8, disc 3)",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.section(
    field,
    plane=Plane(origin=(0.0, 1.5, 0.0), normal=(0.0, 1.0, 0.0)),
    component=lambda E, position: np.linalg.norm(E, axis=1),
    limits=(0.0, 3.0),
    contours=13,
    subdivisions=2,
    label="|E| [V/m]",
    xlabel="x [m]",
    ylabel="z [m]",
    title="Field strength in the section y = 1.5 m (bar 8, disc 3)",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.line(
    x,
    {
        f"bar eps_r = {case[0]:.0f}": -1e12
        * eps0
        * results[case].field(models[case].electric_field).evaluate(line)[:, 2]
        for case in sweep
    },
    xlabel="x [m]",
    ylabel="σ on the top electrode [pC/m²]",
    title="Bar sweep along y = 1.5 m (disc at 1)",
    legend="upper right",
    ax=fig[2],
)
plots.line(
    points={
        "bar probe, disc at 1": (
            [c[0] for c in sweep],
            [1e12 * sigma[c][0] for c in sweep],
        ),
        "bar probe, disc at 3": (
            [c[0] for c in cases[4:8]],
            [1e12 * sigma[c][0] for c in cases[4:8]],
        ),
        "disc probe, disc at 3": (
            [c[0] for c in cases[4:8]],
            [1e12 * sigma[c][1] for c in cases[4:8]],
        ),
    },
    reference={
        "series column above the bar": (eps_axis, 1e12 * column(eps_axis, bar_height))
    },
    xlabel="relative permittivity of the bar",
    ylabel="probe charge [pC/m²]",
    title="Probe signals of the batch",
    legend="upper left",
    ax=fig[3],
)
plots.show()

Going further

Add the height of the bar as a second sweep parameter by writing one STEP file per height in electric_sensor_permittivity_imaging_geometry.py: tall objects of low permittivity and low objects of high permittivity can give the same probe charge, and a second probe position is needed to tell them apart.
