Shallow-water equations: dam breaks and a lake at rest

Two dam breaks, onto a wet and onto a dry bed, and still water around an island, computed with the one-dimensional shallow-water equations.

Shallow waterFinite volumesMUSCLWell-balanced

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. Runtime Alpha 0.2.1a7 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation solves the Saint-Venant equations with a finite-volume scheme (Rusanov flux, hydrostatic reconstruction over the bed, SSP Runge-Kutta with CFL-limited steps), first order and with MUSCL reconstruction, minmod limiter and a positivity limiter for the dry front, on three grids.

What it checks

Against the exact solutions of Stoker (wet bed) and Ritter (dry bed) the mean depth error falls from 7.0e-3 m to 1.7e-3 m with MUSCL from 100 to 400 cells (observed order 1.0 at the discontinuities); the water volume is conserved to 3e-14 m³, and the lake at rest around a dry island changes its depth by at most 2.7e-4 m in 2 s.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Where water is shallow compared with the length of its waves, the pressure is hydrostatic and the velocity nearly uniform over the depth. Mass and momentum balances over the water column give the shallow-water (Saint-Venant) equations for the depth \(h\) and the discharge \(q=hu\) over a bed of elevation \(b(x)\):

\[\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial q}{\partial x}=0,\qquad \frac{\partial q}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big(\frac{q^2}{h}+\frac{g h^2}{2}\Big)=-g\,h\,\frac{\partial b}{\partial x}.\]

They are used for rivers, floods, tides and tsunamis. Two classic problems test a solver:

  • Dam break. A channel of length 10 m holds water 1 m deep behind a gate at \(x_0=5\) m and water 0.1 m deep (or none) in front of it. When the gate is removed, a rarefaction runs back into the reservoir and a bore (or, on a dry bed, a thin front) runs downstream.
  • Lake at rest. Still water over an uneven bed, with an island that rises above the surface, must stay exactly still. A scheme that does not balance the pressure gradient and the bed slope discretely produces spurious currents.
endcondition
\(x=0\) and \(x=10\) mreflecting wall, \(q=0\) (the waves do not reach them)

Reference. J. J. Stoker, Water Waves, Interscience 1957, ch. 10 (dam break on a wet bed: rarefaction, constant state \(h_m\), bore); A. Ritter, Z. Vereines Deutscher Ingenieure 36, 947 (1892) (dry bed: \(h=(2c_0-\xi)^2/(9g)\) with \(\xi=(x-x_0)/t\), \(c_0=\sqrt{g h_0}\)); E. Audusse, F. Bouchut, M.-O. Bristeau, R. Klein, B. Perthame, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 25, 2050-2065 (2004) for the hydrostatic reconstruction that keeps the lake at rest.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d1 import Line
from onesim.meshing import StructuredLineMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fvm import (
    MUSCL,
    ConvexInvariantDomain,
    FiniteVolume,
    HydrostaticReconstruction,
    MinmodLimiter,
    Rusanov,
)
from onesim.numerics.time import TimeMethod, TimeStepLimits
from onesim.physics.hydrodynamics import TopographicShallowWater
from onesim.simulation import Problem, ReflectingWall, Simulation
from onesim.simulation.initial_conditions import InitialValue
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme
from scipy.optimize import brentq

theme.set()

Exact dam-break solution

On a wet bed of depth \(h_1\) the middle depth \(h_m\) joins the rarefaction from the reservoir to the bore: \(2(c_0-c_m)=(h_m-h_1)\sqrt{g(h_m+h_1)/(2h_mh_1)}\). On a dry bed \(h_m=0\) and the solution is Ritter's.

g, gate, deep, end = 9.81, 5.0, 1.0, 0.6


def dam_break(x, t, shallow):
    """Exact depth of the dam break at time t (Stoker; Ritter for a dry bed)."""
    c0 = np.sqrt(g * deep)
    middle = (
        brentq(
            lambda h: 2 * (c0 - np.sqrt(g * h))
            - (h - shallow) * np.sqrt(g * (h + shallow) / (2 * h * shallow)),
            shallow,
            deep,
        )
        if shallow > 0
        else 0.0
    )
    speed = 2 * (c0 - np.sqrt(g * middle))
    front = middle * speed / (middle - shallow) if shallow > 0 else speed
    xi = (x - gate) / t
    return np.select(
        [xi < -c0, xi < speed - np.sqrt(g * middle), xi < front],
        [deep, (2 * c0 - xi) ** 2 / (9 * g), middle],
        shallow,
    )

Model

One problem for each downstream depth, solved with two finite-volume schemes on four grids. Both use the Rusanov flux, the hydrostatic reconstruction for the bed and a two-stage strong-stability-preserving Runge-Kutta method with a CFL-limited time step; the second scheme adds MUSCL reconstruction with the minmod limiter and a positivity limiter for the dry front.

channel = Line(start=(0, 0, 0), end=(10, 0, 0))
model = TopographicShallowWater(domain=channel.line, bed_elevation=0.0)
released = {
    0.1: lambda x: np.where(x < gate, deep, 0.1),
    0.0: lambda x: np.where(x < gate, deep, 0.0),
}
problems = {
    shallow: Problem(
        name="Dam break",
        geometry=channel,
        physics=(model,),
        boundary_conditions=(
            ReflectingWall(physics=model, boundary=channel.boundaries.all),
        ),
        initial_conditions=(
            InitialValue(model.depth, initial),
            InitialValue(model.discharge, 0.0),
        ),
    )
    for shallow, initial in released.items()
}
schemes = {
    "first order": FiniteVolume(
        convection=Rusanov(),
        provider=HydrostaticReconstruction(),
        positivity=ConvexInvariantDomain(),
        cell_quadrature_order=4,
    ),
    "MUSCL, minmod": FiniteVolume(
        convection=Rusanov(),
        provider=HydrostaticReconstruction(),
        reconstruction=MUSCL(),
        limiter=MinmodLimiter(),
        positivity=ConvexInvariantDomain(),
        cell_quadrature_order=4,
    ),
}
grids = (100, 200, 400)
depths = {
    (shallow, scheme, cells): Simulation(
        problem=problems[shallow],
        mesh=StructuredLineMesher(cells=cells).generate(channel),
        numerics=Numerics(
            spatial=spatial,
            time_integrator=TimeMethod("ssprk2"),
            step_limits=TimeStepLimits(stability_safety_factor=0.8),
        ),
    )
    .run(start_time=0.0, end_time=end, time_step=0.05)
    .field(model.depth)
    for shallow in problems
    for scheme, spatial in schemes.items()
    for cells in grids
}

Results

The error is the mean absolute depth error over the channel at \(t=0.6\) s; the water volume must stay \(5.5\) m³ per metre width (wet bed) and \(5.0\) m³ (dry bed).

x = np.linspace(0.005, 9.995, 2000)
points = np.column_stack((x, 0 * x, 0 * x))
errors = {
    key: np.mean(np.abs(np.ravel(depth.evaluate(points)) - dam_break(x, end, key[0])))
    for key, depth in depths.items()
}
for shallow, label in (
    (0.1, "wet bed, h1 = 0.1 m (Stoker)"),
    (0.0, "dry bed (Ritter)"),
):
    print(label)
    print(
        "  cells    first order    MUSCL      observed order (MUSCL)   volume change (MUSCL)"
    )
    for index, cells in enumerate(grids):
        second = errors[(shallow, "MUSCL, minmod", cells)]
        order = (
            np.log2(errors[(shallow, "MUSCL, minmod", grids[index - 1])] / second)
            if index
            else np.nan
        )
        volume = depths[(shallow, "MUSCL, minmod", cells)].integrate().value - 5 * (
            deep + shallow
        )
        print(
            f"  {cells:5d} {errors[(shallow, 'first order', cells)]:13.2e} {second:10.2e} {order:16.2f} {volume:22.1e} m³"
        )

Lake at rest around an island

A Gaussian hill 0.8 m high under water whose surface lies at 0.6 m: the hilltop is dry. The run lasts two seconds; the exact solution is the initial state, so depth and discharge must not change.

hill = lambda x: 0.8 * np.exp(-(((x - 5.0) / 1.2) ** 2))
lake = TopographicShallowWater(domain=channel.line, bed_elevation=hill)
still = Simulation(
    problem=Problem(
        name="Lake at rest",
        geometry=channel,
        physics=(lake,),
        boundary_conditions=(
            ReflectingWall(physics=lake, boundary=channel.boundaries.all),
        ),
        initial_conditions=(
            InitialValue(lake.depth, lambda x: np.maximum(0.6 - hill(x), 0.0)),
            InitialValue(lake.discharge, 0.0),
        ),
    ),
    mesh=StructuredLineMesher(cells=200).generate(channel),
    numerics=Numerics(
        spatial=schemes["MUSCL, minmod"],
        time_integrator=TimeMethod("ssprk2"),
        step_limits=TimeStepLimits(stability_safety_factor=0.8),
    ),
).run(start_time=0.0, end_time=2.0, time_step=0.1)
lake_depth = still.field(lake.depth)
level = np.ravel(still.field(lake.free_surface).evaluate(points))
wet = np.ravel(lake_depth.evaluate(points)) > 0
print(
    f"\nlake at rest after 2 s: largest change of the depth {np.abs(lake_depth.values - lake_depth.at(0.0).values).max():.1e} m, "
    f"largest discharge {np.abs(still.field(lake.discharge).values).max():.1e} m²/s, "
    f"dry cells {np.count_nonzero(lake_depth.values == 0)} (island)"
)

Figure

Depth profiles of both dam breaks at \(t=0.6\) s on the 200-cell grid against the exact solutions, the grid study, and the lake at rest.

fig = plots.figure(2, 2, size=(15, 10))
for panel, shallow, title in (
    (fig[0], 0.1, "Wet bed: rarefaction and bore"),
    (fig[1], 0.0, "Dry bed: rarefaction and front"),
):
    plots.line(
        x,
        {
            scheme: np.ravel(depths[(shallow, scheme, 200)].evaluate(points))
            for scheme in schemes
        },
        reference={
            "exact (Stoker)" if shallow else "exact (Ritter)": dam_break(
                x, end, shallow
            )
        },
        xlabel="x [m]",
        ylabel="depth h [m]",
        ylimits=(-0.02, 1.05),
        title=f"{title}, t = {end} s, 200 cells",
        ax=panel,
    )
plots.convergence(
    [10 / cells for cells in grids],
    {
        f"{scheme}, {'wet' if shallow else 'dry'} bed": [
            errors[(shallow, scheme, cells)] for cells in grids
        ]
        for shallow in problems
        for scheme in schemes
    },
    order=1,
    xlabel="cell width [m]",
    ylabel="mean depth error [m]",
    title="Grid study",
    ax=fig[2],
)
plots.line(
    x,
    {"water surface after 2 s": np.where(wet, level, np.nan), "bed": hill(x)},
    hlines={"initial surface": 0.6},
    xlabel="x [m]",
    ylabel="elevation [m]",
    ylimits=(0.0, 1.0),
    title="Lake at rest around an island",
    legend="upper right",
    ax=fig[3],
)
plots.show()
