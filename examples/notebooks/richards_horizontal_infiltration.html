Horizontal infiltration into unsaturated soil

Water drawn into a dry loam column from a wetted end, graded against the exact Boltzmann similarity solution of the nonlinear Richards equation.

Richards equationvan GenuchtenSimilarity solution
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation solves the mixed-form Richards equation with van Genuchten-Mualem closures by conservative finite volumes; the notebook solves the similarity ODE independently by shooting and compares profiles, their collapse and the cumulative infiltration.

What it checks

Profile errors stay below 1.5 % of the moisture contrast, all profiles collapse in the similarity variable within 1 %, the infiltration follows S√t within 1 % and mass is conserved to machine precision.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A long horizontal column of soil is initially at a uniform (fairly dry) water content \(\theta_i\). From \(t=0\) on, the inlet face \(x=0\) is held close to saturation, so water is drawn into the column by capillarity alone; gravity plays no role in a horizontal column. The far end stays dry during the whole experiment.

OneSimulation solves the mixed form of the Richards equation with a conservative finite-volume scheme,

\[\frac{\partial\theta(h)}{\partial t} =\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big(K(h)\,\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}\Big),\qquad h(x,0)=h_i,\quad h(0,t)=h_0,\quad q(L,t)=0,\]

with the van Genuchten-Mualem closure for the water content \(\theta(h)\) and the hydraulic conductivity \(K(h)\). Written in terms of \(\theta\) this is a nonlinear diffusion equation with the soil-water diffusivity \(D(\theta)=K/C\), \(C=d\theta/dh\).

Reference. Without gravity and without a length scale the profile depends on Boltzmann's similarity variable \(\varphi=x/\sqrt{t}\) only, \(\theta(x,t)=\Theta(\varphi)\), where \(\Theta\) solves

\[\frac{d}{d\varphi}\Big(D(\Theta)\frac{d\Theta}{d\varphi}\Big) +\frac{\varphi}{2}\frac{d\Theta}{d\varphi}=0,\qquad \Theta(0)=\theta_0,\quad\Theta(\infty)=\theta_i,\]

and the cumulative infiltration grows as the square root of time,

\[I(t)=S\sqrt{t},\qquad S=-2\,D(\theta_0)\,\Theta'(0),\]

with \(S\) the sorptivity of the soil. \(D\) varies by a factor of forty across the wetting front, so \(\Theta(\varphi)\) has no closed form; it is obtained as a boundary-value problem of this ordinary differential equation, independently of the finite-volume solver.

import numpy as np
from onesim.meshing import TensorCollocationMesh
from onesim.numerics.time import ForwardEulerTimeIntegrator
from onesim.physics.hydrodynamics import Richards1DProvider
from onesim.physics.hydrodynamics.porous.numerics.equations.multiphysics.richards import (
    RichardsColumnFiniteVolume,
)
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme
from scipy.integrate import solve_bvp

theme.set()

Soil column

A loam-type soil in a column of 0.5 m, 400 cells. The inlet is held at a small suction of 0.1 m (95 % of saturation), the far end is a no-flow face and gravity is switched off. The solver advances the stored water with explicit steps of 0.02 s and the harmonic mean of the cell conductivities.

length, cells, inlet_head, initial_head = 0.5, 400, -0.10, -1.00
x = (np.arange(cells) + 0.5) * length / cells
column = TensorCollocationMesh(
    axes=(x,), coordinate_system="cartesian", periodic=(False,)
)
soil = Richards1DProvider(
    mesh=column,
    spatial=RichardsColumnFiniteVolume(),
    time_integrator=ForwardEulerTimeIntegrator(),
    residual_water_content=0.08,
    saturated_water_content=0.43,
    van_genuchten_alpha=3.6,
    van_genuchten_n=1.56,
    saturated_hydraulic_conductivity=1.0e-5,
    gravity=0.0,
    initial_pressure_head=initial_head,
    bottom_pressure_head=inlet_head,
    top_pressure_head=None,
    final_time=60.0,
    time_step=0.02,
)
inlet, initial = float(soil.water_content(inlet_head)), float(
    soil.water_content(initial_head)
)
print(f"water content: {inlet:.4f} at the inlet, {initial:.4f} initially")

Simulation

One hour in segments of one minute, each started from the pressure head of the one before; the water that entered through the inlet in each segment adds up to the cumulative infiltration.

times, infiltration, head, profiles, residual = [0.0], [0.0], None, {}, 0.0
for minute in range(1, 61):
    segment = soil.run(head)
    head, residual = segment.pressure_head, max(residual, segment.mass_balance_residual)
    times.append(60.0 * minute)
    infiltration.append(infiltration[-1] + segment.boundary_exchange)
    if minute in (10, 20, 40, 60):
        profiles[60.0 * minute] = segment.water_content
times, infiltration = np.array(times), np.array(infiltration)
print(
    f"cumulative infiltration after one hour: {1e3 * infiltration[-1]:.2f} mm, largest mass-balance residual {residual:.1e} m"
)

Similarity solution

With \(q=D(\Theta)\,\Theta'\) the similarity equation is the first-order system \(\Theta'=q/D\), \(q'=-\tfrac{\varphi}{2}\,q/D\) between \(\Theta(0)=\theta_0\) and \(\Theta=\theta_i\) far ahead of the front. The diffusivity comes from the conductivity and the water capacity of the same soil law.

far = 6.5e-3
diffusivity = lambda theta: (
    soil.hydraulic_conductivity(soil.pressure_head(np.clip(theta, initial, inlet)))
    / soil.water_capacity(soil.pressure_head(np.clip(theta, initial, inlet)))
)
phi = np.linspace(0.0, far, 600)
similarity = solve_bvp(
    lambda p, y: np.vstack(
        (y[1] / diffusivity(y[0]), -0.5 * p * y[1] / diffusivity(y[0]))
    ),
    lambda left, right: np.array([left[0] - inlet, right[0] - initial]),
    phi,
    np.vstack(
        (
            initial + (inlet - initial) * np.exp(-((phi / 2e-3) ** 2)),
            np.full_like(phi, -1e-4),
        )
    ),
    tol=1e-9,
    max_nodes=200000,
)
profile = lambda p: similarity.sol(np.minimum(p, far))[0]
sorptivity = -2.0 * similarity.sol(0.0)[1]
print(f"sorptivity from the similarity solution: {sorptivity:.4e} m/√s")

Comparison

The profiles at four times against \(\Theta(x/\sqrt{t})\), their collapse onto one curve in \(\varphi\), and the cumulative infiltration against \(S\sqrt{t}\).

print("time [min]   largest profile deviation   rms deviation   (fractions of θ0 - θi)")
for t, theta in profiles.items():
    deviation = np.abs(theta - profile(x / np.sqrt(t))) / (inlet - initial)
    print(
        f"{t / 60:10.0f} {100 * deviation.max():24.2f} % {100 * np.sqrt(np.mean(deviation**2)):13.2f} %"
    )
common = np.linspace(0.0, 3.9e-3, 200)
collapsed = np.array(
    [np.interp(common, x / np.sqrt(t), theta) for t, theta in profiles.items()]
)
late = times >= 600.0
print(
    f"spread of the four profiles at equal φ: {100 * np.ptp(collapsed, axis=0).max() / (inlet - initial):.2f} % of θ0 - θi"
)
print(
    f"cumulative infiltration from 10 min on: I / (S √t) - 1 within "
    f"{100 * np.abs(infiltration[late] / (sorptivity * np.sqrt(times[late])) - 1).max():.2f} %, "
    f"fitted exponent {np.polyfit(np.log(times[late]), np.log(infiltration[late]), 1)[0]:.4f} (similarity: 0.5)"
)

Figure

The wetting profiles in the column, the same profiles against the similarity variable, and the cumulative infiltration against \(\sqrt{t}\), a straight line of slope \(S\).

fine = np.linspace(0.0, 0.32, 800)
fig = plots.figure(1, 3, size=(16, 4.8))
plots.line(
    points={
        f"{t / 60:.0f} min": (1e2 * x[::5], theta[::5]) for t, theta in profiles.items()

    ylabel="water content θ",

plots.line(

Going further

Lower initial_head to start from a drier soil: the diffusivity contrast across the front grows and the harmonic-mean conductivity needs finer cells to keep the early infiltration right. Set gravity=1.0 to turn the column vertical and watch the late-time infiltration rate approach the saturated conductivity instead of decaying as \(1/\sqrt{t}\) (Philip's two-term law \(I=S\sqrt{t}+At\)).
