Stirred tank reactors in series with level control

Two ideally mixed tank reactors in series fill up under fixed flows, then proportional level controllers on the outlet valves draw them down to a steady state.

Changing liquid volumeLevel controlReactor cascadeMole balanceClosed-form check
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation integrates volume, moles and level of each tank with ProcessUnit(dynamic_volume=True) and a first-order reaction A -> B; the valve streams are functions of the current levels, so the controllers act on every Runge-Kutta stage. The controlled phase starts from the end state of the filling phase.

What it checks

The volumes grow exactly linearly (13.0000 and 49.5000 m3 after 2 h, deviation 2e-12 m3), the total concentration in tank 1 follows its closed form within 8e-8 mol/m3, the plant mole balance closes to 2e-7, both level responses match the first-order and two-exponential closed forms within 6e-12 m, and the steady levels (1.020 and 2.200 m) and concentrations (1.67574 and 0.32943 mol/m3) agree with the controller law and the CSTR balances within 0.0002 %.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Fermenters and many liquid-phase reactors are ideally mixed tanks whose liquid volume is not fixed: feeds and withdrawals change the filling, and a level controller keeps it within limits. Two tanks with the same cross-section \(S=10\) m\(^2\) are connected in series. Tank 1 receives a feed with reactant A, tank 2 the transfer stream from tank 1 and a second, fresh feed; the product leaves tank 2. In both tanks A turns into B by a first-order reaction, \(r=k\,c_A\).

For every tank the volume, the moles of each species and the level \(h=V/S\) follow from the balances

\[\frac{dV}{dt}=\sum q_\text{in}-\sum q_\text{out},\qquad \frac{d(Vc_i)}{dt}=\sum q_\text{in}c_{i,\text{in}}-\Big(\sum q_\text{out}\Big)c_i+\nu_i\,k\,c_A\,V .\]

OneSimulation integrates them with ProcessUnit(..., dynamic_volume=True, cross_section_area=S); a ProcessStream whose flow is a function of the current state can read the levels, which is how a controller is attached.

| stream | from → to | phase 1: filling | phase 2: level control | |---|---|---|---| | feed | → tank 1, \(c_A=8\) mol/m³ | 1.0 m³/min | 1.0 m³/min | | transfer | tank 1 → tank 2 | 0.9 m³/min | \(q_{b1}+K_1(h_1-h_{s1})\) | | fresh feed | → tank 2, \(c_A=3\) mol/m³ | 0.5 m³/min | 0.5 m³/min | | product | tank 2 → | 1.0 m³/min | \(q_{b2}+K_2(h_2-h_{s2})\) |

Phase 1 starts from 1 m³ and 1.5 m³ of pure solvent and lasts two hours; phase 2 starts from its end state. The rate constant is \(k=0.37\) 1/min.

Reference (O. Levenspiel, Chemical Reaction Engineering, 3rd ed., Wiley 1999, ch. 5 and 6; D. E. Seborg, T. F. Edgar, D. A. Mellichamp and F. J. Doyle, Process Dynamics and Control, 4th ed., Wiley 2016, §2.4, §4 and §11.3). With fixed flows the volumes grow linearly, and the total concentration \(s=c_A+c_B\) in tank 1, untouched by the reaction, has the closed form

\[V_j(t)=V_j(0)+\Big(\sum q_\text{in}-\sum q_\text{out}\Big)_j\,t,\qquad s_1(t)=c_{A,\text{feed}}\Big[1-\Big(\frac{V_1(0)}{V_1(t)}\Big)^{q_\text{feed}/(q_\text{feed}-q_\text{transfer})}\Big].\]

Under proportional control tank 1 is a first-order system with time constant \(\tau_1=S/K_1\) that settles with the offset \((q_\text{feed}-q_{b1})/K_1\) above its set point; tank 2 is driven by it and responds with two exponentials:

\[h_1=h_{1\infty}+\Delta_1e^{-K_1t/S},\qquad h_2=h_{2\infty}+(\Delta_2-\beta)\,e^{-K_2t/S}+\beta\,e^{-K_1t/S},\qquad \beta=\frac{K_1\Delta_1}{K_2-K_1},\]

with \(h_{1\infty}=h_{s1}+(q_\text{feed}-q_{b1})/K_1\), \(h_{2\infty}=h_{s2}+(q_\text{feed}+q_\text{fresh}-q_{b2})/K_2\) and the initial distances \(\Delta_j\) from them. At steady state each tank is a CSTR, \(c_{A1}=q_\text{feed}c_{A,\text{feed}}/(q_\text{feed}+kV_1)\) and \(c_{A2}=(q_\text{feed}c_{A1}+q_\text{fresh}c_{A,\text{fresh}})/(q_\text{feed}+q_\text{fresh}+kV_2)\).

import numpy as np
from onesim.physics.systems import ProcessModelsProvider, ProcessStream, ProcessUnit
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme
from scipy.integrate import cumulative_trapezoid

theme.set()

Phase 1: filling with fixed flows

Flows in m³/s, times in s. The reaction law returns the production rate of each species and the heat released (none here). bind_provider_parameters selects the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method for the balances.

minute = 60.0
area, k, feed_a, fresh_a = 10.0, 0.37 / minute, 8.0, 3.0
feed, transfer, fresh, product = 1.0 / minute, 0.9 / minute, 0.5 / minute, 1.0 / minute
rk4 = ProcessModelsProvider.bind_provider_parameters(
    {"time_integration": "classical_rk4"}
)
reaction = lambda c, temperature, time: ({"A": -k * c["A"], "B": k * c["A"]}, 0.0)
filling = ProcessModelsProvider(
    component_ids=("A", "B"),
    units=(
        ProcessUnit(
            "tank1",
            volume=1.0,
            initial_concentrations={"A": 0.0, "B": 0.0},
            reaction_law=reaction,
            dynamic_volume=True,
            cross_section_area=area,
        ),
        ProcessUnit(
            "tank2",
            volume=1.5,
            initial_concentrations={"A": 0.0, "B": 0.0},
            reaction_law=reaction,
            dynamic_volume=True,
            cross_section_area=area,
        ),
    ),
    streams=(
        ProcessStream("feed", None, "tank1", feed, {"A": feed_a}),
        ProcessStream("transfer", "tank1", "tank2", transfer),
        ProcessStream("fresh", None, "tank2", fresh, {"A": fresh_a}),
        ProcessStream("product", "tank2", None, product),
    ),
    final_time=120 * minute,
    time_step=3.0,
    **rk4,
).execute()

The volumes against the linear closed form, the total concentration in tank 1 against \(s_1(t)\), and the mole balance of the plant: moles in both tanks plus moles that left with the product must equal the moles fed.

t = filling.times
c1, c2 = filling.concentrations["tank1"], filling.concentrations["tank2"]
V1, V2 = filling.volumes["tank1"], filling.volumes["tank2"]
exact_V1, exact_V2 = 1.0 + (feed - transfer) * t, 1.5 + (fresh + transfer - product) * t
exact_s1 = feed_a * (1 - (1.0 / exact_V1) ** (feed / (feed - transfer)))
inventory = V1 * (c1["A"] + c1["B"]) + V2 * (c2["A"] + c2["B"])
left = cumulative_trapezoid(
    filling.flows["product"] * (c2["A"] + c2["B"]), t, initial=0.0
)
fed = (feed * feed_a + fresh * fresh_a) * t
print(
    f"after 2 h: V1 = {V1[-1]:.4f} m³ (closed form {exact_V1[-1]:.4f}), V2 = {V2[-1]:.4f} m³ (closed form {exact_V2[-1]:.4f}); "
    f"largest volume deviation {max(np.abs(V1 - exact_V1).max(), np.abs(V2 - exact_V2).max()):.1e} m³"
)
print(
    f"levels after 2 h: h1 = {filling.levels['tank1'][-1]:.3f} m, h2 = {filling.levels['tank2'][-1]:.3f} m"
)
print(
    f"total concentration in tank 1 against the closed form: largest deviation {np.abs(c1['A'] + c1['B'] - exact_s1).max():.1e} mol/m³ "
    f"(final value {exact_s1[-1]:.4f} mol/m³)"
)
print(
    f"mole balance: {fed[-1]:.1f} mol fed, {inventory[-1]:.1f} mol in the tanks, {left[-1]:.1f} mol left with the product; "
    f"largest relative imbalance {np.abs(inventory + left - fed)[1:].max() / fed[-1]:.1e}"
)

Phase 2: proportional level control

The transfer and the product valves now follow proportional laws of the current levels. Both tanks start from the end of phase 1.

setpoint_1, setpoint_2, bias_1, bias_2 = 1.0, 2.0, 0.9 / minute, 1.0 / minute
gain_1, gain_2 = 5.0 / minute, 2.5 / minute
controlled = ProcessModelsProvider(
    component_ids=("A", "B"),
    units=tuple(
        ProcessUnit(
            name,
            volume=filling.volumes[name][-1],
            initial_concentrations={
                s: filling.concentrations[name][s][-1] for s in "AB"
            },
            reaction_law=reaction,
            dynamic_volume=True,
            cross_section_area=area,
        )
        for name in ("tank1", "tank2")
    ),
    streams=(
        ProcessStream("feed", None, "tank1", feed, {"A": feed_a}),
        ProcessStream(
            "transfer",
            "tank1",
            "tank2",
            lambda state, time: bias_1 + gain_1 * (state.levels["tank1"] - setpoint_1),
        ),
        ProcessStream("fresh", None, "tank2", fresh, {"A": fresh_a}),
        ProcessStream(
            "product",
            "tank2",
            None,
            lambda state, time: bias_2 + gain_2 * (state.levels["tank2"] - setpoint_2),
        ),
    ),
    final_time=60 * minute,
    time_step=1.0,
    **rk4,
).execute()

tc = controlled.times
h1, h2 = controlled.levels["tank1"], controlled.levels["tank2"]
steady_1, steady_2 = (
    setpoint_1 + (feed - bias_1) / gain_1,
    setpoint_2 + (feed + fresh - bias_2) / gain_2,
)
delta_1, delta_2 = h1[0] - steady_1, h2[0] - steady_2
beta = gain_1 * delta_1 / (gain_2 - gain_1)
exact_h1 = steady_1 + delta_1 * np.exp(-gain_1 * tc / area)
exact_h2 = (
    steady_2
    + (delta_2 - beta) * np.exp(-gain_2 * tc / area)
    + beta * np.exp(-gain_1 * tc / area)
)
cstr_1 = feed * feed_a / (feed + k * area * steady_1)
cstr_2 = (feed * cstr_1 + fresh * fresh_a) / (feed + fresh + k * area * steady_2)
print(
    f"level response: largest deviation from the closed form {np.abs(h1 - exact_h1).max():.1e} m (tank 1), "
    f"{np.abs(h2 - exact_h2).max():.1e} m (tank 2); time constants {area / gain_1 / minute:.0f} and {area / gain_2 / minute:.0f} min"
)
print("                          simulation (1 h)   controller law / CSTR   deviation")
for name, value, exact in (
    ("level tank 1 [m]", h1[-1], steady_1),
    ("level tank 2 [m]", h2[-1], steady_2),
    ("c_A tank 1 [mol/m³]", controlled.concentrations["tank1"]["A"][-1], cstr_1),
    ("c_A tank 2 [mol/m³]", controlled.concentrations["tank2"]["A"][-1], cstr_2),
    (
        "product flow [m³/min]",
        minute * controlled.flows["product"][-1],
        minute * (feed + fresh),
    ),
):
    print(
        f"{name:24s} {value:16.5f} {exact:22.5f} {100 * (value / exact - 1):+11.4f} %"
    )
print(
    f"offset of proportional control: tank 1 settles {100 * (steady_1 - setpoint_1):.0f} cm, tank 2 {100 * (steady_2 - setpoint_2):.0f} cm above its set point"
)

Going further

Add an integral part to the valve laws to remove the offset, or replace the proportional law by the blocks of ControlSignalFlowProvider (gain, sum, limiter) to model a valve that cannot open beyond its full stroke. A heat source and a temperature-dependent rate constant turn the tanks into a non-isothermal reactor cascade.
