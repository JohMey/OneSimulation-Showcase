Elastoplastic perforated plate in plane stress

An aluminium strip with a large central hole is pulled into the plastic range and released again, after the classical perforated-plate study of Zienkiewicz, Valliappan and King.

STEP importSection to 2DPlane-stress J2 plasticityLoading and unloading

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. Runtime Alpha 0.2.1a7 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)Download the geometry (.step)Download the geometry script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation reads a quarter of the strip from a STEP file, cuts its mid-plane and solves plane-stress equilibrium on quadratic triangles with von Mises plasticity and linear hardening; at every quadrature point the out-of-plane strain is found so that the out-of-plane stress vanishes. The load rises beyond the net-section limit and returns to zero. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

The elastic stress concentration is 2.127 against 2.156 from Peterson's chart, yield starts between 0.225 and 0.250 σ_y against 0.232 σ_y, 95 % of the ligament has yielded at 0.55 σ_y, the hole edge sits on the hardened yield surface and its residual stress of -242 MPa equals the elastic superposition.

Geometry

The notebook starts at the import of the CAD file holed_plate_elastoplastic_plane_stress.step: STEP (AP242), readable by any CAD program, with the names of the faces that the boundary conditions use. Put it next to the notebook.

The geometry script

The script holed_plate_elastoplastic_plane_stress_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the file. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Elastoplastic perforated plate in plane stress".

Builds a quarter of a perforated strip (full plate 200 mm wide and 360 mm long with a central hole of
100 mm diameter, 1 mm thick) with the CAD objects of OneSimulation, names the faces the simulation
refers to and writes holed_plate_elastoplastic_plane_stress.step. The example computes on its
mid-plane section.
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import Box, Cylinder

quarter = Box(length=0.100, width=0.180, height=0.001) - Cylinder(
    radius=0.050, length=0.003, origin=(0.0, 0.0, -0.001)
)
quarter = quarter.grouped(
    hole=quarter.select(faces=lambda face: face.kind == "cylinder"),
    symmetry_x=quarter.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "plane" and abs(face.center[0]) < 1e-9
    ),
    symmetry_y=quarter.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "plane" and abs(face.center[1]) < 1e-9
    ),
    loaded=quarter.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "plane" and abs(face.center[1] - 0.180) < 1e-9
    ),
    side=quarter.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "plane" and abs(face.center[0] - 0.100) < 1e-9
    ),
)
quarter.save("holed_plate_elastoplastic_plane_stress.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A hole concentrates the stress in a loaded plate. As the load grows, the material at the edge of the hole yields first; a plastic zone spreads across the ligament between hole and edge, and the plate stiffens again only through hardening once the whole net section is plastic. On unloading the elastic spring-back of the surrounding plate puts the plastic zone into compression: residual stresses remain at the hole edge.

The case follows the classical perforated strip of Zienkiewicz, Valliappan and King (Int. J. Numer. Meth. Eng. 1, 1969, 75-100), also in O.C. Zienkiewicz and R.L. Taylor, The Finite Element Method, Vol. 2. In plane stress the out-of-plane stress vanishes; the von Mises yield condition with linear isotropic hardening is

\[\sqrt{\tfrac32\,\mathbf s:\mathbf s}=\sigma_y+H\,\bar\varepsilon_p ,\]

and the out-of-plane strain is found at every quadrature point so that \(\sigma_{zz}=0\).

Setup. A strip 200 mm wide, 360 mm long and 1 mm thick with a central hole of \(d=100\) mm; \(E=68.7\) GPa, \(\nu=0.2\), \(\sigma_y=238\) MPa, \(H=2.21\) GPa (the values of the original study, 7000, 24.3 and 225 kgf/mm²). The ends carry a uniform tension \(\sigma\) that rises to \(0.55\,\sigma_y\) and returns to zero. A quarter of the plate comes from the CAD file holed_plate_elastoplastic_plane_stress.step, written by holed_plate_elastoplastic_plane_stress_geometry.py with the CAD objects of OneSimulation; the computation uses its mid-plane section.

| edge of the section | condition | |---|---| | symmetry_x | \(u_x=0\) | | symmetry_y | \(u_y=0\) | | loaded | traction \(\sigma\) in \(y\) | | hole, side | free |

Reference. (1) For an elastic strip with \(d/W=0.5\) the stress at the hole edge is \(K_{t,n}\,\sigma_\text{net}\) with \(\sigma_\text{net}=\sigma\,W/(W-d)\) and \(K_{t,n}=2.156\) (Pilkey and Pilkey, Peterson's Stress Concentration Factors, 3rd ed., Chart 4.1). (2) Yield therefore starts at \(\sigma=\sigma_y(1-d/W)/K_{t,n}\). (3) Without hardening the plate collapses when the whole net section yields, at \(\sigma=\sigma_y(1-d/W)=0.5\,\sigma_y\). (4) Wherever the material yields, the von Mises stress equals the current yield stress \(\sigma_y+H\bar\varepsilon_p\). (5) If the plate unloads elastically, the residual stress is the peak stress minus the elastic solution for the peak load (Melan's superposition).

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials.solids import IsotropicHardening, J2Plasticity
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.numerics.nonlinear import DampedNewton
from onesim.physics.solid_mechanics import SmallStrainSolid
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns the quarter plate with its named faces; section cuts its mid-plane. The quadratic triangles are 3 mm at the hole and grow to 15 mm.

quarter = load("holed_plate_elastoplastic_plane_stress.step")
faces = quarter.boundaries
plane = quarter.section((0, 0, 1), origin=(0, 0, quarter.size[2] / 2))
edges = plane.boundaries
mesh = TriangularMesher(
    target_size=0.015,
    size_field=lambda p: np.minimum(
        0.003 + 0.08 * np.abs(np.hypot(p[:, 0], p[:, 1]) - 0.05), 0.015
    ),
).generate(plane)

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 7))
plots.geometry(
    quarter,
    selection={
        "hole": faces.hole,
        "loaded end": faces.loaded,
        "symmetry": faces.symmetry_x | faces.symmetry_y,
    },
    labels=False,
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=60, azimuth=-70),
    title="Quarter of the perforated strip",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    mesh,
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"Mid-plane section: {mesh.cell_count} quadratic triangles",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

SmallStrainSolid in plane stress with a J2Plasticity material; the out-of-plane condensation runs at every quadrature point. The end traction follows the load history step by step.

youngs_modulus, poisson_ratio, yield_stress, hardening = 68.7e9, 0.2, 238e6, 2.21e9
width, diameter = 0.200, 0.100
history = np.r_[0.0, np.arange(0.2, 0.551, 0.025), 0.40, 0.25, 0.10, 0.0] * yield_stress
result = SmallStrainSolid(
    youngs_modulus=youngs_modulus,
    poisson_ratio=poisson_ratio,
    kinematics="plane_stress",
    thickness=quarter.size[2],
    material=J2Plasticity(
        youngs_modulus / (2 * (1 + poisson_ratio)),
        IsotropicHardening(yield_stress, hardening),
    ),
).solve(
    mesh=mesh,
    times=np.arange(len(history), dtype=float),
    order=2,
    displacements=((edges.symmetry_x, "x", 0.0), (edges.symmetry_y, "y", 0.0)),
    tractions=((edges.loaded, lambda x, y, t: (0.0, history[int(round(t))])),),
    nonlinear=DampedNewton(
        linear_solver=SparseDirect(),
        relative_tolerance=1e-12,
        absolute_tolerance=1e-6,
        maximum_iterations=40,
    ),
)
points = result.quadrature_coordinates
edge = int(np.argmin(np.hypot(points[:, 0] - diameter / 2, points[:, 1])))
plastic = np.array(
    [
        [point.material.equivalent_plastic_strain for cell in step for point in cell]
        for step in result.material_history
    ]
)
deviator = (
    result.stress
    - np.trace(result.stress, axis1=2, axis2=3)[..., None, None] * np.eye(3) / 3
)
mises = np.sqrt(1.5 * np.sum(deviator**2, axis=(2, 3)))
top = np.isclose(result.space.dof_coordinates[:, 1], 0.180)
stretch = result.displacement[:, top, 1].mean(axis=1)

Comparison with the handbook values

The stress at the hole edge in the first, still elastic step; the load at which the edge yields; the net-section limit; and the von Mises stress at the hole edge against the hardened yield stress at peak load and after unloading.

peterson, ratio = 2.156, 1 - diameter / width
onset = yield_stress * ratio / peterson
peak, final = int(np.argmax(history)), len(history) - 1
print(
    f"hole edge at σ = {history[1] / 1e6:.1f} MPa: σ_yy = {result.stress[1, edge, 1, 1] / 1e6:.1f} MPa, "
    f"K_t,n = {result.stress[1, edge, 1, 1] * ratio / history[1]:.3f} (Peterson {peterson}, point {1e3 * (np.hypot(*points[edge]) - diameter / 2):.2f} mm inside the edge)"
)
first = int(np.argmax(plastic.max(axis=1) > 0))
print(
    f"first yield between σ = {history[first - 1] / yield_stress:.3f} σ_y and {history[first] / yield_stress:.3f} σ_y (reference {onset / yield_stress:.3f} σ_y)"
)
ligament = np.abs(points[:, 1]) < 0.004
print(
    f"net section without hardening collapses at σ = {ratio:.2f} σ_y; at peak load {history[peak] / yield_stress:.3f} σ_y "
    f"{100 * np.mean(plastic[peak][ligament] > 0):.0f} % of the ligament points have yielded"
)
for k, label in ((peak, "peak load"), (final, "after unloading")):
    print(
        f"{label}: hole edge σ_yy = {result.stress[k, edge, 1, 1] / 1e6:7.1f} MPa, von Mises {mises[k, edge] / 1e6:6.1f} MPa, "
        f"current yield stress σ_y + H ε_p = {(yield_stress + hardening * plastic[k, edge]) / 1e6:6.1f} MPa, ε_p = {100 * plastic[k, edge]:.3f} %"
    )
print(
    f"residual stress at the hole edge by elastic superposition: {(result.stress[peak, edge, 1, 1] - result.stress[1, edge, 1, 1] / history[1] * history[peak]) / 1e6:.1f} MPa "
    f"(simulation {result.stress[final, edge, 1, 1] / 1e6:.1f} MPa): the unloading stayed elastic"
)
print(
    f"permanent elongation of the quarter after unloading: {1e6 * stretch[-1]:.1f} µm; largest out-of-plane stress {np.abs(result.stress[..., 2, 2]).max() / 1e3:.2f} kPa"
)

Figure

Left the end tension against the stress at the hole edge, with the elastic concentration, the onset of yield and the net-section limit; in the middle against the elongation of the quarter; right the yielded quadrature points at three loads. The plastic zone starts at the hole edge and crosses the ligament around the net-section limit, where the plate softens to the hardening slope. On unloading the hole edge springs back elastically into compression.

Going further

Set the hardening modulus to zero and drive the end by displacement to see the load level off at the net-section limit, add kinematic hardening to model the Bauschinger effect on unloading, or repeat the cycle and watch whether the hole edge shakes down or keeps yielding.
