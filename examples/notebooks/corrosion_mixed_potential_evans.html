Corrosion kinetics: Evans diagram

Iron in aerated water: anodic dissolution against oxygen reduction with a transport-limited current, the corrosion point and its dependence on the oxygen supply.

Electrode polarizationMixed potentialClosed-form check
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation describes the electrode with ElectrodePolarization and two Tafel reactions, one with a limiting current; solve_stationary gives the polarisation curve for the Evans diagram and mixed_potential solves the current balance with a selectable least-squares solver over seven decades of the oxygen limiting current.

What it checks

The corrosion potential -0.320001 V matches an independent root of the current balance to 2e-15 V over the whole sweep; i_corr follows the limiting current (0.099995 A/m^2 at i_L = 0.1, 0.116 mm/year of iron) and tends to the closed-form Tafel intersection, 77.03 against 77.43 A/m^2 at i_L = 1e4 A/m^2.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A piece of iron in neutral, aerated water corrodes because two electrode reactions run on the same metal surface at the same potential \(E\): the anodic dissolution of the metal and the cathodic reduction of dissolved oxygen,

\[\mathrm{Fe}\rightarrow\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}+2e^-,\qquad \mathrm{O_2}+2\mathrm{H_2O}+4e^-\rightarrow4\,\mathrm{OH^-}.\]

Far from their equilibrium potentials both follow Tafel laws, and the oxygen reduction is limited by the diffusion of oxygen to the surface (limiting current density \(i_L\)):

\[i_a=i_{0,a}\,10^{(E-E_a)/b_a},\qquad |i_c|=\frac{i_k}{1+i_k/i_L},\quad i_k=i_{0,c}\,10^{-(E-E_c)/b_c}.\]

A freely corroding electrode carries no net current, \(i_a=|i_c|\): this fixes the corrosion (mixed) potential \(E_\mathrm{corr}\) and the corrosion current density \(i_\mathrm{corr}\). OneSimulation's ElectrodePolarization holds the two reactions (TafelReaction, one with a transport limit); solve_stationary() returns the polarisation curve over a potential sweep, and mixed_potential() solves the current balance with a selectable nonlinear solver.

Reference. U. R. Evans, The Corrosion and Oxidation of Metals, Arnold 1960; D. A. Jones, Principles and Prevention of Corrosion, 2nd ed., Prentice Hall 1996, ch. 3-4. Without transport limit the intersection of the two Tafel lines is closed form,

\[E_\mathrm{corr}=\frac{\log_{10}(i_{0,c}/i_{0,a})+E_a/b_a+E_c/b_c}{1/b_a+1/b_c},\qquad i_\mathrm{corr}=i_{0,a}\,10^{(E_\mathrm{corr}-E_a)/b_a};\]

when oxygen transport controls, \(i_\mathrm{corr}\to i_L\) and \(E_\mathrm{corr}\to E_a+b_a\log_{10}(i_L/i_{0,a})\). In between, the balance is solved independently with a bracketing root finder. Faraday's law turns the corrosion current into a penetration rate \(\dot d=i_\mathrm{corr}M/(nF\rho)\).

import numpy as np
from onesim.materials import TafelReaction
from onesim.numerics.nonlinear import LeastSquares
from onesim.physics.chemistry import ElectrodePolarization
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme
from scipy.optimize import brentq

theme.set()

Electrode kinetics

Potentials against the standard hydrogen electrode. Iron: \(E_a=-0.44\) V, \(i_{0,a}=10^{-3}\) A/m², \(b_a=60\) mV/decade. Oxygen at pH 7: \(E_c=+0.80\) V, \(i_{0,c}=10^{-6}\) A/m², \(b_c=120\) mV/decade, and a limiting current density of 0.1 A/m² for still, air-saturated water at room temperature (values of the order given by Jones, ch. 3).

iron = {"potential": -0.44, "exchange": 1e-3, "slope": 0.06}
oxygen = {"potential": 0.80, "exchange": 1e-6, "slope": 0.12}
limiting, molar_mass, density, faraday = 0.1, 55.845e-3, 7874.0, 96485.33212
sweep = np.linspace(-0.75, 0.30, 421)
electrode = lambda limit: ElectrodePolarization(
    reactions=(
        TafelReaction(
            reaction_id="iron dissolution",
            exchange_current_density=iron["exchange"],
            equilibrium_potential=iron["potential"],
            tafel_slope=iron["slope"],
            direction="anodic",
        ),
        TafelReaction(
            reaction_id="oxygen reduction",
            exchange_current_density=oxygen["exchange"],
            equilibrium_potential=oxygen["potential"],
            tafel_slope=oxygen["slope"],
            direction="cathodic",
            limiting_current_density=limit,
        ),
    ),
    potential_sweep=sweep,
)

Mixed potential

The current balance is solved with Levenberg-Marquardt between -0.8 V and +0.3 V; the independent reference is a bracketing root of the same balance written out by hand.

solver = LeastSquares(method="levenberg_marquardt")
corrosion = electrode(limiting).mixed_potential(
    bounds=(-0.8, 0.3), solver=solver, current_tolerance=1e-12
)
balance = lambda E, limit: (
    iron["exchange"] * 10 ** ((E - iron["potential"]) / iron["slope"])
    - 1
    / (
        1 / (oxygen["exchange"] * 10 ** (-(E - oxygen["potential"]) / oxygen["slope"]))
        + 1 / limit
    )
)
reference = brentq(balance, -0.8, 0.3, args=(limiting,), xtol=1e-14)
tafel_potential = (
    np.log10(oxygen["exchange"] / iron["exchange"])
    + iron["potential"] / iron["slope"]
    + oxygen["potential"] / oxygen["slope"]
) / (1 / iron["slope"] + 1 / oxygen["slope"])
current = corrosion.reaction_current_densities["iron dissolution"]
print(
    f"corrosion potential {corrosion.potential:.6f} V (independent root {reference:.6f} V, "
    f"transport-limit estimate {iron['potential'] + iron['slope'] * np.log10(limiting / iron['exchange']):.6f} V)"
)
print(
    f"corrosion current density {current:.6f} A/m² (limiting current {limiting} A/m²), balance residual {corrosion.balance_residual:.1e} A/m², "
    f"{corrosion.evaluations} evaluations"
)
print(
    f"penetration rate of iron: {current * molar_mass / (2 * faraday * density) * 1e3 * 3.15576e7:.3f} mm/year"
)
print(
    f"without transport limit the Tafel lines would cross at {tafel_potential:.4f} V and "
    f"{iron['exchange'] * 10 ** ((tafel_potential - iron['potential']) / iron['slope']):.1f} A/m²"
)

Oxygen supply

The limiting current density grows with stirring, flow and oxygen content. Over seven decades of \(i_L\) the corrosion current moves from transport control (\(i_\mathrm{corr}=i_L\)) to activation control (the Tafel intersection).

limits = np.logspace(-3, 4, 29)
points = [
    electrode(limit).mixed_potential(
        bounds=(-0.8, 0.3), solver=solver, current_tolerance=1e-12
    )
    for limit in limits
]
potentials = np.array([p.potential for p in points])
currents = np.array([p.reaction_current_densities["iron dissolution"] for p in points])
roots = np.array(
    [brentq(balance, -0.8, 0.3, args=(limit,), xtol=1e-14) for limit in limits]
)
print(
    f"largest deviation of the corrosion potential from the independent root over the sweep: {np.abs(potentials - roots).max():.1e} V"
)
print(
    f"i_corr / i_L at i_L = 1e-3 A/m²: {currents[0] / limits[0]:.4f};  i_corr at i_L = 1e4 A/m²: {currents[-1]:.2f} A/m² "
    f"(Tafel intersection {iron['exchange'] * 10 ** ((tafel_potential - iron['potential']) / iron['slope']):.2f} A/m²)"
)

Figure

The Evans diagram (potential against the logarithm of the current density of each branch, with the net polarisation curve), and the corrosion current and potential against the oxygen limiting current with their two closed-form limits.

curve = electrode(limiting).solve_stationary()
tafel_current = iron["exchange"] * 10 ** (
    (tafel_potential - iron["potential"]) / iron["slope"]
)
fig = plots.figure(1, 3, size=(18, 5.5))
plots.line(
    y={
        "iron dissolution": (
            np.abs(curve.reaction_current_densities["iron dissolution"]),
            curve.potential,
        ),
        "oxygen reduction": (
            np.abs(curve.reaction_current_densities["oxygen reduction"]),
            curve.potential,
        ),
        "net current |i|": (np.abs(curve.current_density), curve.potential),
    },
    points={"corrosion point": (np.array([current]), np.array([corrosion.potential]))},
    vlines={"limiting current": limiting},
    colors={"net current |i|": 6},
    styles={"net current |i|": "dashed"},
    scale="logx",
    xlimits=(1e-5, 1e3),
    ylimits=(-0.75, 0.3),
    xlabel="current density |i| [A/m²]",
    ylabel="potential E [V vs SHE]",
    title="Evans diagram",
    legend="upper right",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.line(
    points={"OneSimulation": (limits, currents)},
    reference={
        "transport control i = i_L": (limits, limits),
        "Tafel intersection": (limits, np.full_like(limits, tafel_current)),
    },
    scale="loglog",
    ylimits=(5e-4, 3e2),
    xlabel="oxygen limiting current i_L [A/m²]",
    ylabel="corrosion current i_corr [A/m²]",
    title="Corrosion current against oxygen supply",
    legend="lower right",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.line(
    points={"OneSimulation": (limits, potentials)},
    reference={
        "independent root": (limits, roots),
        "E_a + b_a log(i_L / i_0a)": (
            limits,
            iron["potential"] + iron["slope"] * np.log10(limits / iron["exchange"]),
        ),
    },
    hlines={"Tafel intersection": tafel_potential},
    scale="logx",
    ylimits=(-0.65, -0.05),
    xlabel="oxygen limiting current i_L [A/m²]",
    ylabel="corrosion potential E_corr [V vs SHE]",
    title="Corrosion potential",
    legend="lower right",
    ax=fig[2],
)
plots.show()

Going further

Add a second cathodic reaction, hydrogen evolution, and lower the pH: below about pH 4 it overtakes oxygen reduction and the corrosion current no longer follows the oxygen supply. Couple the iron to zinc in a galvanic pair by adding the zinc dissolution branch: the mixed potential drops and the iron current falls (cathodic protection). Measured polarisation data can be fitted for the Tafel parameters with the least-squares fit of a SimulationStudyService built on the same model.
