Biconical antenna: impedance and radiation

Two metal cones tip to tip are fed across a gap of 2 mm; the antenna guides a spherical wave outward and is matched over a wide band.

STEP importAxisymmetric MaxwellLumped portSpherical PMLFar field

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. OneSimulation 1.0 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)Download the geometry (.step)Download the geometry script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation reads the air around the cones from a STEP file, cuts the meridian plane and solves the axisymmetric time-harmonic Maxwell equations with quadratic Nedelec elements from 1 to 5 GHz, with a lumped port of 100 ohm at the gap and a spherical perfectly matched layer around the antenna. The input impedance comes from the port, the radiated power from the far field. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

Above 3 GHz the mean input resistance is 99.4 ohm and the mean reactance +1.0 ohm against the 99.9 ohm of the infinite biconical line, 120 ln cot(47 deg / 2); the largest reflection coefficient there is 0.054. The power radiated through the far field equals the accepted power within 0.7 % at all 17 frequencies.

Geometry

Save the STEP file biconical_antenna_impedance.step next to the notebook before running it.

The geometry script

The script biconical_antenna_impedance_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the file. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Biconical antenna".

Builds with the CAD objects of OneSimulation, as a half solid of revolution about the z axis, the air
around a biconical antenna: two metal cones of half angle 47 degrees and slant length 100 mm with flat
tops, tip to tip across a feed gap of 2 mm bridged by a thin feed wire (radius 1 mm), in a box reaching
450 mm from the axis and from the feed. File biconical_antenna_impedance.step names the faces port
(the surface of the feed wire, where the generator acts), metal (the cones) and outer (the walls of
the box); the axis is left unnamed.
"""

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d2 import Path, Profile, Segment
from onesim.cad.d3 import Revolution

half_angle, slant, wire, gap, box = np.radians(47.0), 0.1, 0.001, 0.002, 0.45
top = (wire + slant * np.sin(half_angle), gap / 2 + slant * np.cos(half_angle))
outline = Path(
    (0.0, box),
    (
        Segment((0.0, top[1]), name="axis"),
        Segment(top, name="metal"),
        Segment((wire, gap / 2), name="metal"),
        Segment((wire, -gap / 2), name="port"),
        Segment((top[0], -top[1]), name="metal"),
        Segment((0.0, -top[1]), name="metal"),
        Segment((0.0, -box), name="axis"),
        Segment((box, -box), name="outer"),
        Segment((box, box), name="outer"),
        Segment((0.0, box), name="outer"),
    ),
)
Revolution(profile=Profile(outline), angle=np.pi).rotated((0, 0, 1), -np.pi / 2).save(
    "biconical_antenna_impedance.step"
)

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Two cones meeting tip to tip form a biconical transmission line: it guides a spherical TEM wave outward from the feed with the characteristic impedance \(Z_c=120\ln\cot(\theta_0/2)\) for the half angle \(\theta_0\) of the cones. Once the cones are longer than the wavelength, little of the wave is reflected at their ends, and the antenna is matched over a wide band with a pattern like that of a dipole: omnidirectional about the axis, strongest in the plane of the feed.

Here each cone has a half angle of 47° (\(Z_c\approx100\ \Omega\)), a slant length of 100 mm and a flat top. The generator drives the gap of 2 mm between the tips across a thin feed wire. Everything is symmetric about the axis, and the TEM wave has only \(E_r\), \(E_z\) and \(H_\varphi\): OneSimulation solves the meridian plane with polarization="Erphiz" and azimuthal_mode=0, and LumpedPort feeds the gap with 1 W from a 100 Ω generator.

boundarycondition
port (feed wire surface)lumped port, 100 Ω, 1 W available
metal (cones)perfect conductor
outer (box walls)perfect conductor behind a spherical PML from 350 to 450 mm

The air comes from biconical_antenna_impedance.step, written by biconical_antenna_impedance_geometry.py with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.

Reference. (i) The characteristic impedance of the infinite biconical line, \(Z_c=120\ln\cot(\theta_0/2)=100.0\ \Omega\) (Schelkunoff, Electromagnetic Waves, 1943; Balanis, Antenna Theory, 4th ed., §9.3); the impedance of the finite antenna oscillates about it, less and less as the cones grow longer than the wavelength. (ii) Energy balance: the power radiated through the far field, \(P_\mathrm{rad}=\frac{1}{2\eta_0}\oint|\mathbf F|^2\,\mathrm d\Omega\), equals the power the antenna accepts from the generator, \(P_\mathrm{acc}=(1-|S_{11}|^2)\cdot1\) W.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import VACUUM_PERMEABILITY, VACUUM_PERMITTIVITY
from onesim.materials.electromagnetic import (
    IsotropicElectromagneticMaterial,
    spherical_pml,
)
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Nedelec, RevolvedH1SpaceFactory
from onesim.numerics.linear import ComplexSparseDirect
from onesim.physics.electromagnetics import (
    FrequencyDomainMaxwell,
    LumpedPort,
    PerfectElectricConductor,
    axisymmetric_far_field,
    input_impedance,
)
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

light = 1 / np.sqrt(VACUUM_PERMEABILITY * VACUUM_PERMITTIVITY)
eta = np.sqrt(VACUUM_PERMEABILITY / VACUUM_PERMITTIVITY)

Geometry and mesh

section with the plane \(y=0\) gives the meridian plane (\(x=r\)). Quadratic triangles of 7 mm (eight per wavelength at 5 GHz), refined to 0.7 mm at the feed gap and 2 mm on the cones.

air = load("biconical_antenna_impedance.step")
meridian = air.section((0, 1, 0))
faces = meridian.boundaries
mesh = TriangularMesher(
    target_size=0.007, local_sizes={"port": 0.0007, "metal": 0.002}, grading=0.25
).generate(meridian)

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 6.5))
plots.geometry(
    air,
    selection={"feed": air.boundaries.port, "cones": air.boundaries.metal},
    labels=False,
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=20, azimuth=-60),
    title="Air around the antenna, half (STEP)",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    mesh,
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"Meridian plane: {mesh.cell_count} quadratic triangles",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

One solve per frequency from 1 to 5 GHz in steps of 0.25 GHz.

model = lambda frequency: FrequencyDomainMaxwell(
    domain=meridian.domain(),
    frequency=frequency,
    material=spherical_pml(IsotropicElectromagneticMaterial(), 0.35, 0.45),
    polarization="Erphiz",
    azimuthal_mode=0,
    phasor_convention="exp(-i*omega*t)",
    amplitude_convention="peak",
)
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Nedelec(order=2), provider=RevolvedH1SpaceFactory()),
    linear_solver=ComplexSparseDirect(),
)
solve = lambda m: Simulation(
    problem=Problem(
        name="Bicone",
        geometry=meridian,
        physics=(m,),
        boundary_conditions=(
            LumpedPort(
                physics=m, boundary=faces.port, impedance=100.0, incident_power=1.0
            ),
            PerfectElectricConductor(physics=m, boundary=faces.metal | faces.outer),
        ),
    ),
    mesh=mesh,
    numerics=numerics,
).solve_stationary()
frequencies = np.arange(1.0e9, 5.01e9, 0.25e9)
runs = {f: (solve(m := model(f)), m) for f in frequencies}

Results

Input impedance (in the engineering convention \(e^{+j\omega t}\), the conjugate of the solver's phasors), reflection coefficient against 100 Ω, and the energy balance from the far field.

impedance = np.array([np.conj(input_impedance(*runs[f])) for f in frequencies])
reflection = np.abs((impedance - 100.0) / (impedance + 100.0))
theta = np.linspace(0, np.pi, 181)
radiated = np.array(
    [
        np.pi
        / eta
        * np.trapezoid(
            np.sum(
                np.abs(axisymmetric_far_field(*runs[f], theta, 0.0, radius=0.3)) ** 2,
                axis=1,
            )
            * np.sin(theta),
            theta,
        )
        for f in frequencies
    ]
)
characteristic = 120 * np.log(1 / np.tan(np.radians(47.0) / 2))
print(f"characteristic impedance of the biconical line: {characteristic:.2f} ohm")
print(
    "f [GHz]   slant / wavelength   Z_in [ohm]            |S11|     P_rad [W]   P_acc [W]   difference"
)
for f, z, s, p in zip(frequencies, impedance, reflection, radiated):
    print(
        f"{f / 1e9:7.2f} {0.1 * f / light:18.2f}   {z.real:7.2f} {z.imag:+8.2f}j {s:10.4f} {p:11.4f} {1 - s**2:11.4f} {p / (1 - s**2) - 1:+11.1e}"
    )
long = frequencies >= 3e9
print(
    f"above 3 GHz (cones longer than one wavelength): mean resistance {impedance[long].real.mean():.1f} ohm, mean reactance {impedance[long].imag.mean():+.1f} ohm, "
    f"largest |S11| {reflection[long].max():.3f}"
)

Figure

Left: the input impedance against frequency with the characteristic impedance of the biconical line. Right: the radiation pattern \(|\mathbf F|^2\) in the elevation plane at 1.5, 3 and 5 GHz, normalized to its maximum; it is symmetric about the plane of the feed, and at high frequencies the finite cones split the main lobe.

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(16, 6))
plots.line(
    frequencies / 1e9,
    {"resistance": impedance.real, "reactance": impedance.imag},
    markers=True,
    hlines={"Z_c of the biconical line": characteristic},
    xlabel="frequency [GHz]",
    ylabel="input impedance [ohm]",
    title="Input impedance",
    legend="lower right",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.line(
    90 - np.degrees(theta),
    {
        f"{f / 1e9:.1f} GHz": (lambda p: p / p.max())(
            np.sum(
                np.abs(axisymmetric_far_field(*runs[f], theta, 0.0, radius=0.3)) ** 2,
                axis=1,
            )
        )
        for f in (1.5e9, 3.0e9, 5.0e9)
    },
    xlabel="elevation above the plane of the feed [°]",
    ylabel="|F|^2 / max",
    title="Radiation pattern",
    legend="upper left",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Going further

Change the half angle in biconical_antenna_impedance_geometry.py: 30° raises the characteristic impedance to about 158 Ω. Spherical caps instead of flat tops smooth the oscillation of the impedance at low frequencies.
