Magnetostatics · 3D

Magnetic prospecting of a buried iron ore body

A magnetite body 100 m below the surface disturbs the inclined Earth field; the magnetometer anomaly is mapped on the surface.

STEP importEarth fieldEdge elementsClosed-form check
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)Download the geometry (.step)Download the geometry script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation reads the half of the surroundings and the ore body from STEP files, embeds the body in one tetrahedral mesh refined at the measuring surface and solves the vector potential with second-order Nedelec elements; the undisturbed Earth field enters on the outer face, the meridian plane is a symmetry plane. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

The north-south anomaly profile follows the exact dipole field of a permeable sphere (peak 1448 nT, minimum -262 nT): the peak is 1494 nT on the finer of two meshes (+3.2 %, +6.0 % on the coarser), and the field inside the body is 64.31 µT against 64.29 µT exact.

Geometry

The notebook starts at the import of the CAD files magnetic_prospecting_ore_body.step, magnetic_prospecting_ore_body_ore.step: STEP (AP242), readable by any CAD program, with the names of the faces that the boundary conditions use. Put them next to the notebook.

The geometry script

The script magnetic_prospecting_ore_body_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Magnetic prospecting of an iron ore body".

Builds the half of the ground and air around a buried magnetite body that lies on one side of the
magnetic meridian plane, with the CAD objects of OneSimulation. The ore body is a sphere of 50 m
radius whose centre lies 100 m below the surface; the modelled region is a half ball of 800 m
radius about that centre. The region is written to magnetic_prospecting_ore_body.step with the face
groups meridian (the plane y = 0) and outer; the half of the ore body goes to
magnetic_prospecting_ore_body_ore.step.
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import Box, Sphere

ore_radius, region_radius = 50.0, 800.0
half = Box(
    length=2 * region_radius,
    width=region_radius,
    height=2 * region_radius,
    origin=(-region_radius, 0.0, -region_radius),
).named("cut")
region = Sphere(radius=region_radius) & half
faces = region.boundaries
region = region.grouped(meridian=faces["cut.y_min"], outer=faces.surface)
region.save("magnetic_prospecting_ore_body.step")
(Sphere(radius=ore_radius) & half).save("magnetic_prospecting_ore_body_ore.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Magnetic prospecting finds ore bodies rich in magnetite by the small changes, the anomalies, they cause in the magnetic field of the Earth. A magnetometer on the surface records the total-field anomaly

\[\Delta T=|\mathbf B|-|\mathbf B_0|\approx(\mathbf B-\mathbf B_0)\cdot\hat{\mathbf B}_0 ,\]

the change of the field strength along the direction of the undisturbed field \(\mathbf B_0\).

Here the Earth's field has a strength of 50 µT and an inclination of 60° (a mid-latitude of the northern hemisphere), pointing north (\(+x\)) and downwards. The ore body is a sphere of radius \(a=50\) m with its centre 100 m below the surface and a magnetic susceptibility \(\chi=0.5\) (\(\mu_r=1.5\), a magnetite-rich ore). Rock and air are non-magnetic. Without currents, \(\nabla\times\mathbf H=0\), \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0\); OneSimulation solves for the vector potential with second-order Nédélec elements and a Coulomb gauge.

The field is symmetric about the magnetic meridian plane through the body, so half of the surroundings is modelled: a half ball of 800 m radius about the centre of the body, from magnetic_prospecting_ore_body.step with the face groups meridian and outer; the half ore body is in magnetic_prospecting_ore_body_ore.step. The script magnetic_prospecting_ore_body_geometry.py builds both with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.

| faces | condition | |---|---| | meridian (the plane \(y=0\)) | field tangential, \(\mathbf n\times\mathbf A=0\) | | outer, 800 m from the body | undisturbed Earth field, \(\mathbf A=\tfrac12\mathbf B_0\times\mathbf r\) |

Reference. A permeable sphere in a uniform field is magnetized uniformly; outside, it acts as a dipole at its centre (Telford, Geldart and Sheriff, Applied Geophysics, 2nd ed., 1990, Sec. 3.7; Blakely, Potential Theory in Gravity and Magnetic Applications, 1995, Ch. 4):

\[\mathbf B=\mathbf B_0+K\,\frac{a^3}{r^3}\big[3(\mathbf B_0\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})\hat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf B_0\big], \qquad K=\frac{\mu_r-1}{\mu_r+2}.\]

The outer boundary sees the undisturbed field only; the dipole field it misses there is about 1 nT, a thousandth of the anomaly.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d2 import Plane
from onesim.cad.d3 import Box
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import VacuumPermeability
from onesim.meshing import LocalRefinement, TetrahedralMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Nedelec
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.physics.magnetostatics import (
    CoulombGauge,
    MagneticInsulation,
    MagneticPotentialBoundary,
    MagneticVectorPotential,
    Magnetostatics,
)
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation, regionwise
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns the half ball with its named faces and the half ore body. The mesher embeds the body and refines a layer around the surface where the magnetometer measures; elsewhere the cells grow with the distance from the body. Two meshes differ in their cell sizes.

region, ore = load("magnetic_prospecting_ore_body.step"), load(
    "magnetic_prospecting_ore_body_ore.step"
)
faces = region.boundaries
radius, depth = ore.size[0] / 2, 100.0
sizes = (0.36 * radius, 0.25 * radius)
meshes = [
    TetrahedralMesher(
        target_size=150.0,
        embedded_regions=(ore,),
        refinements=(
            LocalRefinement(region=ore, target_size=size),
            LocalRefinement(
                region=Box(
                    length=800.0,
                    width=400.0,
                    height=60.0,
                    origin=(-400.0, 0.0, depth - 30.0),
                ),
                target_size=2.5 * size,
            ),
        ),
        size_field=lambda points, size=size: np.clip(
            size * (np.linalg.norm(points, axis=1) / radius) ** 1.5, size, 150.0
        ),
    ).generate(region)
    for size in sizes
]

view = plots.Camera(elevation=20, azimuth=-120)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 6.5))
plots.geometry(
    [region.named("ground and air"), ore.named("ore body")],
    camera=view,
    title="Half of the surroundings of the ore body",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes[0],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    camera=view,
    title=f"Coarsest mesh: {meshes[0].cell_count} tetrahedra",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

regionwise gives the ore body its permeability; the Earth's field enters through the vector potential on the outer face.

earth, inclination, mu_r = 50e-6, np.radians(60.0), 1.5
B0 = earth * np.array((np.cos(inclination), 0.0, -np.sin(inclination)))
mu0 = float(VacuumPermeability().evaluate())
model = Magnetostatics(
    domain=region.volume,
    formulation=MagneticVectorPotential(gauge=CoulombGauge()),
    permeability=regionwise(
        ((lambda points: np.linalg.norm(points, axis=1) < radius, mu0 * mu_r),),
        default=mu0,
        unit="H/m",
    ),
)
problem = Problem(
    name="Magnetic prospecting",
    geometry=region,
    physics=(model,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        MagneticPotentialBoundary(
            boundary=faces.outer,
            physics=model,
            value=lambda x, y, z: 0.5 * np.cross(B0, np.stack((x, y, z), axis=-1)),
        ),
        MagneticInsulation(boundary=faces.meridian, physics=model),
    ),
)
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Nedelec(order=2)), linear_solver=SparseDirect()
)
fields = [
    Simulation(problem=problem, mesh=mesh, numerics=numerics)
    .solve_stationary()
    .field(model.magnetic_flux_density)
    for mesh in meshes
]

Results

The anomaly along the north-south profile over the body on the surface, against the dipole field of the sphere.

K = (mu_r - 1) / (mu_r + 2)


def sphere_field(points):
    """Exact field outside a permeable sphere in the uniform field B0."""
    r = np.linalg.norm(points, axis=1, keepdims=True)
    return B0 + K * (radius / r) ** 3 * (
        3 * (points @ B0)[:, None] * points / r**2 - B0
    )


anomaly = lambda B, position: 1e9 * (B @ B0) / earth - 1e9 * earth
x = np.linspace(-400.0, 400.0, 321)
profile = np.column_stack((x, np.full_like(x, 1e-3), np.full_like(x, depth)))
exact = anomaly(sphere_field(profile), None)
computed = [anomaly(field.evaluate(profile), None) for field in fields]
peaks = [values.max() for values in computed]
print(
    f"exact: largest anomaly {exact.max():.1f} nT at x = {x[np.argmax(exact)]:+.1f} m, smallest {exact.min():.1f} nT at x = {x[np.argmin(exact)]:+.1f} m"
)
print(
    "cell size in the body [m]   tetrahedra   largest anomaly [nT]   deviation   largest deviation on the profile"
)
for size, mesh, values in zip(sizes, meshes, computed):
    print(
        f"{size:25.1f} {mesh.cell_count:12d} {values.max():20.1f} {100 * (values.max() / exact.max() - 1):+10.2f} % "
        f"{np.max(np.abs(values - exact)):12.1f} nT ({100 * np.max(np.abs(values - exact)) / exact.max():.2f} % of the peak)"
    )
print(
    f"field inside the body: {1e6 * np.linalg.norm(fields[-1].evaluate(np.array([[5.0, 5.0, 0.0]]))):.3f} µT, "
    f"exact {1e6 * 3 * mu_r / (mu_r + 2) * earth:.3f} µT"
)

Going further

Replace the sphere in magnetic_prospecting_ore_body_geometry.py by a dipping slab or an irregular body and write the STEP files again; the exact sphere then serves as a first estimate. Add a remanent magnetization (magnetization_provider) to see how it shifts and turns the anomaly, or move the body to the magnetic equator (inclination 0°), where the anomaly over it is negative.

What you need to run it. An installed, activated OneSimulation runtime. The runtime is currently not available; the examples run with its next release. Get notified when it is available again. The notebook itself carries no key. These are numerical verifications against references, not experimental validation.
