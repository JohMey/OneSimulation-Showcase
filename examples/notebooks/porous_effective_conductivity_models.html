Effective thermal conductivity of a porous solid

The effective conductivity of a ceramic with water-filled spherical pores is computed on 3D unit cells for four porosities and placed among the classical averaging models.

What it computes

OneSimulation reads the cubic cell and its pore from STEP files, embeds the pore as a material region and solves steady heat conduction with quadratic tetrahedra for a temperature difference across the cell; the heat flow gives the effective conductivity, which by the mirror symmetry of the cell equals that of the periodic cell problem. The script that builds the STEP files comes with the notebook.

What it checks

At the porosities the meshes hold (9.55, 19.40, 29.32 and 44.21 %) the cells give 8.750, 7.560, 6.441 and 4.849 W/(m K), within +0.03, -0.01, -0.30 and -2.30 % of the Maxwell-Eucken model for dispersed pores, while the power law lies 13-41 % lower and the volume average 4-21 % higher.

Geometry

The notebook starts at the import of the CAD files porous_effective_conductivity_models.step, porous_effective_conductivity_models_pore_10.step, porous_effective_conductivity_models_pore_20.step, porous_effective_conductivity_models_pore_30.step, porous_effective_conductivity_models_pore_45.step: STEP (AP242), readable by any CAD program, with the names of the faces that the boundary conditions use. The geometry script

The script porous_effective_conductivity_models_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Effective thermal conductivity of a porous solid".

Builds the unit cell of a simple cubic array of spherical, fluid-filled pores with the CAD objects of
OneSimulation: a cube of 1 mm, written to porous_effective_conductivity_models.step with its faces
x = 0 and x = 1 mm as the groups hot and cold, and for the porosities 10, 20, 30 and 45 % the pore, a
sphere at the centre of the cube, written to porous_effective_conductivity_models_pore_<percent>.step.
"""

import math

from onesim.cad.d3 import Box, Sphere

side = 1e-3
cell = Box(length=side, width=side, height=side)
cell.grouped(hot=cell.boundaries.x_min, cold=cell.boundaries.x_max).save(
    "porous_effective_conductivity_models.step"
)
for percent in (10, 20, 30, 45):
    Sphere(
        radius=side * (3 * percent / 100 / (4 * math.pi)) ** (1 / 3),
        origin=(side / 2, side / 2, side / 2),
    ).save(f"porous_effective_conductivity_models_pore_{percent}.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A porous medium of a solid with conductivity \(k_s\) whose pores of volume fraction \(\varepsilon\) (the porosity) are filled with a fluid of conductivity \(k_f\) is described, under local thermal equilibrium, by one effective conductivity \(k_\text{eff}\). Common averaging models are

| model | \(k_\text{eff}\) | |---|---| | volume average (parallel layers) | \(\varepsilon k_f+(1-\varepsilon)k_s\) | | reciprocal average (series layers) | \(\big(\varepsilon/k_f+(1-\varepsilon)/k_s\big)^{-1}\) | | power law (geometric mean) | \(k_f^{\,\varepsilon}\,k_s^{\,1-\varepsilon}\) | | Maxwell-Eucken, pores dispersed in the solid | \(k_s\,\dfrac{2k_s+k_f-2\varepsilon(k_s-k_f)}{2k_s+k_f+\varepsilon(k_s-k_f)}\) |

Volume and reciprocal average are the absolute bounds; Maxwell-Eucken equals the upper Hashin-Shtrikman bound of an isotropic two-phase material, with the solid as the connected phase.

Which model applies depends on the microstructure. For separate pores in a connected solid it is Maxwell-Eucken. To check this, unit cells of a simple cubic array of spherical pores are solved: a ceramic of \(k_s=10\) W/(m K) with water in the pores, \(k_f=0.6\) W/(m K), at porosities of 10, 20, 30 and 45 %. A cell with mirror symmetry about its mid-planes gives its effective conductivity from a temperature difference between two opposite faces with the other faces insulated, \(k_\text{eff}=Q\,L/(A\,\Delta T)\), the same as the periodic cell problem.

| face | condition | |---|---| | hot, \(x=0\) | \(T=1\) K | | cold, \(x=1\) mm | \(T=0\) | | all other faces | insulated |

The cell comes from the CAD file porous_effective_conductivity_models.step, the pores from porous_effective_conductivity_models_pore_<percent>.step; the script porous_effective_conductivity_models_geometry.py builds them with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.

Reference. The averaging models above (Nield and Bejan, Convection in Porous Media, 5th ed., 2017, section 2.2.1) and the Hashin-Shtrikman bounds (Hashin and Shtrikman, J. Appl. Phys. 33, 1962, 3125). For dilute pores the cell must approach Maxwell-Eucken; at high porosity the cubic array lies slightly below it, as the pores start to interact.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import IsotropicSolid, regionwise
from onesim.meshing import TetrahedralMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.physics.heat_transfer import FixedTemperature, HeatConduction, HeatFlux
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns the cell with its named faces and each pore, which the mesher embeds as a material region. Quadratic tetrahedra of 0.1 mm resolve the spherical pores; the faceted pores hold a little less volume than the spheres, so each cell is compared at the porosity its mesh holds.

porosities = (10, 20, 30, 45)
cell = load("porous_effective_conductivity_models.step")
faces = cell.boundaries
pores = {
    percent: load(f"porous_effective_conductivity_models_pore_{percent}.step").named(
        "pore"
    )
    for percent in porosities
}
meshes = {
    percent: TetrahedralMesher(target_size=1e-4, embedded_regions=(pore,)).generate(
        cell
    )
    for percent, pore in pores.items()
}

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 6))
plots.geometry(
    [cell.named("solid"), pores[30]],
    labels=False,
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=30, azimuth=-55),
    length_unit="mm",
    title="Cell with a 30 % pore",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes[30],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    length_unit="mm",
    title=f"Mesh: {meshes[30].cell_count} tetrahedra",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Cell problems

regionwise gives solid and pore their conductivities; the heat flow out of the cold face, reaction, gives the effective conductivity.

solid, fluid = 10.0, 0.6
material = IsotropicSolid(
    density=1.0,
    specific_heat_capacity=1.0,
    youngs_modulus=1.0,
    poisson_ratio=0.3,
    thermal_expansion_coefficient=0.0,
    thermal_conductivity=regionwise({"pore": fluid}, default=solid, unit="W/m/K"),
)
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2)), linear_solver=SparseDirect()
)
effective, meshed = {}, {}
for percent, mesh in meshes.items():
    model = HeatConduction(domain=cell.domain(), material=material, heating=0.0)
    problem = Problem(
        name=f"Porous cell, {percent} %",
        geometry=cell,
        physics=(model,),
        boundary_conditions=(
            FixedTemperature(boundary=faces.hot, physics=model, value=1.0),
            FixedTemperature(boundary=faces.cold, physics=model, value=0.0),
            HeatFlux(
                boundary=faces.all - faces.hot - faces.cold, physics=model, value=0.0
            ),
        ),
    )
    temperature = (
        Simulation(problem=problem, mesh=mesh, numerics=numerics)
        .solve_stationary()
        .field(model.temperature)
    )
    effective[percent] = (
        temperature.reaction(faces.hot) * cell.size[0] / (cell.size[1] * cell.size[2])
    )
    meshed[percent] = temperature.integrate(
        region=pores[percent], component=lambda T, position: np.ones_like(T)
    ).value / np.prod(cell.size)

Results

The cell conductivities against the four averaging models, each evaluated at the porosity the mesh of the cell holds (the volume of its pore cells).

models = {
    "volume average": lambda e: e * fluid + (1 - e) * solid,
    "reciprocal average": lambda e: 1 / (e / fluid + (1 - e) / solid),
    "power law": lambda e: fluid**e * solid ** (1 - e),
    "Maxwell-Eucken": lambda e: solid
    * (2 * solid + fluid - 2 * e * (solid - fluid))
    / (2 * solid + fluid + e * (solid - fluid)),
}
print(
    "porosity   meshed   cell [W/(m K)]   volume avg.   reciprocal avg.   power law   Maxwell-Eucken   cell vs Maxwell-Eucken"
)
for percent in porosities:
    e = meshed[percent]
    print(
        f"{percent:7d} % {100 * e:7.2f} % {effective[percent]:14.4f} {models['volume average'](e):13.4f} {models['reciprocal average'](e):17.4f} "
        f"{models['power law'](e):11.4f} {models['Maxwell-Eucken'](e):16.4f} {100 * (effective[percent] / models['Maxwell-Eucken'](e) - 1):+18.2f} %"
    )

The cells follow Maxwell-Eucken within a fraction of a percent at low porosity and stay just below it at 45 %, while the power law underestimates and the volume average overestimates the conductivity by tens of percent: for a connected solid with closed pores, Maxwell-Eucken is the model to choose.

Figure

All averaging models over the porosity with the four cell results.

e = np.linspace(0, 0.6, 121)
plots.line(
    x=e * 100,
    y={name: law(e) for name, law in models.items()},
    points={
        "3D unit cells, spherical pores": (
            [100 * meshed[p] for p in porosities],

        )
    },

    ylabel="k_eff [W/(m K)]",
    title="Effective conductivity: ceramic with water-filled pores",
    legend="upper right",
)
plots.show()
