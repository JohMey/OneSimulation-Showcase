Thin low-permittivity gap in a dielectric capacitor

A capacitor filled with a dielectric of relative permittivity 20 contains a thin circular air gap; the gap is resolved once and replaced by a thin dielectric layer once, for four gap widths.

STEP importThin layerPotential jumpCapacitanceExact solution

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. Runtime Alpha 0.2.1a7 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

What it computes

OneSimulation reads the dielectric and the gaps from STEP files, cuts them to their mid-plane and solves electrostatics with quadratic triangles. The resolved models mesh the gap as its own region; the thin model lets the potential jump across the mid-line of the gap with the normal capacitance of the gap, on less than half the elements. The script that builds the STEP files comes with the notebook.

What it checks

The resolved gaps give the field inside the circle within 1 % of the exact coated-cylinder solution (0.8466, 0.6916, 0.5349 and 0.3778 of the applied field against 0.8484, 0.6945, 0.5384 and 0.3810 for 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 and 1 mm). The thin layer deviates by -2.0, -5.0, -9.9 and -19.9 %, in proportion to the gap width over its radius as expected of a first-order thin-layer model; the loss of capacitance follows likewise (-712 against -727 fF/m for the 0.1 mm gap).

The geometry script

The script thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Thin low-permittivity gap in a dielectric capacitor".

Builds the dielectric between the plates of a capacitor of two parallel plates, 80 mm x 80 mm and 5 mm deep,
with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor.step; its top face is
the group terminal (the upper plate), its bottom face the group ground (the lower plate) and its two
sides the group insulated. A circular gap of 5 mm outer radius sits at the centre, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 or
1 mm wide. For each gap width, in micrometres, the gap goes to
thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor_wall_<um>.step, the interior to thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor_core_<um>.step
and the cylinder up to the middle of the gap to thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor_midline_<um>.step.
The example computes on the cross-section z = 2.5 mm.
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import AnnularCylinder, Box, Cylinder

side, depth, radius = 0.08, 0.005, 0.005
gap = Box(length=side, width=side, height=depth, origin=(-side / 2, -side / 2, 0.0))
faces = gap.boundaries
gap = gap.grouped(
    terminal=faces.y_max, ground=faces.y_min, insulated=faces.x_min | faces.x_max
)
gap.save("thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor.step")
for micrometres in (100, 250, 500, 1000):
    wall = micrometres * 1e-6
    AnnularCylinder(inner_radius=radius - wall, outer_radius=radius, length=depth).save(
        f"thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor_wall_{micrometres}.step"
    )
    Cylinder(radius=radius - wall, length=depth).save(
        f"thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor_core_{micrometres}.step"
    )
    Cylinder(radius=radius - wall / 2, length=depth).save(
        f"thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor_midline_{micrometres}.step"
    )

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Delaminations, air gaps and thin adhesive films in insulation have a much lower permittivity than the material around them. The field crowds into such a gap, and the potential drops across it. A thin dielectric layer replaces the gap by a face across which the potential jumps,

\[D_n=\frac{\varepsilon_2}{d}\,\big(V_1-V_2\big),\]

while the small displacement flux along the gap is carried with \(\varepsilon_2 d\); the mesh no longer needs to resolve the gap.

Two parallel plates 80 mm apart, 80 mm wide, hold a voltage of 1 V across a dielectric of \(\varepsilon_{r1}=20\). In the middle a circular gap of outer radius \(b=5\) mm and width \(d\) is filled with a material of relative permittivity \(\varepsilon_{r2}\); inside the circle is the dielectric again. The electrostatic potential solves

\[\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\,\nabla V)=0 .\]
edgecondition
terminal, upper plate\(V=1\) V
ground, lower plate\(V=0\)
insulated, left and right\(D_n=0\)

Far from the gap the field is \(E_0=12.5\) V/m. Two models are solved for an air gap (\(\varepsilon_{r2}=1\)) of width \(d=0.1,\;0.25,\;0.5,\;1\) mm: the resolved model meshes the gap as its own region, the thin model puts the dielectric layer on the circle \(r=b-d/2\) in the middle of the gap. The thin model is a first-order approximation in \(d/b\): it keeps the dielectric in the place of the gap, so its error grows in proportion to the width of the gap.

The space between the plates and the gap come from the CAD files thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor*.step; the script thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor_geometry.py builds them with the CAD objects of OneSimulation. The two-dimensional model is their mid-plane section.

Reference. A coated cylinder in a uniform field of an unbounded medium has a uniform field inside (Jackson, Classical Electrodynamics, 3rd ed., problem 4.10). With core and surroundings of \(\varepsilon_1\) and a shell of \(\varepsilon_2\) between \(a=b-d\) and \(b\),

\[\frac{E_\text{in}}{E_0}=\frac{4\,\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2}{(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2)^2-(\varepsilon_2-\varepsilon_1)^2\,a^2/b^2}.\]

The low-permittivity gap shields the inside as well. The plates are 16 gap radii apart, so they change the field inside the circle by about a percent.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import regionwise
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher, interface
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.physics.electrostatics import (
    FixedPotential,
    PoissonEquation,
    ThinDielectricLayer,
    ZeroNormalElectricDisplacement,
)
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns each part, section its mid-plane cross-section. The resolved meshes follow the gap with elements of half its width; the thin model needs only the circle of the mid-line, with elements of 0.6 mm around it.

name = "thin_low_permittivity_gap_capacitor"
mid = lambda part: load(f"{name}{part}.step").section((0, 0, 1), origin=(0, 0, 0.0025))
gap = mid("")
edges = gap.boundaries
radius, walls = 0.005, (100, 250, 500, 1000)
meshes = {}
for micrometres in walls:
    thickness = 1e-6 * micrometres
    meshes[micrometres, "resolved"] = TriangularMesher(
        target_size=0.004,
        embedded_regions=(
            mid(f"_core_{micrometres}").named("core"),
            mid(f"_wall_{micrometres}").named("wall"),
        ),
        region_sizes={"wall": thickness / 2, "core": 0.0008},
    ).generate(gap)
    meshes[micrometres, "thin layer"] = TriangularMesher(
        target_size=0.004,
        embedded_regions=(mid(f"_midline_{micrometres}").named("core"),),
        region_sizes={"core": 0.0006},
    ).generate(gap)

fig = plots.figure(1, 3, size=(18, 6))
plots.geometry(
    [
        load(f"{name}.step").named("dielectric between the plates"),
        load(f"{name}_wall_1000.step").named("gap, 1 mm"),
    ],
    labels=False,
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=55, azimuth=-70),
    title="Dielectric with the circular gap",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes[250, "resolved"],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"Resolved 0.25 mm gap: {meshes[250, 'resolved'].cell_count} triangles",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes[250, "thin layer"],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"Thin dielectric layer: {meshes[250, 'thin layer'].cell_count} triangles",
    ax=fig[2],
)
plots.show()

Models

regionwise gives the gap of the resolved model its permittivity; ThinDielectricLayer acts on the faces between the two regions of the thin mesh, which interface returns.

epsilon0, bulk, er = 8.854187817e-12, 20.0, 1.0
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2)), linear_solver=SparseDirect()
)
models, results = {}, {}
for (micrometres, kind), mesh in meshes.items():
    law = (
        regionwise({"wall": er * epsilon0}, default=bulk * epsilon0, unit="F/m")
        if kind == "resolved"
        else regionwise((), default=bulk * epsilon0, unit="F/m")
    )
    model = PoissonEquation(domain=gap.domain(), permittivity=law)
    layer = (
        (
            ThinDielectricLayer(
                physics=model,
                boundary=interface(mesh, "background_core"),
                thickness=1e-6 * micrometres,
                permittivity=er * epsilon0,
            ),
        )
        if kind != "resolved"
        else ()
    )
    problem = Problem(
        name=f"Capacitor with a gap, {kind}",
        geometry=gap,
        physics=(model,),
        boundary_conditions=(
            FixedPotential(boundary=edges.terminal, physics=model, value=1.0),
            FixedPotential(boundary=edges.ground, physics=model, value=0.0),
            ZeroNormalElectricDisplacement(boundary=edges.insulated, physics=model),
        )
        + layer,
    )
    models[micrometres, kind] = model
    results[micrometres, kind] = Simulation(
        problem=problem, mesh=mesh, numerics=numerics
    ).solve_stationary()

Results

The field in the centre of the circle against the coated-cylinder formula, and the capacitance the gap takes from the filled capacitor (\(\varepsilon_1\,w/h=177.08\) pF/m per metre of depth), from the charge on the upper plate.

exact = (
    lambda d: 4
    * bulk
    * er
    / ((bulk + er) ** 2 - (er - bulk) ** 2 * (radius - d) ** 2 / radius**2)
)
E0, empty = 1.0 / gap.size[1], bulk * epsilon0 * gap.size[0] / gap.size[1]
field = {
    key: -result.field(models[key].electric_field).evaluate(np.array([[0.0, 0.0]]))[
        0, 1
    ]
    / E0
    for key, result in results.items()
}
added = {
    key: result.field(models[key].potential).reaction(edges.terminal) - empty
    for key, result in results.items()
}
print(
    "gap [mm]    d/b    E_in/E_0 exact   resolved   thin layer   thin vs resolved   change of C' resolved [fF/m]   thin layer [fF/m]"
)
for micrometres in walls:
    a, b = field[micrometres, "resolved"], field[micrometres, "thin layer"]
    print(
        f"{micrometres / 1e3:9.2f} {micrometres / 5e3:6.2f} {exact(1e-6 * micrometres):16.4f} {a:10.4f} {b:12.4f} {100 * (b / a - 1):+16.1f} % "
        f"{1e15 * added[micrometres, 'resolved']:30.2f} {1e15 * added[micrometres, 'thin layer']:19.2f}"
    )

For a gap of a fiftieth of the radius the thin layer gives the field of the resolved gap within a few percent on a fraction of the elements, with the potential jumping across the circle; at a fifth of the radius the deviation is about 20 %. It falls in proportion to \(d/b\), as expected of a first-order thin-layer model.

Going further

Give the gap a permittivity close to that of the dielectric and watch the jump vanish, or let the gap width vary along the circle (a callable of the position) to model a partial delamination.
