Submodel with plasticity at a hole

A steel plate with a hole is computed elastically as a whole, and the region around the hole, where the stress exceeds the yield limit, is recomputed as an elastoplastic submodel.

STEP importSubmodellingJ2 plasticityNeuber's rule

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. Runtime Alpha 0.2.1a7 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

What it computes

OneSimulation reads one eighth of the plate and the submodel block around the hole from two STEP files and meshes both with curved quadratic tetrahedra. The elastic plate model provides the displacements that drive the cut faces of the submodel, evaluated at its own nodes and ramped with the load factor; the submodel is computed with J2 plasticity and linear hardening on two meshes. The script that builds the STEP files comes with the notebook.

What it checks

The elastic stress concentration of the plate model (3.15) agrees with Peterson's chart (3.14), the submodel matches the plate model in the elastic range (3.23 with its finer mesh); at full load the largest plastic strain is 2.23e-3 against 3.05e-3 from Neuber's rule, which is known to be conservative, and the stress there (245 MPa) agrees with Neuber's 256 MPa within 5 %. The two submodel meshes agree within 1 %.

Geometry

The notebook starts at the import of the CAD files plate_hole_plastic_submodel.step, plate_hole_plastic_submodel_detail.step: STEP (AP242), readable by any CAD program, with the names of the faces that the boundary conditions use. Put them next to the notebook.

The geometry script

The script plate_hole_plastic_submodel_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Submodel with plasticity at a hole in a plate".

Builds one eighth of a steel plate with a central hole (plate 100 mm wide, 200 mm long and 10 mm
thick, hole diameter 20 mm), cut by its three symmetry planes, with the CAD objects of
OneSimulation, and the submodel: the block of 25 mm x 25 mm around the hole cut from the same
eighth. The plate is written to plate_hole_plastic_submodel.step, the submodel to
plate_hole_plastic_submodel_detail.step. In both the faces keep the box names x_min, y_min,
z_min (symmetry planes), x_max, y_max, z_max, and the hole wall is hole.outer_wall; in the
submodel x_max and y_max are the cut faces that take the displacements of the plate model.
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import Box, Cylinder

half_width, half_length, half_thickness, radius, detail = (
    0.050,
    0.100,
    0.005,
    0.010,
    0.025,
)
hole = lambda: Cylinder(
    radius=radius, length=4 * half_thickness, origin=(0.0, 0.0, -half_thickness)
).named("hole")
(Box(length=half_width, width=half_length, height=half_thickness) - hole()).save(
    "plate_hole_plastic_submodel.step"
)
(Box(length=detail, width=detail, height=half_thickness) - hole()).save(
    "plate_hole_plastic_submodel_detail.step"
)

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

An elastic analysis of a whole structure often shows a few small spots where the stress lies above the yield limit. Making the whole model elastoplastic and fine enough for those spots is expensive; a submodel is cheaper. A small region around the spot is cut out, meshed finely, given the plastic material, and driven on its cut faces by the displacements of the global model. As long as the plastic zone stays small, the displacements far from it are hardly changed by plasticity, which is what makes the transfer valid.

Setup. A steel plate 100 mm wide, 200 mm long and 10 mm thick with a central hole of 20 mm diameter, pulled by \(S=150\) MPa at its ends. Steel: \(E=200\) GPa, \(\nu=0.3\), J2 plasticity with yield stress \(\sigma_y=250\) MPa and linear isotropic hardening \(H=2\) GPa. One eighth of the plate (three symmetry planes) comes from plate_hole_plastic_submodel.step, the submodel, a 25 mm × 25 mm block around the hole cut from the same eighth, from plate_hole_plastic_submodel_detail.step; plate_hole_plastic_submodel_geometry.py writes both with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.

References. The elastic stress concentration of a finite-width plate with a hole, \(K_t=\big(2+(1-d/W)^3\big)/(1-d/W)\) on the gross stress (Heywood's fit to Peterson's chart; W.D. Pilkey, D.F. Pilkey, Peterson's Stress Concentration Factors, 3rd ed., Wiley 2008). For the elastoplastic notch root, Neuber's rule \(\sigma\,\varepsilon=(K_tS)^2/E\) with the bilinear stress-strain curve \(\varepsilon=\sigma/E+(\sigma-\sigma_y)/H\) (H. Neuber, J. Appl. Mech. 28, 1961, 544-550), an engineering estimate that is usually somewhat conservative.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials.solids import IsotropicHardening, J2Plasticity
from onesim.meshing import LocalRefinement, TetrahedralMesher
from onesim.physics.solid_mechanics import SmallStrainSolid
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns both parts with their named faces. Curved quadratic tetrahedra: 8 mm in the plate with 3 mm at the hole; the submodel with 1.2 mm and, for the mesh study, 0.8 mm at the hole.

plate, detail = load("plate_hole_plastic_submodel.step"), load(
    "plate_hole_plastic_submodel_detail.step"
)
outer, inner = plate.boundaries, detail.boundaries
radius, width = 0.010, 0.100
plate_mesh = TetrahedralMesher(
    target_size=0.008,
    geometry_order=2,
    refinements=(LocalRefinement(region=outer["hole.outer_wall"], target_size=0.003),),
).generate(plate)
hole_sizes = [0.0012, 0.0008]
detail_meshes = [
    TetrahedralMesher(
        target_size=0.003,
        geometry_order=2,
        refinements=(
            LocalRefinement(region=inner["hole.outer_wall"], target_size=size),
        ),
    ).generate(detail)
    for size in hole_sizes
]

view = plots.Camera(elevation=40, azimuth=-60)
fig = plots.figure(2, 2, size=(14, 11))
plots.geometry(
    plate,
    selection={
        "pulled end": outer.y_max,
        "hole": outer["hole.outer_wall"],
        "symmetry": outer.x_min | outer.y_min | outer.z_min,
    },
    labels=False,
    camera=view,
    title="One eighth of the plate",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.geometry(
    detail,
    selection={
        "cut faces": inner.x_max | inner.y_max,
        "hole": inner["hole.outer_wall"],
    },
    labels=False,
    camera=view,
    title="Submodel around the hole",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.mesh(
    plate_mesh,
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    camera=view,
    title=f"Plate: {plate_mesh.cell_count} tetrahedra",
    ax=fig[2],
)
plots.mesh(
    detail_meshes[-1],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    camera=view,
    title=f"Submodel: {detail_meshes[-1].cell_count} tetrahedra",
    ax=fig[3],
)
plots.show()

Global model

The whole plate, elastic, under the full load.

youngs_modulus, poisson, stress, yield_stress, hardening = 200e9, 0.3, 150e6, 250e6, 2e9
whole = SmallStrainSolid(
    youngs_modulus=youngs_modulus, poisson_ratio=poisson, kinematics="volume"
).solve(
    mesh=plate_mesh,
    times=(1.0,),
    order=2,
    displacements=(
        (outer.x_min, "x", 0.0),
        (outer.y_min, "y", 0.0),
        (outer.z_min, "z", 0.0),
    ),
    tractions=((outer.y_max, (0.0, stress, 0.0)),),
)

Submodel

The cut faces take the displacement of the plate model, evaluated at their own nodes and scaled by the load factor \(t\), which rises in eight steps to 1. The steel is elastoplastic.

transfer = (
    lambda component: lambda x, y, z, t: t
    * whole.evaluate_displacement(np.column_stack((x, y, z)))[:, component]
)
factors = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, 9)
steel = SmallStrainSolid(
    youngs_modulus=youngs_modulus,
    poisson_ratio=poisson,
    kinematics="volume",
    material=J2Plasticity(
        shear_modulus=youngs_modulus / (2 * (1 + poisson)),
        hardening=IsotropicHardening(yield_stress, hardening),
    ),
)
submodels = [
    steel.solve(
        mesh=mesh,
        times=factors,
        order=2,
        displacements=(
            (inner.x_min, "x", 0.0),
            (inner.y_min, "y", 0.0),
            (inner.z_min, "z", 0.0),
        )
        + tuple(
            (face, axis, transfer(k))
            for face in (inner.x_max, inner.y_max)
            for k, axis in enumerate("xyz")
        ),
    )
    for mesh in detail_meshes
]
plastic = [
    np.array(
        [
            [[point.equivalent_plastic_strain for point in cell] for cell in step]
            for step in result.material_history
        ]
    ).reshape(len(factors), -1)
    for result in submodels
]

Comparison with the references

The elastic peak stress of the plate model and of the submodel in its last elastic step against Peterson's chart, then at full load the largest plastic strain and the stress at the same point against Neuber's rule.

peterson = (2 + (1 - 2 * radius / width) ** 3) / (1 - 2 * radius / width)
neuber = lambda elastic: np.where(
    elastic <= yield_stress,
    elastic,
    (
        yield_stress / hardening
        + np.sqrt(
            (yield_stress / hardening) ** 2
            + 4 * (1 / youngs_modulus + 1 / hardening) * elastic**2 / youngs_modulus
        )
    )
    / (2 * (1 / youngs_modulus + 1 / hardening)),
)
print(
    f"elastic stress concentration: plate model {whole.stress[0, :, 1, 1].max() / stress:.3f}, "
    f"submodel at t = 0.25 {[result.stress[2, :, 1, 1].max() / (0.25 * stress) for result in submodels][-1]:.3f}, Peterson's chart {peterson:.3f}"
)
print(
    f"elastic peak at full load K_t S = {peterson * stress / 1e6:.0f} MPa, yield stress {yield_stress / 1e6:.0f} MPa; "
    f"Neuber at full load: σ = {neuber(peterson * stress) / 1e6:.1f} MPa, ε_p = {1e3 * (neuber(peterson * stress) - yield_stress) / hardening:.3f}e-3"
)
print(
    f"{'hole mesh':>9s} {'tetrahedra':>10s} {'largest ε_p [1e-3]':>19s} {'σ_yy there [MPa]':>17s} {'plastic points [%]':>19s} {'first yield at t':>17s}"
)
for size, mesh, result, values in zip(hole_sizes, detail_meshes, submodels, plastic):
    worst = values[-1].argmax()
    print(
        f"{1e3 * size:6.1f} mm {mesh.cell_count:10d} {1e3 * values[-1].max():19.3f} {result.stress[-1, worst, 1, 1] / 1e6:17.1f}"
        f" {100 * np.mean(values[-1] > 0):19.2f} {factors[np.argmax(values.max(axis=1) > 0)]:17.3f}"
    )

Figure

The largest plastic strain over the load factor for both submodel meshes, and the stress at the point where it occurs, with Neuber's rule and the purely elastic stress.

line = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, 201)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(16, 6))
plots.line(
    points={
        f"submodel, {mesh.cell_count} tetrahedra": (factors, 1e3 * values.max(axis=1))
        for mesh, values in zip(detail_meshes, plastic)
    },
    reference={
        "Neuber's rule": (
            line,
            1e3
            * (
                neuber(peterson * stress * line)
                - np.minimum(neuber(peterson * stress * line), yield_stress)
            )
            / hardening,
        )
    },
    xlabel="load factor t",
    ylabel="largest plastic strain ε_p [1e-3]",
    title="Plastic strain at the hole",
    legend="upper left",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.line(
    points={
        f"submodel, {mesh.cell_count} tetrahedra": (
            factors,
            result.stress[:, values[-1].argmax(), 1, 1] / 1e6,
        )
        for mesh, result, values in zip(detail_meshes, submodels, plastic)
    },
    reference={
        "Neuber's rule": (line, neuber(peterson * stress * line) / 1e6),
        "elastic, K_t S t": (line, peterson * stress * line / 1e6),
    },
    hlines={"yield stress": yield_stress / 1e6},
    xlabel="load factor t",
    ylabel="σ_yy at the point of largest plastic strain [MPa]",
    title="Stress at the hole",

    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Going further

Raise the load until the plastic zone reaches the cut faces: then the submodel assumption fails and the submodel must grow. Use kinematic hardening and unload to see the residual compressive stress that a notch keeps after an overload; or replace the plate by your own part and cut the submodel from it in the geometry script.
