3D thermal bridge (ISO 10211 case 4)

An iron bar crossing an insulation layer: the 3D validation case of the building standard for thermal bridges.

Finite volumes vs Q2 FEMHarmonic interface conductivityISO 10211 reference
Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation solves steady 3D conduction with surface resistances on a graded hexahedral block mesh twice, with cell-centred finite volumes (harmonic-mean face conductivity) and with quadratic finite elements, and refines both three times.

What it checks

Both methods meet the standard's tolerances for the heat flow (0.540 W) and the maximum external surface temperature (0.805 degC); the finite-element heat flow is exact to the quoted digits and the energy balance closes to 1e-9.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

EN ISO 10211 (Thermal bridges in building construction - Heat flows and surface temperatures - Detailed calculations) validates numerical methods with four test reference cases. A program that reproduces all of them is classed as a three-dimensional steady-state high-precision method. Case 4 is the three-dimensional one with the steepest gradients: a square insulation layer, 1 m x 1 m and 0.2 m thick, is pierced by an iron bar of 0.1 m x 0.05 m cross section. The bar starts flush with the cold (external) surface, crosses the insulation and sticks out 0.4 m into the warm (internal) room:

| part | extent / m | conductivity / (W/(m K)) | |---|---|---| | insulation layer | \(0\le x\le1,\ 0\le y\le0.2,\ 0\le z\le1\) | 0.1 | | iron bar | \(0.45\le x\le0.55,\ 0\le y\le0.6,\ 0.475\le z\le0.525\) | 50 |

The temperature solves the steady heat equation \(\nabla\cdot(k\nabla T)=0\). Both sides exchange heat with the air through a surface resistance \(R_s=0.10\) m² K/W (\(h=1/R_s=10\) W/(m² K)); the internal air is at 1 °C, the external air at 0 °C. Boundary conditions on every face:

| face | condition | |---|---| | external face \(y=0\) (insulation and bar end) | \(-k\,\partial_nT=h\,(T-0\,^\circ\mathrm{C})\) | | internal face \(y=0.2\) of the insulation and all faces of the protruding bar | \(-k\,\partial_nT=h\,(T-1\,^\circ\mathrm{C})\) | | the four cut edges of the layer (\(x=0,1\); \(z=0,1\)) | adiabatic (zero flux) |

The field is genuinely three-dimensional: it depends on the depth \(y\) and on both distances from the bar. The problem is symmetric about the planes \(x=0.5\) and \(z=0.5\), so one quarter (\(x\ge0.5\), \(z\ge0.5\)) is computed with zero-flux symmetry planes and mirrored for the figures; heat flows are multiplied by four.

Numerical methods (both on the same meshes, same OneSimulation problem). - Cell-centred finite volumes: one temperature per hexahedron, two-point diffusive face fluxes (CentralDifference) with least-squares cell gradients (LeastSquaresGradient) for the non-orthogonal correction, Robin faces closed by the exact face balance. On faces between bar and insulation the face conductivity is the harmonic mean, the classical series-resistance rule of finite volumes (S. V. Patankar, Numerical Heat Transfer and Fluid Flow, Hemisphere 1980, Sec. 4.2-3); the mesh has equal cell widths on both sides of every material interface, so the harmonic mean is the exact series resistance of the two half cells. - Quadratic finite elements: continuous Galerkin with 27-node tensor-product Lagrange elements (Q2) on the same hexahedra. - Linear solver: sparse direct LU in both cases (the problem is linear and steady).

Reference. The test reference values of EN ISO 10211:2007, Annex A (2017 edition: Annex C), case 4: heat flow \(\Phi=0.540\) W between the internal and the external environment and the highest external surface temperature \(0.805\,^\circ\)C, to be met within 1 % (heat flow) and 0.005 K (temperature). Supporting checks: the one-dimensional solution far from the bar, the energy balance (heat in = heat out) and three-level mesh studies with observed order and Richardson estimate for both methods.

from dataclasses import replace
import time
import numpy as np

from onesim.cad.d3 import Box, Regrouped
from onesim.materials import IsotropicSolid
from onesim.meshing import Geometric, Piecewise, StructuredHexahedralMesher, region_mesh
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange
from onesim.numerics.fvm import CentralDifference, FiniteVolume, LeastSquaresGradient
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.physics.heat_transfer import ConvectiveHeatFlux, HeatConduction, HeatFlux
from onesim.results.comparisons import convergence
from onesim.cad.d2 import Plane
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation, regionwise
from onesim.visualization import theme, plots

theme.set()

Parameters

k_ins, k_iron = 0.1, 50.0
h_s = 1.0 / 0.10
T_int, T_ext = 274.15, 273.15
x_bar, z_bar = 0.55, 0.525
t_ins, y_tip = 0.2, 0.6
Phi_ref, Phi_tol = 0.540, 0.01
T_ref, T_tol = 0.805, 0.005
U_1d = 1.0 / (0.10 + t_ins / k_ins + 0.10)
print(f"1D far field: U = {U_1d:.5f} W/(m^2 K), heat flux {U_1d * 1.0:.5f} W/m^2, "
      f"external surface temperature {U_1d * 0.10:.5f} degC")

Geometry and mesh

The quarter model (\(x\ge0.5\), \(z\ge0.5\)) is the insulation slab joined with the bar (|); the joined faces keep the names of the part they lie on (bar. for the bar) and are grouped into the four boundary kinds of the standard. The mesh is a graded tensor-product block mesh of the bounding box with a node plane on every material interface, restricted to the solid (region_mesh): geometric grading away from the bar surfaces \(x=0.55\) and \(z=0.525\) (same first width on both sides), towards both faces of the insulation in \(y\) and along the protruding bar. A mesh level \(\ell\) scales the cell counts by \(\ell\) and the smallest cell width (\(8\) mm\(/\ell\)) by \(1/\ell\).

slab = Box(length=0.5, width=t_ins, height=0.5, origin=(0.5, 0.0, 0.5))
bar = Box(length=x_bar - 0.5, width=y_tip, height=z_bar - 0.5, origin=(0.5, 0.0, 0.5))
construction = Regrouped(source=slab | bar.named("bar"), groups={
    "external": ("y_min",),
    "internal": ("y_max", "bar.x_max", "bar.z_max", "bar.y_max"),
    "symmetry": ("x_min", "z_min", "bar.x_min", "bar.z_min"),
    "layer_edges": ("x_max", "z_max"),
})
bounding_box = Box(length=0.5, width=y_tip, height=0.5, origin=(0.5, 0.0, 0.5))


def mesh_for(level):
    cells = lambda base: int(round(base * level))
    first = 0.008 / level
    grading = (
        Piecewise(((x_bar - 0.5, Geometric(cells(2), first=first, toward="end")),
                   (1.0 - x_bar, Geometric(cells(5), first=first)))),
        Piecewise(((t_ins / 2, Geometric(cells(3), first=first)),
                   (t_ins / 2, Geometric(cells(3), first=first, toward="end")),
                   (y_tip - t_ins, Geometric(cells(3), first=first)))),
        Piecewise(((z_bar - 0.5, Geometric(cells(1), first=first, toward="end")),
                   (1.0 - z_bar, Geometric(cells(5), first=first)))),
    )
    counts = tuple(g.cells for g in grading)
    block = StructuredHexahedralMesher(cells=counts, grading=grading).generate(bounding_box)
    return region_mesh(block, construction)

Model

One HeatConduction problem serves both methods. The conductivity is region-wise: 50 W/(m K) in the bar and 0.1 W/(m K) in the insulation. On the bar-insulation interface, where only the finite-volume faces sample it, interfaces="harmonic" returns the harmonic mean (the series resistance of the two equal half cells); the quadrature points of the finite elements always lie inside a cell and see the plain values.

conductivity = regionwise(((bar, k_iron),), default=k_ins, unit="W/m/K", interfaces="harmonic")
material = replace(IsotropicSolid(density=500.0, specific_heat_capacity=1700.0, thermal_conductivity=k_ins,
                                  youngs_modulus=1e9, poisson_ratio=0.3, thermal_expansion_coefficient=0.0),
                   thermal_conductivity=conductivity)

METHODS = {
    "FV": FiniteVolume(gradient=LeastSquaresGradient(), diffusion=CentralDifference()),
    "Q2": FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2)),
}
b = construction.boundaries


def solve(level, method):
    start = time.time()
    mesh = mesh_for(level)
    model = HeatConduction(domain=construction.domain(), material=material, heating=0.0)
    problem = Problem(name="ISO 10211 case 4 thermal bridge", geometry=construction, physics=(model,),
                      boundary_conditions=(
                          ConvectiveHeatFlux(boundary=b.internal, physics=model, heat_transfer_coefficient=h_s,
                                             ambient_temperature=T_int),
                          ConvectiveHeatFlux(boundary=b.external, physics=model, heat_transfer_coefficient=h_s,
                                             ambient_temperature=T_ext),
                          HeatFlux(boundary=b.symmetry | b.layer_edges, physics=model, value=0.0)))
    result = Simulation(problem=problem, mesh=mesh, numerics=Numerics(
        spatial=METHODS[method], linear_solver=SparseDirect())).solve_stationary()
    field = result.field(model.temperature)
    external = field.sample(along=b.external, subdivisions=4)
    return dict(
        level=level, method=method, mesh=mesh, field=field,
        unknowns=result.operators[model.model_id].problem_size,
        Phi_out=4 * field.boundary_flux(b.external), Phi_in=-4 * field.boundary_flux(b.internal),
        T_max=float(np.max(external.values)) - 273.15,
        T_far=float(field.evaluate([[1.0, 0.0, 1.0]])[0]) - 273.15,
        seconds=time.time() - start,
    )

Solve

Three levels per method. The finite-volume levels \(\ell=2,3,4\) and the finite-element levels \(\ell=1,2,3\) use the same mesh family.

runs = {"FV": [], "Q2": []}
for method, levels in (("FV", (2, 3, 4)), ("Q2", (1, 2, 3))):
    for level in levels:
        out = solve(level, method)
        runs[method].append(out)
        print(f"{method} level {level}: {out['mesh'].cell_count:6d} cells, {out['unknowns']:6d} unknowns, "
              f"Phi = {out['Phi_out']:.5f} W, T_max,ext = {out['T_max']:.5f} degC  ({out['seconds']:.0f} s)")

Comparison with the reference

print("EN ISO 10211 case 4, finest mesh of each method")
print("   quantity                       reference      FV (cells)     Q2 (elements)")
fv, fe = runs["FV"][-1], runs["Q2"][-1]
print(f"   heat flow Phi / W              {Phi_ref:9.3f}    {fv['Phi_out']:9.4f}      {fe['Phi_out']:9.4f}")
print(f"     deviation (tolerance 1 %)                 {100 * (fv['Phi_out'] / Phi_ref - 1):+8.2f} %     "
      f"{100 * (fe['Phi_out'] / Phi_ref - 1):+8.2f} %")
print(f"   max external T / degC          {T_ref:9.3f}    {fv['T_max']:9.4f}      {fe['T_max']:9.4f}")
print(f"     deviation (tolerance 0.005 K)              {fv['T_max'] - T_ref:+8.4f} K     {fe['T_max'] - T_ref:+8.4f} K")

print("Mesh studies (cell size ~ 1/level)")
for method, levels in (("FV", (2, 3, 4)), ("Q2", (1, 2, 3))):
    for key, label in (("Phi_out", "Phi / W"), ("T_max", "T_max / degC")):
        values = [r[key] for r in runs[method]]
        study = convergence(values, [1.0 / level for level in levels])
        order = "-" if study.order is None else f"{study.order:.2f}"
        extra = study.reason if study.extrapolated is None else f"{study.extrapolated:.5f}"
        print(f"   {method} {label:13s} " + "  ".join(f"{v:.5f}" for v in values)
              + f"   order {order}, Richardson {extra}, change last two meshes {abs(values[-1] - values[-2]):.1e}")

print("Energy balance (heat entering on the warm side = heat leaving on the cold side)")
for method in runs:
    r = runs[method][-1]
    print(f"   {method}: in {r['Phi_in']:.6f} W, out {r['Phi_out']:.6f} W, relative difference "
          f"{abs(r['Phi_in'] - r['Phi_out']) / r['Phi_out']:.1e}")
print(f"1D far field at the layer corner (x = z = 1): external surface temperature, closed form "
      f"{U_1d * 0.10:.5f} degC")
for method in runs:
    print(f"   {method}: {runs[method][-1]['T_far']:.5f} degC")
print(f"Thermal bridge effect: Phi - U A = {fe['Phi_out'] - U_1d * 1.0:.4f} W "
      f"(point thermal transmittance chi = {(fe['Phi_out'] - U_1d) / 1.0:.4f} W/K)")
