Creating a fluid domain inside a solid structure

The water inside a manifold block with a through bore and a branch, extracted as a solid of its own with walls and three closed openings.

Interior extractionAssembly meshBoundary layersClosed-form check

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. OneSimulation 1.0 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation builds the solid space enclosed by the bore walls of the CAD block, keeps the wall names and closes every opening with a new named face. Housing and fluid become an assembly meshed as one with a shared wall, and the fluid alone is meshed with five boundary layers on its wall.

What it checks

Fluid volume, both wall areas and the three openings match the Steinmetz-based closed forms (63.2504 cm3, 84.718 and 16.859 cm2, 4.524 cm2) to better than 6e-7, the contact face equals the wall area to 2e-7, and the five measured layer heights equal the stated 0.200 to 0.571 mm; linear cells place the meshed fluid 1.1-1.2 % below the exact volume (chords of the round walls).

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

CAD models of valves, manifolds, pumps and heat exchangers describe the metal. A flow or conjugate heat transfer model needs the opposite: the space the fluid fills, closed at the inlets and outlets. part.interior(walls) builds that space from the inner faces of the part. The walls keep the identifiers they have on the part, and every opening is closed by a new flat face, so inlet and outlet conditions have a face to refer to.

face group of the fluidmeaning
wall (main.outer_wall, branch.outer_wall)the bore walls of the block: no-slip, heat exchange with the metal
opening (opening_1 ... opening_3)the three ends of the bores: inlet and outlets

The block is \(L\times W\times H\) = 120 x 60 x 60 mm; a bore of radius \(r\) = 12 mm runs through its length at mid-height and a branch bore of the same radius is drilled from the top down to its axis.

Reference. Two cylinders of equal radius that cross at right angles share the Steinmetz solid of volume \(16r^3/3\); the branch, which ends at the axis of the main bore, shares half of it. Each cylinder wall loses the area \(4r^2\) to the other. Hence

\[V_\text{fluid} = \pi r^2 L + \pi r^2 \frac{H}{2} - \frac{8r^3}{3},\qquad A_\text{main} = 2\pi r L - 4r^2,\qquad A_\text{branch} = \pi r H - 4r^2,\qquad A_\text{opening} = \pi r^2 .\]
import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d3 import Assembly, Box, Cylinder
from onesim.meshing import BoundaryLayers, TetrahedralMesher
from onesim.meshing.quality import quality
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

The solid structure

The bores are named cylinders subtracted from the block, so their walls are the faces main.outer_wall and branch.outer_wall.

length, width, height, radius = 0.12, 0.06, 0.06, 0.012
block = (
    Box(length=length, width=width, height=height)
    - Cylinder(
        radius=radius,
        length=length,
        origin=(0.0, width / 2, height / 2),
        axis=(1, 0, 0),
    ).named("main")
    - Cylinder(
        radius=radius, length=height / 2, origin=(length / 2, width / 2, height / 2)
    ).named("branch")
)
walls = block.boundaries["main.outer_wall"] | block.boundaries["branch.outer_wall"]
print(f"block faces: {', '.join(face.id for face in block.faces)}")

The fluid domain

interior takes the faces that enclose the fluid and returns a solid. Housing and fluid together are an Assembly: one mesh with a volume for each part that meet node by node at the wall.

fluid = block.interior(walls)
manifold = Assembly(parts=[block.named("housing"), fluid.named("water")])
groups = fluid.boundary_groups()
print(f"fluid faces: {', '.join(face.id for face in fluid.faces)}")
print(f"walls: {', '.join(groups['wall'])}; openings: {', '.join(groups['opening'])}")

mesh = TetrahedralMesher(target_size=0.006, curvature_angle=0.3).generate(manifold)
view = plots.Camera(elevation=30, azimuth=-55)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(16, 6))
plots.geometry(
    block,
    selection={"bore walls": walls},
    labels=False,
    camera=view,
    title="Manifold block with its bores",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    mesh,
    cell_values=quality(mesh)["shape"],
    limits=(0, 1),
    plane=((length / 2, width / 2, height / 2), (0.0, 1.0, 0.0)),
    label="shape measure",
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=15, azimuth=90),
    title=f"Assembly mesh of housing and water cut open: {mesh.cell_count} tetrahedra",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Results

Volume and face areas of the fluid domain against the closed forms; the volume of the housing is the block less the fluid, and the contact face between the two parts is the whole wall. The linear tetrahedra cut the round walls as chords, so the meshed fluid is about \(1-\sin\alpha/\alpha \approx 1.5\,\%\) smaller for a turn of \(\alpha\) = 0.3 rad per cell, and the housing larger by the same volume.

exact = {
    "fluid volume [cm3]": (
        fluid.measure,
        np.pi * radius**2 * length + np.pi * radius**2 * height / 2 - 8 * radius**3 / 3,
        1e6,
    ),
    "main bore wall [cm2]": (
        fluid.topology.face("main.outer_wall").area,
        2 * np.pi * radius * length - 4 * radius**2,
        1e4,
    ),
    "branch bore wall [cm2]": (
        fluid.topology.face("branch.outer_wall").area,
        np.pi * radius * height - 4 * radius**2,
        1e4,
    ),
    **{
        f"{name} [cm2]": (fluid.topology.face(name).area, np.pi * radius**2, 1e4)
        for name in groups["opening"]
    },
    "housing volume [cm3]": (
        block.measure,
        length * width * height - fluid.measure,
        1e6,
    ),
}
print("quantity                    CAD           closed form    deviation")
for name, (value, reference, scale) in exact.items():
    print(
        f"{name:24s} {value * scale:12.5f} {reference * scale:14.5f} {value / reference - 1:+12.1e}"
    )
(contact,) = manifold.interfaces
print(
    f"contact between {' and '.join(contact.parts)}: {contact.area * 1e4:.5f} cm2, wall area "
    f"{(exact['main bore wall [cm2]'][1] + exact['branch bore wall [cm2]'][1]) * 1e4:.5f} cm2"
)
volumes = quality(mesh)["size"]
for part in manifold.volumes:
    print(
        f"mesh of {part.id:8s}: {len(mesh.region_cells(manifold.volume_of(part.id))):6d} cells, volume {volumes[list(mesh.region_cells(manifold.volume_of(part.id)))].sum() * 1e6:.4f} cm3 "
        f"(CAD {part.measure * 1e6:.4f} cm3)"
    )

Fluid mesh with boundary layers

For the flow alone the fluid gets its own mesh with five prism-like layers of tetrahedra on the wall, 0.2 mm high at the wall and growing by 1.3, where the velocity rises from zero.

layers = BoundaryLayers(
    walls=fluid.boundaries.wall, count=5, first_height=2e-4, growth=1.3
)
flow_mesh = TetrahedralMesher(
    target_size=0.004, curvature_angle=0.3, boundary_layers=(layers,)
).generate(fluid)
report = flow_mesh.layer_report
print(
    f"fluid mesh: {flow_mesh.cell_count} cells, {report['layer_count']} layers, heights "
    f"{', '.join(f'{1e3 * item['mean']:.3f}' for item in report['layer_heights'])} mm, stated "
    f"{', '.join(f'{1e3 * h:.3f}' for h in layers.layer_heights())} mm"
)
print(
    f"fluid mesh volume {quality(flow_mesh)['size'].sum() * 1e6:.4f} cm3, closed form {exact['fluid volume [cm3]'][1] * 1e6:.4f} cm3"
)

Figure

The fluid domain with its wall and its three openings, and its layered mesh cut through the axis of the main bore.

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(16, 6))
plots.geometry(
    fluid,
    selection={"wall": fluid.boundaries.wall, "openings": fluid.boundaries.opening},
    labels=False,
    camera=view,
    title="Fluid domain: wall and openings",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    flow_mesh,
    cell_values=flow_mesh.cell_layers,
    limits=(0, 5),
    plane=((length / 2, width / 2, height / 2), (0.0, 1.0, 0.0)),
    label="layer (0 = core)",
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=15, azimuth=90),
    title=f"Fluid mesh with 5 wall layers: {flow_mesh.cell_count} cells",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()
