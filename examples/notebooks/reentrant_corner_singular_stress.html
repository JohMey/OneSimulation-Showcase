Singular stress at a re-entrant corner

An L-shaped plate whose sharp inner corner carries a stress that is infinite in theory, compared with the same plate with a 2 mm fillet.

STEP importPlane stressStress singularityWilliams exponentMesh refinement

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. OneSimulation 1.0 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

Download the notebook (.ipynb)Download the script (.py)Download the geometry (.step)Download the geometry script (.py)

What it computes

OneSimulation cuts both plates from their STEP files to the mid-plane and solves plane stress with quadratic triangles graded to the corner, on four meshes that each split every triangle into four. The stress at the integration points is followed along the corner bisector and as peak value near the corner. The script that builds the STEP files comes with the notebook.

What it checks

The peak at the sharp corner grows by 1.366 to 1.368 per refinement against Williams' 2^0.4555 = 1.371 and never converges; the fitted exponent along the bisector is -0.451 against -0.4555 (-0.9 %); the filleted corner converges to 368 MPa (change 0.04 %).

Geometry

The notebook starts at the import of the CAD files reentrant_corner_singular_stress.step, reentrant_corner_singular_stress_fillet.step: STEP (AP242), readable by any CAD program, with the names of the faces that the boundary conditions use. Put them next to the notebook.

The geometry script

The script reentrant_corner_singular_stress_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Singular stress at a re-entrant corner".

Builds an L-shaped plate, 100 mm x 100 mm with legs 50 mm wide and 5 mm thick, with the CAD
objects of OneSimulation. reentrant_corner_singular_stress.step has the sharp re-entrant corner at
(50, 50) mm; reentrant_corner_singular_stress_fillet.step is the same plate with the corner rounded
to a radius of 2 mm. The faces are named fixed (x = 100 mm), loaded (y = 100 mm) and corner (the two
faces that meet at the re-entrant corner, with the fillet between them).
"""

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d3 import Box

leg, length, thickness, radius = 0.050, 0.100, 0.005, 0.002
for suffix, fillet in (("", None), ("_fillet", radius)):
    plate = Box(length=length, width=length, height=thickness) - Box(
        length=leg, width=leg, height=thickness, origin=(leg, leg, 0.0)
    )
    if fillet:
        plate = plate.fillet(
            fillet,
            edges=plate.select(
                edges=lambda edge: np.allclose(np.asarray(edge.start)[:2], (leg, leg))
                and np.allclose(np.asarray(edge.end)[:2], (leg, leg))
            ),
        )
    corner = plate.select(
        faces=lambda face: (face.kind == "cylinder")
        or (
            face.kind == "plane"
            and abs(face.normal[2]) < 0.5
            and (abs(face.center[0] - leg) < 1e-9 or abs(face.center[1] - leg) < 1e-9)
            and face.center[0] > leg / 2
            and face.center[1] > leg / 2
        )
    )
    plate = plate.grouped(
        fixed=plate.boundaries.x_max, loaded=plate.boundaries.y_max, corner=corner
    )
    plate.save(f"reentrant_corner_singular_stress{suffix}.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

At a sharp inner corner the material surrounds the corner point over an angle of \(270°\). The elastic field near such a corner was found by Williams (1952): the stresses vary like

\[\sigma_{ij}\sim K\,r^{\lambda-1}f_{ij}(\theta),\]

where \(r\) is the distance from the corner and \(\lambda\) the smallest root of the eigenvalue equation of the wedge. For the symmetric (opening) field of a wedge of opening \(2\alpha\) it is \(\lambda\sin2\alpha+\sin(2\alpha\lambda)=0\); with \(2\alpha=3\pi/2\) the root is \(\lambda=0.5445\), so the stress grows like \(r^{-0.4555}\). The stress at the corner is infinite: a finer mesh always shows a higher peak, roughly by \(2^{0.4555}=1.37\) for every halving of the cells next to the corner. A real part has a fillet, and with it the peak converges to a finite value.

The plate, 100 mm x 100 mm with legs 50 mm wide and 5 mm thick, comes in two versions from reentrant_corner_singular_stress.step (sharp corner) and reentrant_corner_singular_stress_fillet.step (corner rounded to 2 mm); the script reentrant_corner_singular_stress_geometry.py builds both with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.

boundarycondition
end of the horizontal leg, \(x=100\) mm (fixed)\(\mathbf u=0\)
end of the vertical leg, \(y=100\) mm (loaded)shear traction \((-10\ \mathrm{MPa},\ 0)\), which opens the corner
all other edgestraction free

Aluminium, \(E=70\) GPa, \(\nu=0.33\), plane stress.

Reference. M. L. Williams, Stress singularities resulting from various boundary conditions in angular corners of plates in extension, J. Appl. Mech. 19 (1952) 526; the exponent \(\lambda=0.5445\) for the \(270°\) wedge is tabulated there.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher
from onesim.physics.solid_mechanics import SmallStrainSolid
from onesim.results.derived import voigt, von_mises
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns the plates with their named faces, section the mid-plane regions. The base mesh has 5 mm cells, 0.2 mm along the two edges that meet at the corner (and along the fillet); every refinement level splits each triangle into four.

plates = {
    "sharp corner": load("reentrant_corner_singular_stress.step"),
    "2 mm fillet": load("reentrant_corner_singular_stress_fillet.step"),
}
faces = plates["sharp corner"].boundaries
thickness = plates["sharp corner"].size[2]
regions = {
    name: plate.section((0, 0, 1), origin=(0, 0, thickness / 2))
    for name, plate in plates.items()
}
levels = (0, 1, 2, 3)
meshes = {
    name: [
        TriangularMesher(
            target_size=0.005,
            grading=0.25,
            refinements=level,
            local_sizes={
                side: 0.0002
                for side in region.boundary_groups()
                if side.startswith(("cut", "fillet"))
            },
        ).generate(region)
        for level in levels
    ]
    for name, region in regions.items()
}

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 6))
plots.geometry(
    plates["sharp corner"],
    selection={"fixed": faces.fixed, "loaded": faces.loaded, "corner": faces.corner},
    labels=False,
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=50, azimuth=-60),
    title="L-shaped plate with its named faces",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes["sharp corner"][1],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"Mid-plane mesh after one refinement: {meshes['sharp corner'][1].cell_count} triangles",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

Plane stress with quadratic triangles; the same model on all eight meshes.

plate = SmallStrainSolid(
    youngs_modulus=70e9,
    poisson_ratio=0.33,
    kinematics="plane_stress",
    thickness=thickness,
)
runs = {
    name: [
        plate.solve(
            mesh=mesh,
            times=(0.0,),
            order=2,
            tractions=((regions[name].boundaries.y_max, (-10e6, 0.0)),),
            displacements=(
                (regions[name].boundaries.x_max, "x", 0.0),
                (regions[name].boundaries.x_max, "y", 0.0),
            ),
        )
        for mesh in meshes[name]
    ]
    for name in regions
}

Results

The von Mises stress at the integration points. The peak is taken within 5 mm of the corner, away from the clamped end, which has a singularity of its own. Along the bisector of the corner, the line into the material at \(45°\), the slope of the stress against the distance on a log-log scale is the exponent \(\lambda-1\).

corner = np.array([0.050, 0.050])
mises = {
    name: [von_mises(voigt(result.stress[0])) for result in results]
    for name, results in runs.items()
}
near = {
    name: [
        np.linalg.norm(result.quadrature_coordinates - corner, axis=1) < 0.005
        for result in results
    ]
    for name, results in runs.items()
}
peaks = {
    name: [
        stress[inside].max() / 1e6 for stress, inside in zip(mises[name], near[name])
    ]
    for name in runs
}
offset = runs["sharp corner"][-1].quadrature_coordinates - corner
distance = np.linalg.norm(offset, axis=1)
bisector = (
    np.abs(np.arctan2(-offset[:, 1], -offset[:, 0]) - np.pi / 4) < np.radians(4)
) & (distance < 0.01)
fitted = bisector & (distance > 5e-5) & (distance < 1e-3)
slope = np.polyfit(
    np.log(distance[fitted]), np.log(mises["sharp corner"][-1][fitted]), 1
)[0]

print(
    "refinement   triangles   peak at the sharp corner [MPa]   peak at the 2 mm fillet [MPa]"
)
for level in levels:
    print(
        f"{level:10d} {meshes['sharp corner'][level].cell_count:11d} {peaks['sharp corner'][level]:32.1f} {peaks['2 mm fillet'][level]:31.1f}"
    )
print(
    f"growth of the sharp-corner peak per refinement: {', '.join(f'{b / a:.3f}' for a, b in zip(peaks['sharp corner'], peaks['sharp corner'][1:]))}"
    f" (Williams: 2^0.4555 = {2**0.4555:.3f})"
)
print(
    f"change of the fillet peak on the last refinement: {100 * (peaks['2 mm fillet'][-1] / peaks['2 mm fillet'][-2] - 1):+.2f} %"
)
print(
    f"exponent along the bisector between 0.05 and 1 mm: {slope:.4f}, Williams λ - 1 = -0.4555, deviation {100 * (slope / -0.4555 - 1):+.1f} %"
)

Figure

The von Mises stress around the corner on the finest mesh of each plate, the stress along the bisector against the Williams power law, and the peak stress over the refinement: the sharp corner climbs without end, the fillet settles.

cells = {
    name: mises[name][-1].reshape(meshes[name][-1].cell_count, -1).max(axis=1) / 1e6
    for name in runs
}
fig = plots.figure(1, 3, size=(19, 5.5))
plots.mesh(
    meshes["sharp corner"][-1],
    cell_values=cells["sharp corner"],
    edges=False,
    limits=(0, 300),
    label="von Mises stress [MPa]",
    title="Sharp corner, finest mesh",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.line(
    points={
        "simulation, sharp corner": (
            1e3 * distance[bisector],
            mises["sharp corner"][-1][bisector] / 1e6,
        )
    },
    reference={
        "Williams, r^-0.4555": (
            np.geomspace(0.01, 10, 50),
            np.exp(
                np.mean(
                    np.log(mises["sharp corner"][-1][fitted] / 1e6)
                    + 0.4555 * np.log(1e3 * distance[fitted])
                )
            )
            * np.geomspace(0.01, 10, 50) ** -0.4555,
        )
    },
    scale="loglog",
    xlabel="distance from the corner along the bisector [mm]",
    ylabel="von Mises stress [MPa]",
    title="Power law along the bisector",
    legend="lower left",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.line(
    [meshes["sharp corner"][level].cell_count for level in levels],
    peaks,
    markers=True,
    scale="logx",
    xlabel="triangles",
    ylabel="peak von Mises stress near the corner [MPa]",
    title="Diverging and converging peaks",
    legend="upper left",
    ax=fig[2],
)
plots.show()

Going further

Change the fillet radius in reentrant_corner_singular_stress_geometry.py and write the STEP files again: the converged peak follows the stress concentration charts for shoulder fillets. Evaluate the generalised stress intensity \(K\) from the fitted line, or open the corner angle and watch the exponent go to zero at \(180°\).
