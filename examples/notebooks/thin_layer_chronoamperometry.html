Electrochemistry · 1D

Thin-layer chronoamperometry

A potential step reduces all electroactive species in a 50 um solution layer; current and charge are followed until the layer is exhausted.

Species transportSolution gradientAdaptive BDF2Closed-form check

Run locally with OneSimulation. The example downloads are free. Running them requires an activated runtime. Runtime Alpha 0.2.1a7 · CPython 3.13 on macOS (Apple Silicon), Ubuntu 22.04 (x86-64), and Windows x64. Installation and activation · View pilot plans. Scope of the reference checks.

What it computes

OneSimulation solves the diffusion equation with quadratic finite elements on a mesh graded towards the electrode and BDF2 steps under adaptive step control, starting from the exact early profile at 1e-5 s; the current is the gradient of the computed concentration at the electrode, the charge the amount that has left the layer.

What it checks

Current and charge match the exact thin-layer series within 0.39 % and 0.06 % from 1e-4 s on; Cottrell's law holds until D t / L^2 = 0.2, and the charge reaches 99.4 % of n F c0 L after 5 s.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A thin layer of solution of thickness \(L\) is trapped between a working electrode at \(x=0\) and an inert wall at \(x=L\). It contains an electroactive species O at the bulk concentration \(c_0\). At \(t=0\) the electrode potential is stepped far beyond the formal potential, so every O that reaches the electrode is reduced at once (\(\mathrm{O}+n\,e^-\to\mathrm{R}\)) and its surface concentration drops to zero. O then diffuses towards the electrode until the layer is exhausted:

\[\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}=D\,\frac{\partial^2c}{\partial x^2},\qquad c(x,0)=c_0,\quad c(0,t)=0,\quad \left.\frac{\partial c}{\partial x}\right|_{x=L}=0.\]

The current density is \(i=nFD\,\partial c/\partial x|_{x=0}\), and the charge passed is \(q(t)=nF\left(c_0L-\int_0^Lc\,dx\right)\). OneSimulation solves the diffusion equation with quadratic finite elements on a mesh graded towards the electrode and BDF2 steps under adaptive step control. The current is the gradient of the computed concentration at the electrode, the charge the amount of O that has left the layer.

Reference. A. J. Bard and L. R. Faulkner, Electrochemical Methods, 2nd ed., Wiley 2001, sections 5.2 and 11.7; A. T. Hubbard, Study of the kinetics of electrochemical reactions by thin-layer voltammetry, J. Electroanal. Chem. 22, 165-174 (1969). As long as the depletion layer \(\sqrt{Dt}\) is much thinner than \(L\), the electrode does not feel the wall (Cottrell, Anson):

\[i=nFc_0\sqrt{\frac{D}{\pi t}},\qquad q=2nFc_0\sqrt{\frac{Dt}{\pi}}.\]

The exact solution of the finite layer is the series

\[i=\frac{2nFDc_0}{L}\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-\lambda_k^2Dt},\qquad q=nFc_0L\Big(1-\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{2}{\lambda_k^2L^2}e^{-\lambda_k^2Dt}\Big),\qquad \lambda_k=\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2L},\]

so the current decays exponentially once the layer is depleted and the charge approaches \(nFc_0L\): the cell works as a coulometer.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d1 import Line
from onesim.meshing import NonuniformLineMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange, Nitsche
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.numerics.nonlinear import Newton
from onesim.numerics.time import BDF, AdaptiveTimeStep, BackwardEuler
from onesim.physics.chemistry import SpeciesTransport, TransportSpecies
from onesim.simulation import (
    OutwardFluxBoundary,
    PrescribedValueBoundary,
    Problem,
    Simulation,
)
from onesim.simulation.initial_conditions import InitialValue
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme
from scipy.special import erf

theme.set()

Cell

A 50 µm layer of a 1 mM solution of a one-electron redox species with the diffusion coefficient \(10^{-9}\) m²/s. The diffusion time \(L^2/D\) is 2.5 s; the experiment runs for twice that. The electrode holds the concentration at zero; the inert wall is a zero outward flux.

thickness, diffusivity, bulk, electrons, faraday = 50e-6, 1.0e-9, 1.0, 1, 96485.33212
end_time, start = 2.0 * thickness**2 / diffusivity, 1e-5
print(
    f"diffusion time L²/D = {thickness**2 / diffusivity:.2f} s, exhaustive charge n F c0 L = {electrons * faraday * bulk * thickness:.4f} C/m²"
)

line = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(thickness, 0.0, 0.0))
model = SpeciesTransport(
    domain=line.line,
    velocity=0.0,
    nonnegative=False,
    species=(TransportSpecies("O", diffusivity=diffusivity),),
)
problem = Problem(
    name="Thin-layer chronoamperometry",
    geometry=line,
    physics=(model,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        PrescribedValueBoundary(
            physics=model,
            boundary=line.boundaries.start,
            value=lambda time, coordinate: {"O": 0.0},
        ),
        OutwardFluxBoundary(
            physics=model,
            boundary=line.boundaries.end,
            flux=lambda **kw: np.zeros(1),
            jacobian=lambda **kw: np.zeros((1, 1)),
        ),
    ),
    initial_conditions=(
        InitialValue(
            model.concentration("O"),
            lambda x, y, z: bulk * erf(x / (2 * np.sqrt(diffusivity * start))),
        ),
    ),
)

Mesh and simulation

Sixty quadratic elements, geometrically graded so that the first one is 50 times thinner than the last: the element next to the electrode is 0.05 µm thick and resolves the depletion layer already after about \(10^{-5}\) s. The simulation starts at \(t_0=10^{-5}\) s from the exact early profile \(c=c_0\,\mathrm{erf}\big(x/2\sqrt{Dt_0}\big)\), which the wall 50 µm away cannot yet feel; this avoids the jump between the initial and the electrode concentration. The step control starts at \(10^{-7}\) s and lets the steps grow as the transient slows down.

faces = thickness * (np.geomspace(1.0, 51.0, 61) - 1.0) / 50.0
mesh = NonuniformLineMesher(faces=tuple(faces)).generate(line)
result = Simulation(
    problem=problem,
    mesh=mesh,
    numerics=Numerics(
        spatial=FiniteElement(
            element=Lagrange(order=2), boundary_enforcement=Nitsche()
        ),
        nonlinear_solver=Newton(linear_solver=SparseDirect(), jacobian="analytic"),
        time_integrator=BDF(order=2, startup=BackwardEuler()),
    ),
).run(
    start_time=start,
    end_time=end_time,
    time_step=AdaptiveTimeStep(
        initial=1e-7,
        minimum=1e-9,
        maximum=0.05,
        relative_tolerance=1e-3,
        absolute_tolerance=1e-4,
    ),
)
concentration = result.field(model.concentration("O"))
amount = concentration.integrate()
times = np.asarray(amount.times)
charge = electrons * faraday * (bulk * thickness - np.asarray(amount.values))
current = np.array(
    [
        electrons
        * faraday
        * diffusivity
        * concentration.gradient(np.zeros((1, 3)), time=t)[0, 0]
        for t in times
    ]
)
print(
    f"{len(times) - 1} accepted steps from {np.diff(times)[0]:.1e} s to {np.diff(times).max():.3f} s"
)

Comparison

The thin-layer series with 400 terms, and the Cottrell and Anson expressions, at the accepted times from \(10^{-4}\) s on.

def thin_layer(t):
    rates = (
        (((2 * np.arange(400) + 1) * np.pi / (2 * thickness)) ** 2)[:, None]
        * diffusivity
        * t
    )
    return (
        2
        * electrons
        * faraday
        * diffusivity
        * bulk
        / thickness
        * np.exp(-rates).sum(axis=0),
        electrons
        * faraday
        * bulk
        * thickness
        * (
            1
            - (2 * diffusivity * t / (rates * thickness**2) * np.exp(-rates)).sum(
                axis=0
            )
        ),
    )


exact_current, exact_charge = thin_layer(times[1:])
cottrell = electrons * faraday * bulk * np.sqrt(diffusivity / (np.pi * times[1:]))
compared = times[1:] >= 1e-4
print(
    "    t [s]   current [A/m²]  thin-layer   Cottrell     charge [C/m²]  thin-layer    Anson"
)
for target in (1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.5, 4.0):
    k = np.argmin(np.abs(times[1:] - target))
    print(
        f"{times[1 + k]:9.2e} {current[1 + k]:13.4f} {exact_current[k]:11.4f} {cottrell[k]:10.4f} "
        f"{charge[1 + k]:16.5f} {exact_charge[k]:11.5f} {2 * electrons * faraday * bulk * np.sqrt(diffusivity * times[1 + k] / np.pi):9.5f}"
    )
print(
    f"largest charge deviation from the series from 1e-4 s on: {np.abs(charge[1:] / exact_charge - 1)[compared].max():.2%}"
)
print(
    f"largest current deviation from the series from 1e-4 s on: {np.abs(current[1:] / exact_current - 1)[compared].max():.2%}"
)
departure = times[1:][compared][np.argmax((cottrell / exact_current)[compared] > 1.01)]
print(
    f"Cottrell overestimates the current by more than 1 % from t = {departure:.3f} s on (D t / L² = {diffusivity * departure / thickness**2:.3f})"
)
print(
    f"charge after {end_time:.0f} s: {charge[-1] / (electrons * faraday * bulk * thickness):.4f} of n F c0 L"
)

Figure

The depletion profiles, the current on logarithmic axes (Cottrell's \(t^{-1/2}\) law and the exponential decay of the exhausted layer), and the charge approaching the coulometric limit.

positions = 0.5 * (faces[1:] + faces[:-1])
profiles = {
    f"t = {t:g} s": np.r_[
        0.0,
        np.asarray(
            result.field(model.concentration("O")).evaluate(
                np.column_stack((positions, 0 * positions, 0 * positions)),
                time=times[np.argmin(np.abs(times - t))],
            )
        ).reshape(-1),
    ]
    for t in (0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 1.5, 5.0)
}
fig = plots.figure(1, 3, size=(17, 5))
plots.line(
    np.r_[0.0, positions] * 1e6,
    profiles,
    xlabel="distance from the electrode x [µm]",
    ylabel="concentration of O [mol/m³]",
    title="Depletion of the layer",
    legend="lower right",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.line(
    points={"OneSimulation": (times[1:][compared][::3], current[1:][compared][::3])},
    reference={
        "thin-layer series": (times[1:][compared], exact_current[compared]),
        "Cottrell": (times[1:][compared], cottrell[compared]),
    },
    scale="loglog",
    xlabel="time t [s]",
    ylabel="current density i [A/m²]",
    title="Current transient",
    legend="lower left",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.line(
    points={"OneSimulation": (times[1:][compared][::3], charge[1:][compared][::3])},
    reference={
        "thin-layer series": (times[1:][compared], exact_charge[compared]),
        "Anson (semi-infinite)": (
            times[1:][compared],

    scale="logx",

    title="Charge: exhaustive electrolysis",

)
plots.show()

Going further

Make the layer ten times thicker and the Cottrell regime extends a hundred times longer: the classical semi-infinite chronoamperometry. Replace the zero surface concentration by a Butler-Volmer flux in an OutwardFluxBoundary to see the kinetically limited current at small overpotentials. With a second species R and a reverse potential step the same model gives double-step chronoamperometry.
